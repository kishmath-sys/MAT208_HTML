2. Nonstandard Normal Distributions

These checks cover the Introduction and item 1 on page 1 and Classroom Example 6.2.1 on page 5 of the Section 6.2 notes.

Check 1 (Nonstandard Normal Distributions). The lifetimes of a brand of phone battery are normally distributed with a mean of 42 months and a standard deviation of 6 months. Which statement matches item 1 of the notes?
Show answer

It is a nonstandard normal distribution, because its mean is not 0 and its standard deviation is not 1.

Where this is: The Introduction and item 1 (Working with Nonstandard Normal Distributions) on page 1 of the notes.

3. Finding a Probability

These checks cover the examples on pages 1 to 3 and Classroom Examples 6.2.2, 6.2.4 and 6.2.6 part a on pages 6 and 7 of the Section 6.2 notes.

Check 4 (Greater Than a Value). Resting heart rates of adults in a study are normally distributed with a mean of 71 beats per minute and a standard deviation of 9. To find the probability that a randomly selected adult has a resting heart rate greater than 80, which StatCrunch setup matches the notes?
Show answer

Mean 71, Std. Dev. 9, the ≥greater than or equal to sign, and 80 in the X box

Where this is: Item 1 and the example on pages 1 and 2 of the notes, and the video "Using StatCrunch to Find Area Under Nonstandard Normal Curve Greater than a Given Value" on page 1.

Check 5 (Between Two Values). Commute times in a city are normally distributed with a mean of 27 minutes and a standard deviation of 8 minutes. Which StatCrunch setup finds the probability that a commute takes between 20 and 30 minutes?
Show answer

Click Between, enter mean 27 and Std. Dev. 8, then enter 20 and 30

Where this is: The example on pages 2 and 3 of the notes, and the video "Using StatCrunch to Find Area Under Nonstandard Normal Curve Between Two Values" on page 2.

4. Finding a Data Value

These checks cover Example 3 on pages 4 and 5 and Classroom Examples 6.2.3, 6.2.5 and 6.2.6 part b on pages 6 and 7 of the Section 6.2 notes.

Check 9 (Top Percent Cutoff). Scores on a state driving test are normally distributed. You want the score that separates the top 10% from the bottom 90%. Using the same approach as Example 3, after entering the mean and standard deviation, which entry matches?
Show answer

The ≥greater than or equal to sign, with 0.100 point 1 0 typed after the equals sign and the X box left empty

Where this is: Example 3 on pages 4 and 5 of the notes, and the video "Using StatCrunch to A Data Value On A Nonstandard Normal Curve" on page 4.

Check 10 (Area on the Left). A graph of weights that are normally distributed shows a shaded region to the left of an unknown weight x, labeled with an area of 0.30 point 3. After entering the mean and standard deviation, which entry finds x?
Show answer

The ≤less than or equal to sign, with 0.30 point 3 typed after the equals sign

Where this is: Classroom Example 6.2.3 on page 6 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 6.2.3" on page 6.

Check 11 (Smallest Percent and Largest Percent). Men's shoulder widths are normally distributed with a mean of 18.218 point 2 in. and a standard deviation of 1.01 point 0 in. A bus seat designer wants the width that separates the smallest 95% from the largest 5%. Following the Classroom Example 6.2.5 video, which entry matches?
Show answer

The ≥greater than or equal to sign, with 0.050 point 0 5 typed after the equals sign

Where this is: Classroom Example 6.2.5 on page 7 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 6.2.5" on page 7.

Show answer

Two computations: ≤less than or equal to with 0.030 point 0 3 for the lower limit, and ≥greater than or equal to with 0.050 point 0 5 for the upper limit
