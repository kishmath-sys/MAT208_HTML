2. Squaring numbers, including decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Square a whole number by multiplying it by itself.
  • Square a decimal and place the decimal point by counting decimal places.
  • Tell squaring apart from doubling.
  • Predict whether squaring makes a number larger or smaller.

Squaring means multiplying a number by itself

The small raised 2 is called an exponent. 7²7 squared is read "7 squared" and means 7 × 7 = 497 times 7 equals 49. It does not mean 7 × 27 times 2.

Squaring a decimal. Multiply as if there were no decimal points. Then count the decimal places in the number you squared, and double that count. The answer gets that many decimal places, because you are multiplying two copies of the same number.

  • 0.3²0.3 squared: 3 × 3 = 93 times 3 equals 9. Each 0.3 has one decimal place, so the answer has two: 0.090 point 0 9.
  • 0.02²0.02 squared: 2 × 2 = 42 times 2 equals 4. Each 0.02 has two decimal places, so the answer has four: 0.00040 point 0 0 0 4.

Three slips to watch for

  • Doubling instead of squaring. 1.5²1.5 squared is not 3. Squaring multiplies the number by itself, not by 2.
  • Too few decimal places. 0.3²0.3 squared is not 0.90 point 9. One place times one place gives two places: 0.090 point 0 9.
  • Expecting the answer to be bigger. A number between 0 and 1 gets smaller when you square it. 0.5²0.5 squared is 0.250 point 2 5, which is less than 0.50 point 5. Squaring each piece separately is also a slip: 2.5²2.5 squared is 6.256 point 2 5, not 2² + 0.5² = 4.252 squared plus 0 point 5 squared, which is 4 point 2 5.

Worked example: Square 1.5

Find 1.5²1.5 squared.

  1. 15 × 15 = 22515 times 15 equals 225Multiply as if there were no decimal points.
  2. Decimal places: 1 + 1 = 2Each 1.5 has one decimal place, so the answer has two.
  3. 225 → 2.25225 becomes 2 point 2 5Count two places in from the right.
  4. Check: 1² = 11 squared equals 1 and 2² = 42 squared equals 4Since 1.5 is between 1 and 2, its square should be between 1 and 4. The answer 2.25 fits. Doubling would give 3, which is a different number.

Answer: 2.252 point 2 5

3. Powers typed on one line

What you will be able to do

  • Read and type a power on one line with the ^ symbol.
  • Work out powers before multiplication, division, addition and subtraction.
  • Use parentheses when a whole expression should be squared.

The ^ symbol means "to the power of"

Calculators and computer programs often cannot show a raised exponent, so they use the ^ symbol (called a caret) on one line. 7^27 caret 2 means 7²7 squared, and 2.5^22 point 5 caret 2 means 2.5²2.5 squared. On most keyboards, ^ is above the 6 key, so you press Shift and 6.

The order

  1. Parentheses first.
  2. Powers next. The power applies only to the number right before the ^. A decimal such as 2.5 counts as one number.
  3. Multiplication and division, working from left to right.
  4. Addition and subtraction last, also from left to right.

To square a whole expression, put it in parentheses: (3 + 2)^2open parenthesis 3 plus 2 close parenthesis, caret 2 is 5^2 = 255 caret 2 equals 25, but 3 + 2^23 plus 2 caret 2 is 3 + 4 = 73 plus 4 equals 7.

Two slips to watch for

  • Multiplying before the power. In 3 × 4^23 times 4 caret 2, only the 4 is squared. Multiplying first would square 12 instead.
  • Reading ^2 as "times 2." 9^29 caret 2 is 81, not 18.

Worked example: 3 × 4^2

Evaluate 3 × 4^23 times 4 caret 2.

  1. 4^2 = 164 caret 2 equals 16The power comes first, and it applies only to the 4.
  2. 3 × 16 = 483 times 16 equals 48Then multiply.
  3. (3 × 4)^2 = 144open parenthesis 3 times 4 close parenthesis, caret 2, equals 144Multiplying first would give this very different value. Without parentheses, that is not what the expression says.

Answer: 48

4. Comparing decimals and inequality symbols

What you will be able to do

  • Compare two decimals that have different numbers of digits.
  • Read and write the symbols <, >, ≤, ≥, = and ≠.
  • Decide whether a statement such as 0.062 ≤ 0.050 point 0 6 2 is less than or equal to 0 point 0 5 is true.

Line up the decimal points

  1. Write the numbers so their decimal points line up.
  2. Add zeros at the end until both have the same number of decimal places. Zeros at the end do not change the value: 0.50 point 5, 0.500 point 5 0 and 0.5000 point 5 0 0 are equal.
  3. Compare the digits after the point as if they were whole numbers.
Inequality symbols and how to read them
SymbolRead it asA true example
<is less than0.04 < 0.40 point 0 4 is less than 0 point 4
>is greater than0.7 > 0.650 point 7 is greater than 0 point 6 5
≤is less than or equal to0.30 ≤ 0.30 point 3 0 is less than or equal to 0 point 3
≥is greater than or equal to0.25 ≥ 0.2050 point 2 5 is greater than or equal to 0 point 2 0 5
=is equal to0.80 = 0.80 point 8 0 is equal to 0 point 8
≠is not equal to0.08 ≠ 0.80 point 0 8 is not equal to 0 point 8

The open side of < or > faces the larger number. The line under ≤ or ≥ means "or equal to," so 0.3 ≤ 0.30 point 3 is less than or equal to 0 point 3 is true.

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer looks larger. 0.0980 point 0 9 8 has more digits than 0.10 point 1, but 0.098 < 0.1000 point 0 9 8 is less than 0 point 1 0 0.
  • Shorter looks larger. The reverse slip: 0.450 point 4 5 is not smaller than 0.40 point 4 just because it has more digits. 0.45 > 0.400 point 4 5 is greater than 0 point 4 0.
  • Forgetting the "or equal to." A number can satisfy ≤ or ≥ by being equal. It cannot satisfy < or > that way.

Worked example: Compare 0.3 and 0.047

Which is larger, 0.30 point 3 or 0.0470 point 0 4 7?

  1. 0.300 and 0.0470 point 3 0 0 and 0 point 0 4 7Add zeros so both have three decimal places.
  2. 300 is greater than 47Compare the digits after the point as whole numbers.
  3. 0.3 > 0.0470 point 3 is greater than 0 point 0 4 7The same fact can be written 0.047 < 0.30 point 0 4 7 is less than 0 point 3.

Answer: 0.30 point 3 is larger.

5. Rounding to two or three decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two decimal places and to three decimal places.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Look at only the one digit right after the last digit you keep.
  • Keep the zeros that show the number of decimal places, as in 0.5000 point 5 0 0.

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. For two decimal places, that is the second digit after the point. For three decimal places, it is the third.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit. Keep any zeros up to the kept digit, so the answer shows the number of places asked for.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 2.44492 point 4 4 4 9 to two decimal places is 2.442 point 4 4. Rounding to 2.4452 point 4 4 5 first and then to 2.452 point 4 5 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. Dropping digits without looking at the next one is not rounding. 0.36780 point 3 6 7 8 to three decimal places is 0.3680 point 3 6 8, not 0.3670 point 3 6 7.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.29970 point 2 9 9 7 to three decimal places is 0.3000 point 3 0 0.

Worked example: Round 0.68351 two ways

Round 0.683510 point 6 8 3 5 1 to three decimal places, and then to two decimal places.

  1. Three decimal places: keep the 3, look at the 5The 5 is 5 or more, so the 3 becomes 4: 0.6840 point 6 8 4.
  2. Two decimal places: keep the 8, look at the 3The 3 is 4 or less, so the 8 stays: 0.680 point 6 8.

Answer: 0.6840 point 6 8 4 to three decimal places and 0.680 point 6 8 to two decimal places.

Directions decide the answer. "Two decimal places" means exactly two digits after the point. "Three decimal places" means exactly three. If the rounded number ends in zeros, such as 0.5000 point 5 0 0, those zeros show that it was rounded to three places. "Do not round" means type every digit.
