2. Adding, subtracting and halving with decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Add a list of whole numbers without leaving any out.
  • Split a whole number or a decimal in half and write the result as a decimal.
  • Add or subtract a decimal and a whole number by lining up the decimal points.
  • Subtract a larger number from a smaller one and keep the minus sign.
  • Put negative and positive decimals in order.

Line up the decimal points

To add or subtract a whole number and a decimal, write the whole number with a decimal point and zeros, then line up the points. For example, 46 − 0.546 minus 0 point 5 is the same as 46.0 − 0.546 point 0 minus 0 point 5, which is 45.545 point 5.

Halving. Half of a number is the number divided by 2. Half of 1 is 0.50 point 5, half of 3 is 1.51 point 5, and half of 0.10 point 1 is 0.050 point 0 5. Halving a number smaller than 1 gives an even smaller number.

Going below zero. When you subtract a larger number from a smaller one, the answer is negative. Find the difference of the two sizes, then put a minus sign in front. On a number line, a negative number is to the left of zero, and −0.5negative 0 point 5 is farther left than −0.05negative 0 point 0 5, so −0.5negative 0 point 5 is the smaller of the two.

Three slips to watch for

  • Treating 0.50 point 5 as 5. 46 − 0.546 minus 0 point 5 is not 41. Half of 1 is subtracted, so the answer is 45.545 point 5.
  • Dropping the minus sign. 0 − 0.250 minus 0 point 2 5 is −0.25negative 0 point 2 5, not 0.250 point 2 5. The number taken away is larger, so the result is below zero.
  • Losing a place when halving a decimal. Half of 0.10 point 1 is 0.050 point 0 5, not 0.50 point 5. Write 0.10 point 1 as 0.100 point 1 0 first: half of 10 hundredths is 5 hundredths.

Worked example: Find 6 − 6.25

Find 6 − 6.256 minus 6 point 2 5.

  1. The second number is largerSo the answer will be below zero.
  2. 6.25 − 6.00 = 0.256 point 2 5 minus 6 point 0 0 equals 0 point 2 5Find the difference of the two sizes, lining up the decimal points.
  3. 6 − 6.25 = −0.256 minus 6 point 2 5 equals negative 0 point 2 5Put a minus sign in front.

Answer: −0.25negative 0 point 2 5

3. Order of operations on one line

What you will be able to do

  • Work inside parentheses first.
  • Do multiplication and division from left to right, before addition and subtraction.
  • Type a fraction whose top is a sum so that the whole sum gets divided.
  • Work out these expressions when one number is negative.

The order

  1. Parentheses first.
  2. Multiplication and division next, working from left to right.
  3. Addition and subtraction last, also from left to right.

On one line, a slash (/) means divide. A fraction bar groups everything above it, so the fraction 18 + 252the fraction with 18 plus 25 on top and 2 on the bottom means add first, then divide. Typed on one line, it needs parentheses: (18 + 25) / 2open parenthesis 18 plus 25 close parenthesis, slash 2. Without them, only the 25 gets divided.

Worked example: (18 + 25) / 2

Evaluate (18 + 25) / 2open parenthesis 18 plus 25 close parenthesis, slash 2, then see what happens without the parentheses.

  1. 18 + 25 = 4318 plus 25 equals 43Parentheses first.
  2. 43 / 2 = 21.543 slash 2 equals 21 point 5Then divide.
  3. 18 + 25 / 2 = 18 + 12.5 = 30.518 plus 25 slash 2 equals 18 plus 12 point 5 equals 30 point 5Leaving out the parentheses divides only the 25, which gives a very different value.

Answer: 21.521 point 5

4. Part of a total as a decimal or percent

What you will be able to do

  • Pick out the part and the total, and find the total by adding all the counts when it is not given.
  • Divide the part by the total to get a decimal.
  • Change a decimal to a percent by multiplying by 100.
  • Answer a two-step question about the part that is left over.

Part divided by total

To find what part of a total one count is, divide the part by the total. The answer is a decimal between 0 and 1. To write it as a percent, multiply by 100, which moves the decimal point two places to the right, and add the percent sign.

  • The total is the whole group. If you are given several counts that make up the group, the total is all of them added together.
  • The part is the one count the question asks about.
  • A part is never bigger than its total, so the decimal must be 1 or less. An answer bigger than 1 usually means the division was flipped.

Three slips to watch for

  • Dividing by the number of groups. If 4 shelves hold 40 books, the total is 40 books, not 4 shelves.
  • Flipping the division. Total divided by part gives a number bigger than 1, which cannot be a part of the whole.
  • Moving the point the wrong distance. 0.040 point 0 4 is 4%, not 40% or 0.4%0 point 4 percent. Multiplying by 100 moves the point exactly two places.

Worked example: Book club

A book club has 25 members, and 9 of them prefer mysteries. What part of the club prefers mysteries, as a decimal and as a percent?

  1. Part: 9 members. Total: 25 members.Label both numbers before dividing.
  2. 9 / 25 = 0.369 slash 25 equals 0 point 3 6Divide the part by the total to get the decimal.
  3. 0.36 × 100 = 360 point 3 6 times 100 equals 36Multiply by 100 to get the percent.

Answer: 0.360 point 3 6 as a decimal, or 36%

5. Rounding to one or two decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two decimal places.
  • Round to one decimal place.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Follow the rounding direction in the question, even when a whole number looks tidier.

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. For one decimal place, that is the first digit after the point. For two decimal places, it is the second digit after the point.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 0.24490 point 2 4 4 9 to two decimal places is 0.240 point 2 4. Rounding to 0.2450 point 2 4 5 first and then to 0.250 point 2 5 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. Dropping digits without looking at the next one is not rounding. 0.28750 point 2 8 7 5 to two decimal places is 0.290 point 2 9, not 0.280 point 2 8.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.5960 point 5 9 6 to two decimal places is 0.600 point 6 0.

Worked example: 5 / 12 two ways

A calculator shows 0.4166666670 point 4 1 6 6 6 6 6 6 7 for 5 / 125 slash 12. Round it to two decimal places. Then write it as a percent rounded to one decimal place.

  1. Two decimal places: keep the 1, look at the 6The 6 is 5 or more, so the 1 becomes 2: 0.420 point 4 2.
  2. 0.416666667 × 100 = 41.66666670 point 4 1 6 6 6 6 6 6 7 times 100 equals 41 point 6 6 6 6 6 6 7For the percent, multiply the unrounded decimal by 100.
  3. One decimal place: keep the 6 after the point, look at the next 6The next digit is 6, so the kept 6 becomes 7: 41.7%41 point 7 percent.

Answer: 0.420 point 4 2 to two decimal places, and 41.7%41 point 7 percent to one decimal place

Directions decide the answer. "Two decimal places" means exactly two digits after the point. "One decimal place" means exactly one. A direction such as "Round to one decimal place as needed" still means one decimal place, not a whole number.
