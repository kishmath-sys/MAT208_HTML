2. Adding decimals with different lengths

What you will be able to do

  • Line up decimal points before adding, even when the numbers have different numbers of digits after the point.
  • Fill empty places with zeros so every number has the same number of digits after the point.
  • Carry correctly across places, and treat a 0 in a list as adding nothing.
  • Add a column of five or six decimals read from a table.

Line up the points, not the last digits

With whole numbers, you line up the last digits. With decimals, you line up the decimal points instead, so tenths sit under tenths and hundredths sit under hundredths. Then add as usual and bring the point straight down.

  • Fill in zeros. 0.40 point 4 is the same as 0.400 point 4 0 and 0.4000 point 4 0 0. Writing the extra zeros makes every number the same length, so nothing slides out of place.
  • A 0 in the list adds nothing. Keep it in the list so you do not lose your place, but it does not change the sum.
  • Carry the same way as with whole numbers. Ten hundredths make one tenth, and ten tenths make one whole.

Three slips to watch for

  • Lining up the last digits. Adding 0.4 + 0.350 point 4 plus 0 point 3 5 as if the 4 sits under the 5 gives 0.390 point 3 9. The 4 is 4 tenths, so it goes under the 3: the sum is 0.750 point 7 5.
  • Reading a small decimal as a bigger one. 0.060 point 0 6 is 6 hundredths, not 6 tenths. Treating it as 0.60 point 6 makes the sum too big by 0.540 point 5 4.
  • Losing a carry. In 0.008 + 0.0340 point 0 0 8 plus 0 point 0 3 4, 8 thousandths plus 4 thousandths is 12 thousandths. Write the 2 and carry the 1.

Worked example: Add 0.2 + 0.135 + 0 + 0.07

Find 0.2 + 0.135 + 0 + 0.070 point 2 plus 0 point 1 3 5 plus 0 plus 0 point 0 7.

  1. 0.200, 0.135, 0.000, 0.0700 point 2 0 0, 0 point 1 3 5, 0 point 0 0 0, 0 point 0 7 0Give every number three digits after the point by filling in zeros. Now the points line up.
  2. Thousandths: 0 + 5 + 0 + 0 = 50 plus 5 plus 0 plus 0 equals 5Write 5 in the thousandths place.
  3. Hundredths: 0 + 3 + 0 + 7 = 100 plus 3 plus 0 plus 7 equals 10Write 0 and carry 1 to the tenths place.
  4. Tenths: 1 + 2 + 1 + 0 + 0 = 41 plus 2 plus 1 plus 0 plus 0 equals 4The carried 1 plus the tenths digits gives 4. Bring the point straight down.

Answer: 0.4050 point 4 0 5

3. Comparing decimals and percents

What you will be able to do

  • Compare two decimals by lining up the points and comparing place by place.
  • Avoid the "longer is larger" and "shorter is larger" traps.
  • Change a small percent such as 4% to a decimal, and compare a decimal with a percent.
  • Put several decimals in order from smallest to largest.

Compare place by place, from the left

  1. Line up the decimal points. Fill in zeros so both numbers have the same number of digits after the point.
  2. Start at the left and compare the digits in the same place. The first place where they differ decides which number is larger.
  3. To compare a decimal with a percent, change the percent to a decimal first: divide by 100, which moves the point two places to the left.

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer is larger. 0.0350 point 0 3 5 has more digits than 0.040 point 0 4, but it is smaller. As thousandths, they are 0.0350 point 0 3 5 and 0.0400 point 0 4 0, and 35 thousandths is less than 40 thousandths.
  • Shorter is larger. 0.10 point 1 is not larger than 0.1050 point 1 0 5 just because it is shorter. As thousandths, they are 0.1000 point 1 0 0 and 0.1050 point 1 0 5.
  • Moving the point only one place. 4% is 0.040 point 0 4, not 0.40 point 4. 0.40 point 4 is 40%.

Worked example: Which is larger, 0.09 or 0.105?

Which is larger, 0.090 point 0 9 or 0.1050 point 1 0 5?

  1. 0.090 and 0.1050 point 0 9 0 and 0 point 1 0 5Fill in a zero so both have three digits after the point.
  2. Tenths: 0 and 1The tenths digits already differ, and 1 tenth is more than 0 tenths. The later digits do not matter.

Answer: 0.1050 point 1 0 5 is larger, even though 0.090 point 0 9 has a bigger digit (9) in it.

4. Reading "or more," "or fewer" and "inclusive"

What you will be able to do

  • Match "or more," "at least," "or fewer" and "at most" to ≥greater than or equal to and ≤less than or equal to.
  • Tell when the boundary number itself is included.
  • List every whole number in a range that a phrase describes.
  • Read "between 2 and 5, inclusive."

Does the number itself count?

Phrases such as "3 or more" and "3 or fewer" include the 3. Phrases such as "more than 3" and "fewer than 3" leave it out. The word inclusive means both end numbers count, so "between 2 and 5, inclusive" includes 2 and 5 and everything between them.

Phrases and the symbols they match
PhraseSymbolIs the number itself included?
"3 or more," "at least 3," "no fewer than 3"x ≥ 3x is greater than or equal to 3Yes
"3 or fewer," "at most 3," "no more than 3"x ≤ 3x is less than or equal to 3Yes
"more than 3," "greater than 3"x > 3x is greater than 3No
"fewer than 3," "less than 3"x < 3x is less than 3No

Three slips to watch for

  • Leaving out the boundary. "3 or fewer" includes 3. Starting the list at 2 drops a value.
  • Forgetting 0. When the values start at 0, "3 or fewer" means 0, 1, 2 and 3. That is four values, not three.
  • Flipping the direction. "At least 4" means 4 or more, not 4 or fewer. "At least" sets the lowest value allowed.

Worked example: Which whole numbers from 0 to 7 are "5 or fewer"?

A list runs over the whole numbers 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. Which of them are "5 or fewer"?

  1. "5 or fewer" means x ≤ 5x is less than or equal to 5The 5 itself is included.
  2. Start at the bottom of the list: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5Go up from 0 and stop at 5. Six numbers fit, because the list starts at 0.

Answer: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5

5. Order of operations on one line

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply before adding or subtracting, even when the multiplication comes second.
  • Read an asterisk (*) as multiplication in a one-line entry.
  • Spot when parentheses would change the value.
  • Handle an answer that comes out negative.

Multiply first, then add or subtract

In a line such as 20 + 2 × 4.520 plus 2 times 4 point 5, the multiplication happens first, even though it sits on the right. Reading left to right and adding first gives a different, wrong value.

  1. Parentheses first.
  2. Multiplication and division next, from left to right.
  3. Addition and subtraction last, from left to right.

Calculators and StatCrunch use an asterisk (*) for multiplication when everything is typed on one line, so 20 + 2 * 4.520 plus 2 asterisk 4 point 5 means 20 + 2 × 4.520 plus 2 times 4 point 5. The calculator follows the same order of operations.

Three slips to watch for

  • Adding or subtracting first. 20 + 2 × 4.520 plus 2 times 4 point 5 is not 22 × 4.522 times 4 point 5. That would need parentheses: (20 + 2) × 4.5open parenthesis 20 plus 2 close parenthesis times 4 point 5.
  • Dropping the multiplication. 9.4 − 2 × 1.79 point 4 minus 2 times 1 point 7 takes away twice 1.71 point 7, not 2 and then 1.71 point 7.
  • Losing a negative sign. When the amount taken away is bigger than the starting number, the answer is negative: 1 − 3 = −21 minus 3 equals negative 2.

Worked example: 12.5 − 2 × 3.1

Evaluate 12.5 − 2 × 3.112 point 5 minus 2 times 3 point 1.

  1. 2 × 3.1 = 6.22 times 3 point 1 equals 6 point 2Multiply first.
  2. 12.5 − 6.2 = 6.312 point 5 minus 6 point 2 equals 6 point 3Then subtract.
  3. (12.5 − 2) × 3.1 = 32.55open parenthesis 12 point 5 minus 2 close parenthesis times 3 point 1 equals 32 point 5 5Subtracting first would give this very different value. That is why the order matters.

Answer: 6.36 point 3

6. Rounding at the end, to one decimal place

What you will be able to do

  • Round to one decimal place by looking only at the digit right after the tenths place.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Avoid rounding in steps from the right.
  • Keep every digit until the final answer, then round once.

How to round to one decimal place

  1. The kept digit is the tenths digit, the first digit after the point.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it, in the hundredths place.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Round once, at the end. When one result feeds into another computation, use every digit the calculator shows, and round only the final answer. Rounding a middle step can change the final answer.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. 4.34994 point 3 4 9 9 to one decimal place is 4.34 point 3. Rounding to 4.354 point 3 5 first and then to 4.44 point 4 is a slip. Only the hundredths digit, 4, matters.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 7.967 point 9 6 rounds to 8.08 point 0, not 7.107 point 1 0.
  • Rounding too early. Rounding a value before using it in the next step can move the final answer by a tenth or more.

Worked example: Round once, at the end

Find 3.2 + 2 × 1.463 point 2 plus 2 times 1 point 4 6, rounded to one decimal place.

  1. 2 × 1.46 = 2.922 times 1 point 4 6 equals 2 point 9 2Multiply first, keeping every digit.
  2. 3.2 + 2.92 = 6.123 point 2 plus 2 point 9 2 equals 6 point 1 2Add.
  3. Kept digit 1, next digit 2The 2 is 4 or less, so the 1 stays: 6.16 point 1.
  4. 3.2 + 2 × 1.5 = 6.23 point 2 plus 2 times 1 point 5 equals 6 point 2Rounding 1.461 point 4 6 to 1.51 point 5 first would give this different answer. That is why you round once, at the end.

Answer: 6.16 point 1

Directions decide the answer. "Round to one decimal place" means exactly one digit after the point. "Do not round" means type every digit the calculator shows.
