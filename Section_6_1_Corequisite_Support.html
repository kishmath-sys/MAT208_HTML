2. Comparing decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Compare two decimals by place value, even when one has more digits.
  • Put several decimals in order from smallest to largest.
  • Recognize decimals that are equal, such as 0.40 point 4 and 0.4000 point 4 0 0.

Line up the decimal points

The digits after the decimal point are tenths, hundredths, thousandths and ten-thousandths. To compare two decimals:

  1. Line up the decimal points.
  2. Fill any empty places at the end with zeros, so both numbers have the same number of digits after the point.
  3. Compare from left to right. The first place where the digits differ decides which number is larger.

Adding zeros at the end does not change a decimal. 0.40 point 4, 0.400 point 4 0 and 0.4000 point 4 0 0 are all the same number.

Place values in 0.58130 point 5 8 1 3
PlaceDigitValue
Tenths50.50 point 5
Hundredths80.080 point 0 8
Thousandths10.0010 point 0 0 1
Ten-thousandths30.00030 point 0 0 0 3

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer is larger. More digits do not make a decimal bigger. 0.12340 point 1 2 3 4 is smaller than 0.60 point 6, because 1 tenth is less than 6 tenths.
  • Reading the digits as a whole number. 9 is more than 1, but 0.090 point 0 9 is less than 0.10 point 1. Written with two digits each, they are 0.090 point 0 9 and 0.100 point 1 0.
  • Shorter is larger. Fewer digits do not make a decimal bigger either. 0.850 point 8 5 is larger than 0.70 point 7.

Worked example: Which is larger, 0.6 or 0.5813?

Which is larger, 0.60 point 6 or 0.58130 point 5 8 1 3?

  1. 0.6000 and 0.58130 point 6 0 0 0 and 0 point 5 8 1 3Fill in zeros so both numbers have four digits after the point.
  2. Tenths: 6 and 5The tenths digits already differ, and 6 is more than 5. The rest of the digits do not matter.

Answer: 0.60 point 6 is larger.

3. Negative numbers on a number line

What you will be able to do

  • Place a negative decimal between the right two whole numbers.
  • Decide which of two negative numbers is greater.
  • Put a mix of negative and positive decimals in order.
  • Find the number on the other side of 0 that is the same distance from 0.

Left is less, right is more

On a number line, numbers increase from left to right. Every negative number is to the left of 0, and every positive number is to the right of 0.

  • A number farther to the right is greater. So −0.6negative 0 point 6 is greater than −1.8negative 1 point 8, even though 1.81 point 8 is bigger than 0.60 point 6.
  • −1.25negative 1 point 2 5 is between −2negative 2 and −1negative 1. It is 0.250 point 2 5 from −1negative 1 and 0.750 point 7 5 from −2negative 2, so it is closer to −1negative 1.
  • 1.51 point 5 and −1.5negative 1 point 5 are opposites. They sit on opposite sides of 0, and each one is 1.51 point 5 units from 0.

Three slips to watch for

  • Comparing negatives as if they were positive. With two negative numbers, the one with the larger number after the minus sign is farther from 0, so it is farther left and smaller. −2.4negative 2 point 4 is less than −2.15negative 2 point 1 5.
  • Counting the wrong way from a whole number. −1.6negative 1 point 6 is between −2negative 2 and −1negative 1, not between −1negative 1 and 0.
  • Dropping the sign. −0.7negative 0 point 7 and 0.70 point 7 are different numbers on opposite sides of 0.

Worked example: Put four numbers in order

Put −0.6negative 0 point 6, −1.25negative 1 point 2 5, 0.30 point 3 and −1.8negative 1 point 8 in order from smallest to largest.

  1. The only positive number, 0.30 point 3, is the largest.Every negative number is to its left.
  2. Distances from 0: 1.81 point 8, 1.251 point 2 5, 0.60 point 6Among the negatives, the one farthest from 0 is farthest left, so it is the smallest.

4. Inequality symbols and words

What you will be able to do

  • Read <, >, ≤ and ≥ in words.
  • Decide whether a number makes an inequality true.
  • Describe the numbers that make an inequality true as a direction on a number line.
  • Write "between" with the smaller number on the left.

Four symbols

Inequality symbols and how to read them
SymbolRead it asDoes the number itself count?
<is less thanNo
>is greater thanNo
≤is less than or equal toYes
≥is greater than or equal toYes
  • x ≥ −1.5x is greater than or equal to negative 1 point 5 reads "x is greater than or equal to −1.5negative 1 point 5." The numbers that make it true are −1.5negative 1 point 5 itself and every number to its right on a number line.
  • x < 0.6x is less than 0 point 6 reads "x is less than 0.60 point 6." The numbers that make it true are every number to the left of 0.60 point 6, but not 0.60 point 6 itself.
  • −1 ≤ x ≤ 2negative 1 is less than or equal to x, which is less than or equal to 2 means x is between −1 and 2, including both ends. The smaller number goes on the left, just as it does on a number line.

Three slips to watch for

  • Flipping the symbol. x ≤ −0.8x is less than or equal to negative 0 point 8 and x ≥ −0.8x is greater than or equal to negative 0 point 8 describe opposite sides of the same number.
  • Forgetting "or equal to." 0.60 point 6 does not make x < 0.6x is less than 0 point 6 true, but it does make x ≤ 0.6x is less than or equal to 0 point 6 true.
  • Treating negatives as positives. −1.7negative 1 point 7 is not greater than −1.25negative 1 point 2 5. It is to the left.

Worked example: Which numbers make x ≥ −1.5 true?

Which of −2negative 2, −1.5negative 1 point 5, −0.4negative 0 point 4, 0 and 1.21 point 2 make x ≥ −1.5x is greater than or equal to negative 1 point 5 true?

  1. Read it: x is greater than or equal to −1.5negative 1 point 5Greater means to the right on a number line.
  2. −1.5negative 1 point 5 countsBecause of "or equal to," the number itself makes it true.
  3. −2negative 2 does not count−2negative 2 is to the left of −1.5negative 1 point 5. −0.4negative 0 point 4, 0 and 1.21 point 2 are to the right, so they count.

Answer: −1.5negative 1 point 5, −0.4negative 0 point 4, 0 and 1.21 point 2 (four numbers)

5. Rounding to two, three or four decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two, three or four decimal places.
  • Keep a zero at the end when rounding creates one.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Round a negative number.

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. Two decimal places keeps the hundredths digit, three keeps the thousandths digit, and four keeps the ten-thousandths digit.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Negative numbers. Round the digits exactly as if the number were positive, then keep the minus sign. −1.2861negative 1 point 2 8 6 1 to two decimal places is −1.29negative 1 point 2 9.

A zero made by rounding stays. 0.439820 point 4 3 9 8 2 to three decimal places is 0.4400 point 4 4 0, which shows all three decimal places. (0.440 point 4 4 has the same value.)

Four slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 0.23490 point 2 3 4 9 to two decimal places is 0.230 point 2 3. Rounding to 0.2350 point 2 3 5 first and then to 0.240 point 2 4 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. 0.51670 point 5 1 6 7 to two decimal places is 0.520 point 5 2, not 0.510 point 5 1.
  • A 9 that rounds up. A 9 that goes up by 1 becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.29960 point 2 9 9 6 to three decimal places is 0.3000 point 3 0 0.
  • Rounding a negative toward 0. −0.6287negative 0 point 6 2 8 7 to two decimal places is −0.63negative 0 point 6 3, not −0.62negative 0 point 6 2.

Worked example: Round 0.62849 two ways

Round 0.628490 point 6 2 8 4 9 to three decimal places, and then to two decimal places.

  1. Three decimal places: keep the 8, look at the 4The 4 is 4 or less, so the 8 stays: 0.6280 point 6 2 8.
  2. Two decimal places: keep the 2, look at the 8Start again from 0.628490 point 6 2 8 4 9. The 8 is 5 or more, so the 2 becomes 3: 0.630 point 6 3.

Answer: 0.6280 point 6 2 8 to three decimal places and 0.630 point 6 3 to two decimal places.

Directions decide the answer. "Round to three decimal places" means exactly three digits after the point. "Two decimal places" means exactly two. Read the direction at the end of each question before you round.
