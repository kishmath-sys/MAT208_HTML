2. Concepts

These checks cover items 1 to 5 on pages 1 and 2 of the Section 1.1 notes.

Check 1 (Data and Statistics). An animal shelter writes down the breed of every dog that arrives in March: beagle, boxer, mixed breed, beagle, and so on. Which statement matches items 1 and 2 of the notes?
Show answer

The list of breeds is data, and a single recorded breed is a datum.

Where this is: Items 1 and 2 (Data, Statistics) on page 1 of the notes.

Check 2 (Population, Census and Sample). A restaurant chain wants to know how long lunch service takes at its 48 restaurants, and it only cares about those 48 restaurants. Which statement matches items 3 to 5?
Show answer

Timing lunch at all 48 restaurants would be a census, and timing it at 6 of them would be a sample.

Where this is: Items 3 to 5 (Population, Census, Sample) on pages 1 and 2 of the notes.

If any of these felt shaky

3. Prepare the Study

These checks cover items 6 to 9 on pages 2 and 3 of the Section 1.1 notes.

Check 3 (Context). Which of these has context in the sense used in item 6?
Show answer

The number of minutes each of 30 patients waited at an urgent care clinic, collected to see whether a new check-in system shortens waits.

Where this is: Item 6 (Context) on page 2 of the notes.

Check 4 (Source of the Data). A trade group for dairy farmers pays for a study of whether drinking milk helps adults build stronger bones. Which statement matches item 7 and its Important Note?
Show answer

There is potential for bias because the source has a motive to get a certain result, but that does not guarantee the results are biased.

Where this is: Item 7 (Source of the Data) on page 2 of the notes, including the Important Note.

Check 5 (Sampling Method). A city wants to know how its residents feel about a new recycling program. Which plan is most likely to give a sample that represents all residents, in the sense of item 8?
Show answer

Have a computer randomly select 400 residents from a list of every resident.

Where this is: Item 8 (Sampling Method) on page 3 of the notes.

Check 6 (Voluntary Response Sample). A phone app asks users to rate a new feature, and 3,200 users choose to leave a rating. Which statement matches item 9 and its Important Note?
Show answer

This is a voluntary response sample, so it is often unrepresentative, though not always.

Where this is: Item 9 (Voluntary Response Sample) on page 3 of the notes, including the Important Note.

4. Analyze the Data and Conclusions

These checks cover items 10 to 13 on pages 4 and 5 of the notes and the Classroom Example 1.1.1 and 1.1.2 videos on page 13.

Check 7 (Graph and Explore). A scatterplot shows the outdoor temperature (horizontal axis) and a household's daily heating cost (vertical axis). The points form a fairly straight band that falls from the upper left to the lower right. Which description uses the language of item 10 correctly?
Show answer

A negative linear correlation: as the temperature increases, the heating cost decreases.

Where this is: Item 10 (Graph and Explore) on page 4 of the notes.

Check 8 (Apply Statistical Methods). A quality inspector randomly selects 250 bags of chips from one day's production and finds an average weight of 9.89 point 8 ounces. Which conclusion matches item 11?
Show answer

The average weight of all the bags made that day is likely close to 9.89 point 8 ounces, though it may differ slightly.

Where this is: Item 11 (Apply Statistical Methods) on page 4 of the notes.

Check 9 (Statistical Significance). A toy company says about 10% of its prize capsules hold a rare toy. A collector opens 400 capsules, so about 40 rare toys are expected. In Result A, the collector finds 43 rare toys. In Result B, the collector finds 112. Which result appears to be statistically significant?
Show answer

Result B only

Where this is: Item 12 (Statistical Significance) on page 5 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 1.1.2" on page 13.

Check 10 (Statistical and Practical Significance). In a study of a memory app, 6 users raised their test scores by an average of 18 points. There is about a 27% chance of getting results like these with an app that has no effect. Which statement matches the notes and the Classroom Example 1.1.1 video?
Show answer

It is not statistically significant, because a 27% chance is not unlikely enough.

Where this is: Items 12 and 13 (Statistical Significance, Practical Significance) on page 5 of the notes, including the Important Note, and the video "Classroom Example 1.1.1" on page 13.

Check 11 (Practical Significance). A city spends $3 million on new traffic signals. A formal analysis shows the drop in average commute time was unlikely to happen by chance. The average commute fell from 27.027 point 0 minutes to 26.826 point 8 minutes. Which conclusion matches item 13?
Show answer

Statistically significant, but not practically significant, since a drop of 0.20 point 2 minutes is hard to justify for $3 million.

Where this is: Item 13 (Practical Significance) on page 5 of the notes.

5. Pitfalls to Analyzing Data

These checks cover items 14 to 20 on pages 5 to 7 of the Section 1.1 notes.

Check 12 (Misleading Conclusion). Across many neighborhoods, the ones with more coffee shops tend to have higher home prices. A headline says, "Coffee shops drive up home prices." Which statement matches item 14?
Show answer

The headline is misleading, because correlation does not imply causation.

Where this is: Item 14 (Misleading Conclusion) on page 5 of the notes.

Check 13 (Reported Results and Loaded Questions). A clinic's intake form asks two questions. Q1: "How many hours of sleep did you get each night last week?" Q2: "Wouldn't you agree that our staff is friendly?" Which statement matches items 15 and 16?
Show answer

Q1 relies on what patients report, so answers may be off from forgetting or estimating, and Q2 is loaded because it pushes toward agreeing.

Where this is: Items 15 and 16 (Reported Results, Loaded Questions) on page 6 of the notes.

Check 14 (Order of Questions). Which survey has the problem described in item 17?
Show answer

A survey that asks, "How many traffic tickets have you received this year?" and then, "How would you rate the city's police department?"

Where this is: Item 17 (Order of Questions) on page 6 of the notes.

Check 15 (Nonresponse and Low Response Rates). A company sends a survey about a new manager to 600 employees. Only 30 send it back (a 5% response rate), and many of those 30 skip the questions about the manager. Which statement matches items 18 and 19?
Show answer

The low response rate and the skipped questions are both concerns, because the employees who did not answer may have different views from those who did.

Where this is: Items 18 and 19 (Nonresponse, Low Response Rates) on page 6 of the notes.

Check 16 (Misleading Use of Percentages). A store's ad says, "Online orders increased by 100% this month!" Last month the store had 40 online orders. Which reading is most reasonable?
Show answer

The store had 80 online orders this month, double last month.

Where this is: Item 20 (Misleading Use of Percentages) on page 7 of the notes.

If any of these felt shaky

  • Misleading Conclusion: item 14, page 5
  • Reported Results, Loaded Questions, Order of Questions, Nonresponse, Low Response Rates: items 15 to 19, page 6
  • Misleading Use of Percentages: item 20, page 7

6. StatCrunch check

These checks cover item 21 on pages 7 to 13 of the notes, its four StatCrunch videos, and the Classroom Example 1.1.3 and 1.1.4 videos on page 14.

Check 17 (Percent of a Number). In a survey of 1,387 renters, 46% said they would pay more for an apartment with in-unit laundry. Set this up in StatCrunch yourself and find the exact value of 46% of 1,387. Do not round.

Show answer

638.02

Where this is: Item 21a (Percentage of) on pages 7 and 8 of the notes, the video "Using StatCrunch to compute a percent of a number" on page 7, and the video "Classroom Example 1.1.4" on page 14.

Check 18 (Whole-Number Counts). Could 638.02 be the actual number of renters in Check 17 who said they would pay more? Which answer is correct? (Round to the nearest whole number as needed.)
Show answer

No. A count of people must be a whole number, so the actual number is 638.

Where this is: The video "Classroom Example 1.1.4" on page 14 of the notes.

Check 19 (Fraction to Percent). Among the same 1,387 renters, 151 said their building has no laundry room. Use StatCrunch to find the percentage of respondents who said this. Round to the nearest whole number.

Show answer

11

Where this is: Item 21c (Fraction to Percent) on pages 9 to 11 of the notes, the video "Using StatCrunch to convert a fraction to a percent" on page 10, and the videos "Classroom Example 1.1.3" and "Classroom Example 1.1.4" on page 14.

Check 20 (Decimal to Percent and Percent to Decimal). A StatCrunch result shows 0.0350 point 0 3 5, and a problem gives the percent 2.5%2 point 5 percent. Which pair of conversions is correct?
Show answer

0.0350 point 0 3 5 is 3.5%3 point 5 percent, and 2.5%2 point 5 percent is 0.0250 point 0 2 5.

Where this is: Items 21b (Decimal to Percent) on pages 8 and 9 and 21d (Percent to Decimal) on pages 12 and 13 of the notes, and the videos "Using StatCrunch to convert a decimal to a percent" on page 8 and "Using StatCrunch to convert a percent to a decimal" on page 12.
