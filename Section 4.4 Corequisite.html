2. Exponents and multiplying several numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply three or more numbers, in any order.
  • Read an exponent as repeated multiplication of the base.
  • Avoid the two common slips: multiplying the base by the exponent, and swapping the base and the exponent.
  • Work out powers such as 353 to the 5th power and 464 to the 6th power.

Multiplying several numbers

To multiply three or more numbers, multiply two of them, then multiply that result by the next number, and keep going. The order does not change the answer: 7 × 3 × 4 = 21 × 47 times 3 times 4 equals 21 times 4 and 4 × 7 × 3 = 28 × 34 times 7 times 3 equals 28 times 3 both give 84. A common slip is to add the numbers instead, which gives 14.

An exponent counts the factors

An exponent is shorthand for multiplying the same number by itself. In 343 to the 4th power, the 3 is the base and the small raised 4 is the exponent. The exponent tells how many times the base appears as a factor:

34 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 813 to the 4th power equals 3 times 3 times 3 times 3, which equals 81

Typed on one line, an exponent is often written with a caret, so 343 to the 4th power is typed 3^43 caret 4. Spoken, it is "3 to the 4th power."

Three slips to watch for

  • Multiplying the base by the exponent. 343 to the 4th power is not 3 × 43 times 4. That slip gives 12 instead of 81.
  • Swapping the base and the exponent. 434 to the 3rd power is 4 × 4 × 44 times 4 times 4, which is 64, not 81. The order matters.
  • Writing one factor too few or too many. 343 to the 4th power has exactly 4 threes. Count them before you multiply.

Worked example: Work out 2 to the 5th power

Work out 252 to the 5th power.

  1. 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 22 times 2 times 2 times 2 times 2The base is 2 and the exponent is 5, so write five 2s.
  2. 4 × 2 × 2 × 2 → 8 × 2 × 2 → 16 × 2 → 324 times 2 times 2 times 2, then 8 times 2 times 2, then 16 times 2, then 32Multiply one factor at a time, keeping track of the running result.
  3. Check: not 2 × 52 times 5 and not 525 to the 2nd power2 × 52 times 5 is 10 and 525 to the 2nd power is 25. Neither one is the same as 252 to the 5th power.

Answer: 32

3. Reading and typing large numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Read a number in the millions in groups of three digits.
  • Type a large number with or without commas and keep every zero.
  • Change a number written in words, or as "3.2 million," into digits.
  • Compare and subtract large numbers by lining up place values.

Groups of three

Commas split a whole number into groups of three digits, starting from the right. Each group has a name: ones, thousands, millions. Read each group as a number from 1 to 999, then say the group name.

The number 4,072,315 split into groups
GroupMillionsThousandsOnes
Digits4072315
Read asfour millionseventy-two thousandthree hundred fifteen

So 4,072,315 is read "four million, seventy-two thousand, three hundred fifteen." The zero in 072 holds the hundred thousands place. Leaving it out would give a different number.

Typing large numbers. Calculators and StatCrunch show results without commas, such as 4072315. To read one, count digits from the right and put a comma after every third digit.

Three slips to watch for

  • Dropping a zero group. "Two million, forty thousand, six" needs every place filled: 2,040,006. Each group after the millions has exactly three digits.
  • Comparing by the first digit. 989,999 starts with a 9, but it is less than 1,002,000, which has more digits. Count digits first.
  • Lining up from the left. When subtracting, line up the ones digits on the right, not the first digits on the left.

Worked example: Write 5.3 million in digits

Write 5.35 point 3 million as a whole number.

  1. 1 million = 1,000,0001 million equals 1,000,000One million is a 1 followed by six zeros.
  2. 5.3 × 1,000,000 = 5,300,0005 point 3 times 1,000,000 equals 5,300,000Multiplying by 1,000,000 moves the decimal point six places to the right. The 3 lands in the hundred thousands place.

Answer: 5,300,000

4. Fractions with 1 on top, and simplifying

What you will be able to do

  • Compare fractions that have 1 on top.
  • Put fractions with 1 on top in order from smallest to largest.
  • Fully simplify a fraction by dividing the top and bottom by a common factor.
  • Check that a fraction is fully simplified.

Fractions with 1 on top

A fraction such as 1301 over 30 means 1 part when something is split into 30 equal parts. Splitting into more parts makes each part smaller. So with 1 on top, a bigger bottom number means a smaller fraction: 13001 over 300 is smaller than 1301 over 30.

This runs against how whole numbers work, where 300 is bigger than 30. It is a slip adults make too, so slow down when you compare fractions.

Fully simplifying a fraction

A fraction is fully simplified when the top and bottom have no common factor other than 1. To simplify, divide the top and the bottom by the same number. You can do it in one step with the largest common factor, or in several smaller steps. Either way, keep going until nothing but 1 divides both.

Three slips to watch for

  • Stopping too soon. 6456 over 45 is equal to 129012 over 90, but both 6 and 45 still divide by 3. It is not fully simplified yet.
  • Dividing only the top or only the bottom. Whatever you divide the top by, divide the bottom by the same number.
  • Flipping the fraction. 171 over 7 and 717 over 1 are very different numbers. Keep the top on top.

Worked example: Simplify 12/90

Fully simplify 129012 over 90.

  1. 12 ÷ 2 = 6 and 90 ÷ 2 = 4512 divided by 2 equals 6, and 90 divided by 2 equals 45Both numbers are even, so divide the top and bottom by 2. That gives 6 over 45.
  2. 6 ÷ 3 = 2 and 45 ÷ 3 = 156 divided by 3 equals 2, and 45 divided by 3 equals 15Both 6 and 45 divide by 3, so keep going. That gives 2 over 15.
  3. Check 2 and 15Nothing but 1 divides both 2 and 15, so the fraction is fully simplified. Dividing by 6 at the start gets there in one step.

Answer: 2152 over 15

5. Fractions to decimals, rounding to five places

What you will be able to do

  • Change a fraction to a decimal by dividing the top by the bottom.
  • Count decimal places, including the zeros right after the decimal point.
  • Round a small decimal to five decimal places.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up, and avoid rounding in steps.

Fraction to decimal

A fraction bar means divide: 181 over 8 means 1 ÷ 81 divided by 8, which is 0.1250 point 1 2 5. Divide the top by the bottom, not the bottom by the top. When the top is smaller than the bottom, the decimal is less than 1.

Counting decimal places

Every digit after the decimal point is a decimal place, including zeros. In 0.012340 point 0 1 2 3 4, the five decimal places are 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4. Rounding to five decimal places means keeping exactly five digits after the point.

  1. Count five digits after the point, zeros included. The fifth one is the kept digit.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it, the sixth digit.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Three slips to watch for

  • Skipping the zeros. Starting the count at the first digit that is not zero keeps too many digits. In 0.0004080 point 0 0 0 4 0 8, the zeros after the point count as decimal places.
  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the sixth digit decides. 0.00834490 point 0 0 8 3 4 4 9 rounds to 0.008340 point 0 0 8 3 4 at five places, because the sixth digit is 4. Rounding the last digits first and working left gives 0.008350 point 0 0 8 3 5, which is a slip.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.02999640 point 0 2 9 9 9 6 4 to five decimal places is 0.030000 point 0 3 0 0 0.

Worked example: Write 1/70 as a decimal, rounded to five places

Write 1701 over 70 as a decimal. Round to five decimal places.

  1. 1 ÷ 70 = 0.0142857…1 divided by 70 equals 0 point 0 1 4 2 8 5 7, and it keeps goingDivide the top by the bottom.
  2. 0.01428 | 57…0 point 0 1 4 2 8, then 5 7Count five digits after the point: 0, 1, 4, 2, 8. The kept digit is 8, and the sixth digit is 5.
  3. 0.014290 point 0 1 4 2 9The 5 is 5 or more, so the 8 goes up to 9. Drop the rest.

Answer: 0.014290 point 0 1 4 2 9

Directions decide the answer. "Do not round" means type every digit. "Round to five decimal places" means exactly five digits after the point, zeros included.
