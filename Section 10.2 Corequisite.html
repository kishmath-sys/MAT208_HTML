2. Numbers written in thousands

What you will be able to do

  • Change a number to thousands by dividing by 1,000.
  • Change a number given in thousands back to the full number by multiplying by 1,000.
  • Keep zeros in place, such as 4.0754 point 0 7 5 for 4,075.
  • Check the direction: a bigger unit gives a smaller number.

A column labeled "in thousands"

Tables often save space by giving numbers in thousands. A column labeled "Altitude (thousands of feet)" with the entry 12 means 12 thousand feet, which is 12,000 feet.

  • To thousands: divide by 1,000. That moves the decimal point three places to the left.
  • From thousands back to the full number: multiply by 1,000. That moves the decimal point three places to the right. Fill any empty places with zeros.
  • Check the direction. A thousand feet is a bigger unit than a foot, so the number of thousands is always smaller than the number of feet.

Three slips to watch for

  • Moving only two places. Dividing by 100 instead of 1,000 turns 2,380 into 23.823 point 8, which is 10 times too big.
  • Losing a zero. 4,075 in thousands is 4.0754 point 0 7 5, not 4.754 point 7 5. The zero holds the hundreds place.
  • Going the wrong way. Changing 12.612 point 6 thousand to 0.01260 point 0 1 2 6 divides when it should multiply. The full number must be bigger.

Worked example: Write 7,450 feet in thousands of feet

Write 7,450 feet in thousands of feet.

  1. 7,450 ÷ 1,0007,450 divided by 1,000Changing to thousands means divide by 1,000.
  2. 7,450. → 745.0 → 74.50 → 7.4507,450 point, then 745 point 0, then 74 point 5 0, then 7 point 4 5 0Move the decimal point three places to the left, one place at a time.
  3. Check7 thousand feet is 7,000 feet, and 7,450 is a little more. A number a little over 7 fits.

Answer: 7.457 point 4 5 thousand feet

3. Negative numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Decide which of two negative numbers is smaller.
  • Put a list of positive and negative numbers in order.
  • Find how far apart a positive number and a negative number are.
  • Add a negative number, and multiply a negative number by a positive one.

Think of a thermometer or a number line

Numbers to the left on a number line (or lower on a thermometer) are smaller. Every negative number is smaller than 0, and among negatives, the one farther from 0 is the smaller one. So −41negative 41 is smaller (colder) than −30negative 30.

  • Adding a negative is the same as subtracting: 20 + (−6) = 1420 plus open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals 14.
  • Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: 20 − (−6) = 2620 minus open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis equals 26. This is how you find the distance from a negative number up to a positive one.
  • Multiplying a negative number by a positive number gives a negative result: −2.5 × 4 = −10negative 2 point 5 times 4 equals negative 10.

Three slips to watch for

  • Reading the digits only. 12 is bigger than 8, but −12negative 12 is smaller than −8negative 8.
  • Dropping the sign. From −9negative 9 up to 23 is 32 steps, not 14. Write the negative number in parentheses so its sign stays visible.
  • Losing the sign in a product. −3.5 × 4negative 3 point 5 times 4 is −14negative 14, not 14.

Worked example: A temperature drop

A thermometer reads 18°F at noon and −7°Fnegative 7 degrees Fahrenheit at midnight. How many degrees did the temperature drop?

  1. 18 − (−7)18 minus open parenthesis negative 7 close parenthesisThe drop is the higher temperature minus the lower one. Keep the negative in parentheses.
  2. 18 + 7 = 2518 plus 7 equals 25Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: 18 degrees down to 0, then 7 more.

Answer: a drop of 25 degrees

4. Reading and using a line's equation

What you will be able to do

  • Pick out the slope and the y-intercept when the equation is written with the y-intercept first.
  • Keep a minus sign with the number it is in front of.
  • Substitute a value for x and multiply before you add or subtract.
  • Handle decimals and a negative y-intercept or a negative result.

Two numbers in a line's equation

The equation of a straight line has two numbers that matter. One is multiplied by x, and one stands alone. The equation can be written in either order:

  • y = 30 + 2xy equals 30 plus 2 x and y = 2x + 30y equals 2 x plus 30 are the same line.
  • The number multiplied by x is the slope. Here it is 2.
  • The number standing alone is the y-intercept, where the line crosses the y-axis. Here it is 30.
  • A minus sign belongs to the number right after it. In y = 30 − 4.5xy equals 30 minus 4 point 5 x, the slope is −4.5negative 4 point 5. That is the same as writing y = 30 + (−4.5)xy equals 30 plus open parenthesis negative 4 point 5 close parenthesis x.

Finding y for a given x

Replace x with the number, in parentheses. Then follow the order of operations: multiply first, then add or subtract.

Three slips to watch for

  • Taking the first number as the slope. When the y-intercept is written first, the slope is the second number, the one next to x.
  • Adding before multiplying. 12 + 2.5 × 812 plus 2 point 5 times 8 is 12 + 20 = 3212 plus 20 equals 32, not 14.5 × 814 point 5 times 8.
  • Dropping the minus sign. In y = 64 − 3.2xy equals 64 minus 3 point 2 x, the product is subtracted, not added.

Worked example: y = 30 − 4.5xy equals 30 minus 4 point 5 x when x = 6

For the line y = 30 − 4.5xy equals 30 minus 4 point 5 x, name the slope and y-intercept, then find y when x = 6.

  1. Slope −4.5negative 4 point 5, y-intercept 30The number next to x, with its minus sign, is the slope. The number standing alone is the y-intercept.
  2. y = 30 − 4.5(6)y equals 30 minus 4 point 5 open parenthesis 6 close parenthesisReplace x with 6, in parentheses.
  3. 4.5 × 6 = 274 point 5 times 6 equals 27Multiply first.
  4. 30 − 27 = 330 minus 27 equals 3Then subtract.

Answer: slope −4.5negative 4 point 5, y-intercept 30, and y = 3 when x = 6

5. Rounding to a set number of decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to one, three or four decimal places.
  • Count decimal places starting right after the point, zeros included.
  • Keep the minus sign when you round a negative number.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up, and avoid rounding in steps.

How to round to a set number of decimal places

  1. Count places to the right of the decimal point, starting with the first digit after the point. Zeros count as places. In 0.05280 point 0 5 2 8, the 0 right after the point is the first place.
  2. Stop at the last place you keep. Look at the one digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept one. For a negative number, round the digits the same way and keep the minus sign.

Three slips to watch for

  • Skipping the zeros. Four decimal places for 0.0837160 point 0 8 3 7 1 6 is 0.08370 point 0 8 3 7. Starting the count at the 8 gives 0.083720 point 0 8 3 7 2, which has five places.
  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. −0.238449negative 0 point 2 3 8 4 4 9 to three decimal places is −0.238negative 0 point 2 3 8. Rounding it to −0.2385negative 0 point 2 3 8 5 first and then to −0.239negative 0 point 2 3 9 is a slip.
  • A 9 that rounds up. It becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.049680 point 0 4 9 6 8 to three decimal places is 0.0500 point 0 5 0.

Worked example: Round 0.0528490 point 0 5 2 8 4 9 two ways

Round 0.0528490 point 0 5 2 8 4 9 to four decimal places, and then to three decimal places.

  1. Four places: 0, 5, 2, 8. Next digit: 4The 4 is 4 or less, so the 8 stays: 0.05280 point 0 5 2 8.
  2. Three places: 0, 5, 2. Next digit: 8The 8 is 5 or more, so the 2 becomes 3: 0.0530 point 0 5 3.

Answer: 0.05280 point 0 5 2 8 to four decimal places and 0.0530 point 0 5 3 to three decimal places

Directions decide the answer. "One decimal place" means exactly one digit after the point. "Three decimal places" means three digits after the point, and "four decimal places" means four. "Round to the nearest whole number" means no digits after the point.

6. Reading paired tables and "within"

What you will be able to do

  • Read a table where each row (or column) is one pair of values.
  • Find the smallest and largest values in one variable.
  • Find the numbers that are within a given amount of a target.
  • Decide whether a value is within a given amount of another.

Tables of pairs

Many data tables list two values for each item, one pair per row or per column. To find a value, find the item first, then read across (or down) to the other variable. Keep the units from the labels.

What "within" means

"Within 4 of 30" means no more than 4 away from 30 in either direction. Subtract and add to find the smallest and largest numbers that count: 30 − 4 = 2630 minus 4 equals 26 and 30 + 4 = 3430 plus 4 equals 34. Any number from 26 to 34 is within 4 of 30.

Another way to check: find the gap between the two numbers (larger minus smaller) and compare it with the allowed amount.

Three slips to watch for

  • Checking only one side. "Within" goes both ways. 27 is within 4 of 30, and so is 33.
  • Reading the wrong row or column. Put a finger on the item first, then read straight across to its partner.
  • Dropping a sign when finding a gap. From −3negative 3 up to 24 is 27, not 21.

Worked example: Is 35 within 4 of 30?

Is 35 within 4 of 30?

  1. 30 − 4 = 2630 minus 4 equals 26 and 30 + 4 = 3430 plus 4 equals 34These are the smallest and largest numbers within 4 of 30.
  2. 35 is greater than 34So 35 is outside. The gap check agrees: 35 − 30 = 535 minus 30 equals 5, and 5 is more than 4.

Answer: No, 35 is not within 4 of 30.
