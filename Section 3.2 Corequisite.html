2. Comparing and ordering decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Compare two decimals by lining up the decimal points.
  • Find the largest and smallest numbers in an unsorted list, even when a value repeats.
  • Put decimals in order from smallest to largest.
  • Say whether a number is below, between or above two other numbers.

Line up the decimal points

To compare decimals, line them up by the decimal point and fill empty places with zeros so they have the same number of digits after the point. Then compare from the left, one place at a time. The first place where the digits differ decides which number is larger.

Comparing 4.074 point 0 7 and 4.74 point 7 place by place
NumberOnesTenthsHundredths
4.074 point 0 7407
4.704 point 7 0470

The ones digits match. In the tenths place, 7 is more than 0, so 4.74 point 7 is larger. The 7 in 4.074 point 0 7 is only 7 hundredths.

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer looks larger. More digits after the point does not make a number larger. 241.06241 point 0 6 is less than 241.6241 point 6, because 0 tenths is less than 6 tenths.
  • Shorter looks smaller. The reverse slip. 3.83 point 8 is larger than 3.083 point 0 8 and 3.0083 point 0 0 8, even though it has fewer digits.
  • Comparing only the digits after the point. Compare the whole-number parts first. 99.8599 point 8 5 is less than 214.06214 point 0 6, even though 85 is bigger than 06.

Below, between and above. A number is between two others when it is greater than the smaller one and less than the larger one. "Greater than" leaves out a number that is equal.

Worked example: Order four decimals

Put 4.074 point 0 7, 4.74 point 7, 4.0674 point 0 6 7 and 4.64 point 6 in order from smallest to largest.

  1. 4.070, 4.700, 4.067, 4.6004 point 0 7 0, 4 point 7 0 0, 4 point 0 6 7, 4 point 6 0 0Fill in zeros so each number has three digits after the point.
  2. Tenths digits: 0, 7, 0, 6The two numbers with 0 tenths are the smallest. Compare them at the hundredths place: 6 is less than 7, so 4.0674 point 0 6 7 comes before 4.074 point 0 7.
  3. Then 6 tenths, then 7 tenths4.64 point 6 comes before 4.74 point 7.

Answer: 4.0674 point 0 6 7, 4.074 point 0 7, 4.64 point 6, 4.74 point 7

3. Subtracting and order of operations

What you will be able to do

  • Subtract decimals by lining up the decimal points.
  • Work out expressions such as a + 2 × ba plus 2 times b and a − 2 × ba minus 2 times b with the multiplication first.
  • Keep the whole top of a fraction together when it is typed on one line.
  • Handle an answer that comes out negative.

Multiply before you add or subtract

  1. Parentheses first.
  2. Multiplication and division next, from left to right.
  3. Addition and subtraction last, from left to right.

Reading strictly from left to right is the most common slip, especially when the first operation you see is a subtraction or an addition. In 63.42 − 2 × 7.8663.42 minus 2 times 7.86, the multiplication 2 × 7.862 times 7.86 has to happen before the subtraction.

On one line, many calculators use * for times and / for divide. A fraction bar groups its whole top, so when a fraction is typed on one line, the top needs parentheses: 96.2 − 40.64the fraction with 96.2 minus 40.6 on top and 4 on the bottom becomes (96.2 − 40.6) / 4open parenthesis 96.2 minus 40.6 close parenthesis, slash 4.

Subtracting decimals. Line up the decimal points and fill empty places with zeros, so 125.6 − 98.75125.6 minus 98.75 becomes 125.60 − 98.75125.60 minus 98.75. Then subtract, borrowing where you need to.

Worked example: 63.42 − 2 × 7.86

Evaluate 63.42 − 2 × 7.8663.42 minus 2 times 7.86.

  1. 2 × 7.86 = 15.722 times 7.86 equals 15.72Multiply first.
  2. 63.42 − 15.72 = 47.7063.42 minus 15.72 equals 47.70Then subtract. Line up the decimal points.
  3. (63.42 − 2) × 7.86 = 482.7612open parenthesis 63.42 minus 2 close parenthesis, times 7.86, equals 482.7612Working left to right would give this very different value. That is why the order matters.

Answer: 47.747 point 7 (also written 47.7047 point 7 0)

4. Squaring a number

What you will be able to do

  • Square a whole number or a decimal.
  • Tell squaring apart from doubling.
  • Place the decimal point when you square a decimal.
  • Name the units of an area, such as square feet.

Squaring means a number times itself

A small raised 2 means multiply the number by itself: 7² = 7 × 7 = 497 squared equals 7 times 7, which equals 49. It does not mean times 2. Doubling 7 gives 14, which is a different operation.

  • Decimals: count the digits after the point in the number, then double that count for the answer. 1.21 point 2 has one digit after the point, so 1.2²1 point 2 squared has two: 1.441 point 4 4.
  • Decimals less than 1 get smaller. 0.4² = 0.160 point 4 squared equals 0 point 1 6, which is less than 0.40 point 4.
  • Units square too. A square that is 3 feet on each side has an area of 3 ft × 3 ft = 9 ft²3 feet times 3 feet equals 9 square feet, read "9 square feet" or "9 feet squared."

Three slips to watch for

  • Doubling instead of squaring. 12²12 squared is 144, not 24.
  • Squaring each part separately. 15.3²15 point 3 squared is not 15² + 0.3²15 squared plus 0 point 3 squared. Multiply 15.3 × 15.315.3 times 15.3.
  • Too few decimal places. 0.4²0 point 4 squared is 0.160 point 1 6, not 1.61 point 6 or 0.80 point 8.

Worked example: Find 2.5²

Find 2.5²2 point 5 squared.

  1. 2.5 × 2.52.5 times 2.5Squaring means multiplying the number by itself.
  2. 25 × 25 = 62525 times 25 equals 625Multiply without the decimal points first.
  3. 6.256 point 2 5Each 2.5 has one digit after the point, so the answer has two. A quick check: 2.5 is between 2 and 3, so its square is between 4 and 9.

Answer: 6.256 point 2 5 (not 5, which is 2.5 doubled)

5. Rounding and when to round

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two decimal places and to one decimal place.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Round only once, from the full number, not in steps.
  • Keep every digit until the last step of a calculation.

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. For one decimal place, it is the tenths digit. For two decimal places, it is the hundredths digit.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Wait until the end

When a calculation has more than one step, keep every digit the calculator shows until the final answer, then round once. Rounding the numbers along the way can change the last digit of the answer.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps. 18.444918 point 4 4 4 9 to one decimal place is 18.418 point 4. Going 18.4449 → 18.445 → 18.45 → 18.518.4449, then 18.445, then 18.45, then 18.5 is a slip. Only the digit right after the kept digit matters.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. 73.485173 point 4 8 5 1 to two decimal places is 73.4973 point 4 9, not 73.4873 point 4 8.
  • A 9 that rounds up. 209.996209 point 9 9 6 to two decimal places is 210.00210 point 0 0. The 9s turn to 0s and carry 1 to the left.

Worked example: Round at the end

Evaluate 41.83333 − 2 × 6.2746141.83333 minus 2 times 6.27461. Round the final answer to two decimal places.

  1. 2 × 6.27461 = 12.549222 times 6.27461 equals 12.54922Multiply first, keeping every digit.
  2. 41.83333 − 12.54922 = 29.2841141.83333 minus 12.54922 equals 29.28411Subtract, still keeping every digit.
  3. 29.28411 → 29.2829.28411 rounds to 29.28Round once at the end. The digit after the 8 is 4, so the 8 stays.
  4. 41.83 − 2 × 6.27 = 29.2941.83 minus 2 times 6.27 equals 29.29Rounding both numbers first would give 29.29, which is off by one in the last place.

Answer: 29.2829 point 2 8

Directions decide the answer. "Do not round" means type every digit. "Round to two decimal places" means exactly two digits after the point. "If rounding is necessary, then round to one decimal place" means round only when the answer has more than one digit after the point.
