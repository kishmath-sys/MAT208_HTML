2. Percents become decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Change a percent to a decimal, including percents under 10% and percents such as 0.8%0 point 8 percent.
  • Change a percent written in words to a decimal.
  • Change a decimal from a calculator back to a percent.

Divide by 100: two places to the left

Percent means "per hundred," so 35% is 35 hundredths, or 0.350 point 3 5. Before a percent goes into a calculation, change it to a decimal by dividing by 100. That moves the decimal point two places to the left. Fill any empty places with zeros.

To go back from a decimal to a percent, multiply by 100. That moves the point two places to the right.

Percents and their decimals
PercentDecimalZeros needed after the point
35%0.350 point 3 5none
6%0.060 point 0 6one
2.75%2 point 7 5 percent0.02750 point 0 2 7 5one
0.4%0 point 4 percent0.0040 point 0 0 4two

Three slips to watch for

  • Moving only one place. 6% is 0.060 point 0 6, not 0.60 point 6. 0.60 point 6 is 60%.
  • Leaving the percent as a whole number. Typing 6 where 0.060 point 0 6 belongs makes the value 100 times too big.
  • Percents written in words. "Seven and a half percent" is 7.5%7 point 5 percent, which is 0.0750 point 0 7 5. Write the number first, then move the point. The same goes for "percentage points": "seven and a half percentage points" is also written 0.0750 point 0 7 5 when you compute with it.

Worked example: Write 92% and 6.5% as decimals

Write 92% and 6.5%6 point 5 percent as decimals.

  1. 92% → 0.9292 percent becomes 0 point 9 2The point starts after the 2 and moves two places left.
  2. 6.5 → 0.65 → 0.0656 point 5, then 0 point 6 5, then 0 point 0 6 5One place left gives 0.65. The second place needs a zero in front of the 6.

Answer: 0.920 point 9 2 and 0.0650 point 0 6 5

3. Part, total and percent of a number

What you will be able to do

  • Find the total when a sentence gives two separate groups.
  • Divide a part by a total to get a decimal.
  • Find a percent of a number, and round a count of people or things to a whole number.
  • Answer a two-step question about the group that is left.

Find the total first

Many sentences give the size of each group instead of the total. "35 voted yes and 15 voted no" means 50 people voted. The total is the sum of the groups, and each group is a part of it.

part ÷ total = decimalpart divided by total equals decimal

To find a percent of a number, change the percent to a decimal and multiply. When the answer counts people or things, it must be a whole number, so follow the rounding direction, such as "round to the nearest integer." An integer here means a whole number.

Three slips to watch for

  • Using one group as the total. In "35 yes and 15 no," dividing by 35 leaves out the 15 people who said no.
  • Flipping the division. The part goes on top. If the part is smaller than the total, the decimal must be less than 1.
  • Chopping a count. 263.9 people rounds to 264, not 263.

Worked example: Club members who drive

A club has 26 members who drive to meetings and 39 who do not. What decimal of the members drive?

  1. 26 + 39 = 6526 plus 39 equals 65Two groups are given, so add them to get the total.
  2. 26 ÷ 65 = 0.426 divided by 65 equals 0 point 4Divide the part (drivers) by the total (all members).

Answer: 0.40 point 4 (not 26 ÷ 3926 divided by 39, which uses only the members who do not drive as the total)

4. Rounding to three or four decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to three or four decimal places, and to the nearest integer.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up, and keep a zero at the end when it is one of the kept places.
  • Look at only the one digit after the last kept digit.
  • Wait until the final answer to round.

How to round

  1. Count the places you keep. Three decimal places keeps the thousandths digit, the third after the point. Four decimal places keeps the fourth.
  2. Look at the single digit right after the last kept digit.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the last kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave it alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept places.

A zero can be one of the kept places. 0.1700 point 1 7 0 rounded to three decimal places is written 0.1700 point 1 7 0, with the zero, to show three places were kept. It has the same value as 0.170 point 1 7.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. 0.314490 point 3 1 4 4 9 to three decimal places is 0.3140 point 3 1 4. Rounding to 0.31450 point 3 1 4 5 first and then to 0.3150 point 3 1 5 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. 0.452810 point 4 5 2 8 1 to three decimal places is 0.4530 point 4 5 3, not 0.4520 point 4 5 2, because the next digit is 8.
  • A 9 that rounds up. It becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.04960 point 0 4 9 6 to three decimal places is 0.0500 point 0 5 0.
Round only at the end. If a result goes into another calculation, keep every digit until the final answer. Rounding a middle step can change the last digit of the final answer.

Worked example: Round 0.528614 two ways

Round 0.5286140 point 5 2 8 6 1 4 to three decimal places, and then to four decimal places.

  1. Three places: keep 0.528, look at the 6The 6 is 5 or more, so the 8 becomes 9: 0.5290 point 5 2 9.
  2. Four places: keep 0.5286, look at the 1The 1 is 4 or less, so the 6 stays: 0.52860 point 5 2 8 6.

Answer: 0.5290 point 5 2 9 to three decimal places and 0.52860 point 5 2 8 6 to four.

5. Comparing decimals and "between"

What you will be able to do

  • Compare decimals that have different numbers of digits.
  • Change a percent to a decimal before comparing it with a decimal.
  • Decide whether a number is between two other numbers.

Line up the places

To compare two decimals, write them with the same number of digits after the point by adding zeros at the end. Adding zeros at the end does not change the value. Then compare digit by digit from the left.

A number is between two others when it is larger than the smaller one and smaller than the larger one.

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer looks larger. 0.2360 point 2 3 6 has more digits than 0.250 point 2 5, but 0.250 point 2 5 is 0.2500 point 2 5 0, which is larger.
  • Comparing a percent with a decimal. 40% is 0.400 point 4 0. Comparing the 40 with 0.4310 point 4 3 1 as if both were decimals gives the wrong answer.
  • Shorter looks larger. Some people decide 0.30 point 3 is smaller than 0.290 point 2 9 because 3 is smaller than 29. Written as 0.300 point 3 0, it is larger.

Worked example: Is 0.4 larger or smaller than 0.386?

Which is larger, 0.40 point 4 or 0.3860 point 3 8 6?

  1. 0.4 = 0.4000 point 4 equals 0 point 4 0 0Add zeros so both have three digits after the point.
  2. 400 thousandths and 386 thousandthsCompare: 400 is more than 386.

Answer: 0.40 point 4 is larger.

6. Order of operations on one line

What you will be able to do

  • Work inside parentheses first.
  • Type a fraction with a subtraction on top so the whole top is divided.
  • Multiply a number by a decimal typed with an asterisk.
  • Keep every digit through a long calculation.

The order

  1. Parentheses first.
  2. Multiplication and division next, from left to right.
  3. Addition and subtraction last, from left to right.

On one line, a slash (/) means divide and an asterisk (*) means multiply. A fraction bar groups everything above it, so when the top of a fraction is a subtraction, the top needs parentheses on one line. Without them, only the last number of the top gets divided.

Worked example: (7.84 − 5.26) / 2

Evaluate (7.84 − 5.26) / 2open parenthesis 7 point 8 4 minus 5 point 2 6 close parenthesis, slash 2.

  1. 7.84 − 5.26 = 2.587 point 8 4 minus 5 point 2 6 equals 2 point 5 8Parentheses first.
  2. 2.58 / 2 = 1.292 point 5 8 slash 2 equals 1 point 2 9Then divide.
  3. 7.84 − 5.26 / 2 = 5.217 point 8 4 minus 5 point 2 6 slash 2 equals 5 point 2 1Without the parentheses, only 5.26 is divided by 2, and the value is very different.

Answer: 1.291 point 2 9
