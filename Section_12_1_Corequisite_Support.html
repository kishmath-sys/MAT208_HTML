2. Minutes and seconds to seconds

What you will be able to do

  • Change a time such as 4:374 minutes 37 seconds into seconds only.
  • Explain why 3:143 minutes 14 seconds is not 3.143 point 1 4 and not 314 seconds.
  • Find how many seconds longer one time is than another.
  • Add several times by changing each one to seconds first.

Time counts in sixties

A time written like 4:374 minutes 37 seconds means 4 minutes and 37 seconds. One minute is 60 seconds, so to write the whole time in seconds:

seconds = (minutes × 60) + secondstotal seconds equals, open parenthesis minutes times 60 close parenthesis, plus the seconds

The colon is not a decimal point. Our number system counts in tens, but time counts in sixties, so the digits after the colon can only run from 00 to 59.

Three slips to watch for

  • Reading the colon as a decimal point. 2:452 minutes 45 seconds is not 2.452 point 4 5 minutes. Half a minute is 30 seconds, so 2:302 minutes 30 seconds is 2.52 point 5 minutes.
  • Squeezing the digits together. 2:452 minutes 45 seconds is not 245 seconds. That would be more than 4 minutes.
  • Dropping the leading zero in the seconds. 5:085 minutes 8 seconds has 8 seconds, not 80. The 0 holds the tens place.

Worked example: Write 4:37 in seconds

Write 4:374 minutes 37 seconds in seconds only.

  1. 4 × 60 = 2404 times 60 equals 240Change the 4 minutes to seconds.
  2. 240 + 37 = 277240 plus 37 equals 277Add the 37 seconds that were already there.
  3. Check: 277 is between 240 and 300Every time from 4 minutes to 5 minutes is between 240 and 300 seconds, so 277 fits.

Answer: 277 seconds

3. Rounding to a set number of decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Find the kept digit for two, three or four decimal places.
  • Count zeros right after the decimal point as places.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Avoid rounding in steps from the right, and avoid chopping.

How to round

  1. Count places after the decimal point. Two decimal places means the hundredths digit is the last one you keep. Three means the thousandths digit. Four means the ten-thousandths digit.
  2. Look at the single digit right after the kept digit.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit. Keep any zeros that are needed to show the number of places, such as 0.0500 point 0 5 0 for three places.

Zeros right after the decimal point count as places. In 0.0001420 point 0 0 0 1 4 2, the first four places are 0, 0, 0 and 1.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 0.44490 point 4 4 4 9 to two decimal places is 0.440 point 4 4. Rounding to 0.4450 point 4 4 5 first and then to 0.450 point 4 5 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. Dropping digits without looking at the next one is not rounding. 0.26870 point 2 6 8 7 to two decimal places is 0.270 point 2 7, not 0.260 point 2 6.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.3960 point 3 9 6 to two decimal places is 0.400 point 4 0.

Worked example: Round 0.46271 two ways

Round 0.462710 point 4 6 2 7 1 to two decimal places, and then to three decimal places.

  1. Two decimal places: keep the 6, look at the 2The 2 is 4 or less, so the 6 stays: 0.460 point 4 6.
  2. Three decimal places: keep the 2, look at the 7The 7 is 5 or more, so the 2 becomes 3: 0.4630 point 4 6 3.

Answer: 0.460 point 4 6 to two decimal places and 0.4630 point 4 6 3 to three decimal places.

Directions decide the answer. "Two decimal places" means exactly two digits after the point. "Four decimal places" means exactly four. "Do not round" means type every digit the calculator shows.

4. Comparing decimals and reading inequality symbols

What you will be able to do

  • Decide which of two decimals is larger, even when they have different numbers of digits.
  • Put a short list of decimals in order.
  • Read the symbols <, >, ≤less than or equal to, ≥greater than or equal to and ≠not equal to.
  • Decide whether a statement with one of these symbols is true, including when the two numbers are equal.

Line up the decimal points

To compare two decimals, write them with the decimal points lined up and add zeros at the end until they have the same number of digits. Adding zeros at the end does not change a decimal's value. Then compare digit by digit from the left. The first place where they differ decides it.

For example, compare 0.30 point 3 and 0.28750 point 2 8 7 5. Write 0.30 point 3 as 0.30000 point 3 0 0 0. In the tenths place, 3 is more than 2, so 0.30 point 3 is larger even though it has fewer digits.

Inequality symbols and how to read them
SymbolRead it asA true example
<is less than0.09 < 0.10 point 0 9 is less than 0 point 1
>is greater than0.4 > 0.380 point 4 is greater than 0 point 3 8
≤less than or equal to symbolis less than or equal to0.2 ≤ 0.200 point 2 is less than or equal to 0 point 2 0
≥greater than or equal to symbolis greater than or equal to0.7 ≥ 0.070 point 7 is greater than or equal to 0 point 0 7
≠not equal to symbolis not equal to0.5 ≠ 0.050 point 5 is not equal to 0 point 0 5

The open side of < or > faces the larger number. The line under ≤less than or equal to or ≥greater than or equal to means equal also counts, so 0.2 ≤ 0.200 point 2 is less than or equal to 0 point 2 0 is true.

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer is larger. More digits does not mean a bigger number. 0.09990 point 0 9 9 9 is less than 0.10 point 1.
  • Ignoring zeros after the point. 0.0090 point 0 0 9 is not bigger than 0.050 point 0 5 just because 9 is bigger than 5. The zeros push the 9 into a smaller place.
  • Forgetting the "or equal to." 0.6 ≤ 0.60 point 6 is less than or equal to 0 point 6 is true, but 0.6 < 0.60 point 6 is less than 0 point 6 is false.

Worked example: Is 0.0612 greater than 0.07?

Decide whether 0.0612 > 0.070 point 0 6 1 2 is greater than 0 point 0 7 is true.

  1. 0.0612 and 0.07000 point 0 6 1 2 and 0 point 0 7 0 0Add zeros to the shorter one so both have four digits after the point.
  2. Tenths: 0 and 0. Hundredths: 6 and 7.The hundredths place is the first place that differs, and 6 is less than 7.

Answer: false. 0.06120 point 0 6 1 2 is less than 0.070 point 0 7, even though it has more digits.

5. Reading values from a table

What you will be able to do

  • Find one value in a table from its row and column.
  • Add the values down a column, without mixing in values from a row.
  • Count the values in a table that meet a condition such as "greater than 83."
  • Read a table where each group runs across a row instead of down a column.

Rows go across, columns go down

A table's columns run up and down, and each one has a heading at the top. Its rows run side to side, and each one has a label on the left. To find one value, go down the right column and across the right row to the cell where they meet.

Some tables put each group in its own column. Others put each group in its own row. Read the headings before you read any numbers, so you know which way the groups run.

Three slips to watch for

  • Reading across when the question says down. A column total adds the values under one heading, not the values in one row.
  • Landing one row off. Keep a finger or the cursor on the row label as you move across.
  • Counting the edge value wrong. "Greater than 83" leaves out 83 itself. "83 or more" includes it.
