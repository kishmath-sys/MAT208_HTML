2. Square roots

What you will be able to do

  • Find the square root of a perfect square, such as √49the square root of 49.
  • Estimate a square root that is not a whole number by finding the two whole numbers it falls between.
  • Use a calculator square root key and round the result.
  • Avoid the "divide by 2" slip and the "square it" slip.

Undoing a square

The square root of a number is the number that, multiplied by itself, gives that number. √49the square root of 49 is 7 because 7 × 7 = 497 times 7 equals 49. The sign √ means "the square root of."

A perfect square is a whole number times itself. Knowing the perfect squares up to 144 makes square roots quick to find and quick to estimate.

For a number that is not a perfect square, the square root falls between two whole numbers. √12the square root of 12 is between 3 and 4, because 12 is between 9 and 16. A calculator gives 3.46413 point 4 6 4 1 and more digits.

Three slips to watch for

  • Dividing by 2. √36the square root of 36 is 6, not 18. Check by multiplying: 6 × 6 = 366 times 6 equals 36, but 18 × 18 = 32418 times 18 equals 324.
  • Squaring instead. √9the square root of 9 is 3, not 81. The square root undoes squaring, so the answer is smaller than the number whenever the number is greater than 1.
  • Expecting the root to grow as fast as the number. √16the square root of 16 is 4, but √64the square root of 64 is 8, not 16. Multiplying the number by 4 only doubles its square root.

3. Dividing by a square root on one line

What you will be able to do

  • Divide a number by a square root by finding the square root first.
  • Write a fraction with a square root on the bottom as a one-line expression.
  • Keep the square root exact until the last step, and round only the final answer.
  • Spot an answer that took the square root of the whole fraction by mistake.

The square root covers only the bottom

In 24√924 divided by the square root of 9, the square root sign covers only the 9. Find √9 = 3the square root of 9 equals 3 first, then divide: 24 ÷ 3 = 824 divided by 3 equals 8.

On one line, many calculators write the square root as sqrt( ) with the number inside the parentheses. So 24√924 divided by the square root of 9 becomes 24/sqrt(9)24 slash square root of 9. The parentheses after sqrt hold only the number under the root sign.

Let the calculator keep every digit

When the square root is not a whole number, rounding it before you divide can change the final digits. 30√730 divided by the square root of 7 is 11.338911 point 3 3 8 9 to four decimal places. If you round √7the square root of 7 to 2.62 point 6 first, 30 ÷ 2.630 divided by 2 point 6 gives 11.538511 point 5 3 8 5, which is off. Do the whole calculation, then round once at the end.

Three slips to watch for

  • Taking the square root of the whole fraction. sqrt(24/9)square root of, open parenthesis 24 slash 9 close parenthesis is about 1.631 point 6 3, not 8.
  • Forgetting the square root. 24 ÷ 924 divided by 9 is about 2.672 point 6 7, not 8.
  • Rounding the square root first. Round only the final answer.

Worked example: 42 divided by √49

Find 42√4942 divided by the square root of 49.

  1. √49 = 7the square root of 49 equals 7Find the square root of the bottom first.
  2. 42 ÷ 7 = 642 divided by 7 equals 6Then divide.
  3. 42/sqrt(49)42 slash square root of 49On one line, the same calculation looks like this. It also gives 6.

Answer: 6

4. Rounding to three or four decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to three or four decimal places.
  • Look at only the one digit right after the last kept digit.
  • Carry when a 9 rounds up, even all the way into the ones place.
  • Keep a zero at the end when it is one of the kept digits.

Places after the decimal point

The places after the decimal point in 0.51830 point 5 1 8 3
PlaceDigit
Tenths (1st place)5
Hundredths (2nd place)1
Thousandths (3rd place)8
Ten-thousandths (4th place)3

"Three decimal places" means three digits after the point. "Four decimal places" means four.

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep: the 3rd digit after the point for three places, the 4th for four places.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit, but keep every kept digit, even a zero at the end.

Four slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. 0.274490 point 2 7 4 4 9 to three places is 0.2740 point 2 7 4. Only the 4 right after the kept digit matters. Rounding to 0.27450 point 2 7 4 5 first and then to 0.2750 point 2 7 5 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. 0.61380 point 6 1 3 8 to three places is 0.6140 point 6 1 4, not 0.6130 point 6 1 3.
  • A 9 that rounds up. A 9 that goes up by 1 becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.299970 point 2 9 9 9 7 to four places is 0.30000 point 3 0 0 0.
  • Dropping a kept zero. 0.30000 point 3 0 0 0 and 0.30 point 3 have the same value, but when the directions say four decimal places, write all four digits: 0.30000 point 3 0 0 0.

Worked example: Round 0.38461923 two ways

Round 0.384619230 point 3 8 4 6 1 9 2 3 to three decimal places, and then to four decimal places.

  1. Three places: keep the 4, look at the 6The 6 is 5 or more, so the 4 becomes 5: 0.3850 point 3 8 5.
  2. Four places: keep the 6, look at the 1The 1 is 4 or less, so the 6 stays: 0.38460 point 3 8 4 6.

Answer: 0.3850 point 3 8 5 to three decimal places and 0.38460 point 3 8 4 6 to four decimal places.

5. Inequality symbols and number lines

What you will be able to do

  • Read the symbols <, >, ≤ and ≥.
  • Match phrases such as "more than," "exceeds," "at least" and "no more than" to a symbol.
  • Decide whether the number on the edge of a statement is included.
  • Place negative numbers and decimals on a number line, and pick out the numbers between two values.

Symbols and the words that go with them

Inequality symbols, how to read them, and whether the edge number is included
SymbolPhrases that mean itIs the edge number included?
<less than symbolless than, fewer than, belowNo
>greater than symbolgreater than, more than, exceeds, aboveNo
≤less than or equal to symbolat most, no more than, does not exceedYes
≥greater than or equal to symbolat least, no less than, or moreYes

The symbol always opens toward the larger side. t > 18t is greater than 18 says t is the larger side, so 18 itself does not count, but 18.118 point 1 does.

The number line

Numbers increase from left to right. Every negative number is to the left of 0, and among negative numbers, the one farther from 0 is smaller. So −3.2negative 3 point 2 is less than −1.8negative 1 point 8, even though 3.2 is more than 1.8.

"Between 2 and 5" means greater than 2 and less than 5. On a number line, it is the stretch from 2 to 5.

Three slips to watch for

  • Including the edge for < or >. t > 18t is greater than 18 does not include 18.
  • Ordering negatives by their digits. −3.2negative 3 point 2 is less than −1.8negative 1 point 8, not more.
  • Flipping the symbol. "Exceeds 58" means more than 58, so the symbol is >, not <.

Worked example: A trail age rule

A trail is open to hikers who are at least 12 years old. Write the rule with a for age, and decide whether a 12-year-old can go.

  1. a ≥ 12a is greater than or equal to 12"At least 12" means 12 or more, so the symbol includes the edge.
  2. 12 ≥ 12 is trueThe edge number is included, so a 12-year-old can go.

Answer: a ≥ 12a is greater than or equal to 12, and yes, a 12-year-old can go.

6. Equal shares and decimals as percents

What you will be able to do

  • Split a total into equal shares by dividing.
  • Pick out the total and the number of shares in a sentence.
  • See how each share changes when the number of shares changes.
  • Change a decimal to a percent and compare it with 50%.

Equal shares

When a total is split equally, divide the total by the number of shares.

each share = total ÷ number of shareseach share equals total divided by number of shares

  • The total is the whole amount, such as pounds, dollars or milliliters.
  • The number of shares is how many people or items split it.
  • Fewer shares means each share is bigger. More shares means each share is smaller.

Decimals as percents

To change a decimal to a percent, multiply by 100, which moves the decimal point two places to the right. 0.50 point 5 is 50%, so any decimal above 0.50 point 5 is more than half, and any decimal below it is less than half.

Three slips to watch for

  • Dividing the wrong way. 30 ÷ 2,40030 divided by 2,400 gives a tiny number. The total goes first.
  • Multiplying instead of dividing. Sharing a total makes each share smaller than the total, never bigger.
  • Moving the point only one place. 0.080 point 0 8 is 8%, not 80%.

Worked example: Boxes in a van

A delivery van can carry 2,400 pounds of cargo. It is loaded with 30 boxes that all weigh the same. What is the heaviest each box can be?

  1. Total: 2,400 pounds. Shares: 30 boxes.Label both numbers first.
  2. 2,400 ÷ 30 = 802,400 divided by 30 equals 80Divide the total by the number of shares.

Answer: 80 pounds per box
