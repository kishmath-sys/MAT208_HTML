2. Percents and fractions as decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Change a percent to a decimal, including one-digit percents such as 4%.
  • Change a percent with a decimal in it, such as 2.5%2 point 5 percent, to a decimal.
  • Change a fraction to a decimal by dividing the top by the bottom.
  • Compare numbers written as percents, decimals and fractions.

Two moves to know

  • Percent to decimal: divide by 100. That moves the decimal point two places to the left. Fill any empty places with zeros, so 4% becomes 0.040 point 0 4.
  • Fraction to decimal: divide the top by the bottom. For example, 383 over 8 is 3 ÷ 8 = 0.3753 divided by 8 equals 0 point 3 7 5.

Three slips to watch for

  • Moving only one place. 4% is 0.040 point 0 4, not 0.40 point 4. 0.40 point 4 is 40%.
  • Dropping the percent sign without converting. 30% is 0.30 point 3, not 30.
  • Dividing the bottom by the top. 141 over 4 is 1 ÷ 4 = 0.251 divided by 4 equals 0 point 2 5, not 4 ÷ 1 = 44 divided by 1 equals 4. A fraction with a smaller top than bottom is always less than 1.

Worked example: Write 3% as a decimal

Write 3% as a decimal.

  1. 3 ÷ 1003 divided by 100Percent to decimal means divide by 100.
  2. 3 → 3.0 → 0.3 → 0.033, then 3 point 0, then 0 point 3, then 0 point 0 3The point starts after the 3. Move it one place left, then a second place left. The second move needs a zero right after the point.

Answer: 0.030 point 0 3 (not 0.30 point 3, which would be 30%)

3. Subtracting from 1

What you will be able to do

  • Subtract a decimal from 1 by writing 1 with the same number of decimal places.
  • Regroup (borrow) across zeros correctly.
  • Subtract a fraction from 1 by writing 1 as a fraction with the same bottom.
  • Check a subtraction by adding the answer back.

Give 1 the same number of places

The number 1 has no digits after the decimal point, but you can add as many zeros as you need: 1 = 1.0 = 1.00 = 1.000. Before you subtract, write 1 with as many decimal places as the other number, line up the decimal points, then subtract column by column.

For a fraction, write 1 as a fraction with the same bottom. For example, 1 is 555 over 5, so 1 − 2/5 = 5/5 − 2/5 = 3/51 minus 2 fifths equals 5 fifths minus 2 fifths equals 3 fifths.

Quick check. Add your answer to the number you subtracted. The total should be exactly 1.

Three slips to watch for

  • Bringing the decimal part down. 1 − 0.31 minus 0 point 3 is not 1.31 point 3. Write 1 as 1.01 point 0 and subtract: 0.70 point 7.
  • Taking every digit away from 10. After regrouping 1.00, the tenths place holds 9 and only the last place holds 10. So 1.00 − 0.361 point 0 0 minus 0 point 3 6 is 0.640 point 6 4, not 0.740 point 7 4.
  • Subtracting tops and bottoms. With fractions, only the tops are subtracted, after the bottoms match.

Worked example: 1 − 0.27

Find 1 − 0.271 minus 0 point 2 7.

  1. 1.00 − 0.271 point 0 0 minus 0 point 2 70.27 has two decimal places, so write 1 as 1.00 and line up the points.
  2. Regroup 1.00 as 0 ones, 9 tenths and 10 hundredthsBorrowing across the zeros leaves 9 in the tenths place and 10 in the hundredths place.
  3. 10 − 7 = 3, 9 − 2 = 7, 0 − 0 = 010 minus 7 equals 3, 9 minus 2 equals 7, 0 minus 0 equals 0Subtract each column, starting at the right.
  4. 0.73 + 0.27 = 1.000 point 7 3 plus 0 point 2 7 equals 1 point 0 0Check by adding back.

Answer: 0.730 point 7 3

4. Multiplying decimals and powers

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply decimals and place the decimal point correctly.
  • Read a power, such as 0.630 point 6 to the third power, as repeated multiplication.
  • Raise a fraction to a power by multiplying the tops and the bottoms.
  • Predict whether a product of decimals between 0 and 1 is bigger or smaller than the numbers you started with.

Placing the decimal point

To multiply decimals, multiply as if there were no decimal points. Then count the decimal places in all the numbers you multiplied. The answer gets that many decimal places, with zeros added in front if needed. For example, 0.3 × 0.20 point 3 times 0 point 2: 3 × 2 = 63 times 2 equals 6, and there are 2 decimal places in all, so the answer is 0.060 point 0 6.

Powers are repeated multiplication

A small raised number is a power. It tells how many times the number is used as a factor. So 0.430 point 4 to the third power means 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.40 point 4 times 0 point 4 times 0 point 4. For a fraction, multiply all the tops and all the bottoms: (2/3)² = (2 × 2)/(3 × 3) = 4/92 thirds squared equals 2 times 2 over 3 times 3, which is 4 ninths.

Three slips to watch for

  • Multiplying by the power. 0.430 point 4 to the third power is not 3 × 0.4 = 1.23 times 0 point 4 equals 1 point 2. The 3 says how many factors, not what to multiply by.
  • Too few decimal places. 0.3 × 0.20 point 3 times 0 point 2 is 0.060 point 0 6, not 0.60 point 6.
  • Expecting a bigger answer. A product of numbers between 0 and 1 is smaller than each of them.

5. Order of operations with powers

What you will be able to do

  • Work out powers before multiplying and subtracting.
  • Multiply before subtracting, even when the subtraction comes first in the line.
  • Use parentheses to change the order on purpose.
  • Choose the one-line version of an expression that keeps its value.

The order

  1. Parentheses first.
  2. Powers next.
  3. Multiplication and division, working from left to right.
  4. Addition and subtraction last, working from left to right.

So in 1 − 0.4 × 0.41 minus 0 point 4 times 0 point 4, the multiplication happens before the subtraction, even though the subtraction is written first. When a line is typed into a calculator, * often stands for times and ^ for a power, so 0.430 point 4 to the third power can be typed as 0.4^3.

Worked example: 1 − 0.4 × 0.4

Evaluate 1 − 0.4 × 0.41 minus 0 point 4 times 0 point 4.

  1. 0.4 × 0.4 = 0.160 point 4 times 0 point 4 equals 0 point 1 6Multiplication comes before subtraction.
  2. 1 − 0.16 = 0.841 minus 0 point 1 6 equals 0 point 8 4Then subtract from 1.
  3. (1 − 0.4) × 0.4 = 0.24open parenthesis 1 minus 0 point 4 close parenthesis, times 0 point 4, equals 0 point 2 4Subtracting first would give this different value. Only parentheses make the subtraction go first.

Answer: 0.840 point 8 4

6. Rounding to several decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to three, four or six decimal places.
  • Count zeros right after the decimal point as decimal places.
  • Carry correctly when a 9, or a run of 9s, rounds up.
  • Round once, from the full number, instead of in steps.

How to round

  1. Count that many digits after the decimal point. The last one is the kept digit. Zeros right after the point count.
  2. Look at the single digit right after the kept digit.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit. Keep any zeros up to the kept digit, so the answer shows the number of places asked for.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. 0.244490 point 2 4 4 4 9 to three decimal places is 0.2440 point 2 4 4. Rounding to 0.24450 point 2 4 4 5 first and then to 0.2450 point 2 4 5 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. 0.61870 point 6 1 8 7 to three decimal places is 0.6190 point 6 1 9, not 0.6180 point 6 1 8.
  • A 9 that rounds up. It becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left, which may carry again. 0.29970 point 2 9 9 7 to three decimal places is 0.3000 point 3 0 0.

Worked example: Round 0.427351 two ways

Round 0.4273510 point 4 2 7 3 5 1 to three decimal places, and then to four decimal places.

  1. Three places: keep the 7, look at the 3The 3 is 4 or less, so the 7 stays: 0.4270 point 4 2 7.
  2. Four places: keep the 3, look at the 5The 5 is 5 or more, so the 3 becomes 4: 0.42740 point 4 2 7 4.

Answer: 0.4270 point 4 2 7 to three decimal places and 0.42740 point 4 2 7 4 to four decimal places.
