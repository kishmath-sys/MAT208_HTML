2. Totals and differences from counts

What you will be able to do

  • Find a total when a sentence gives the count of each group.
  • Find half of a count.
  • Find how far apart two counts are, whichever one is larger.

Read every count before you add

Many sentences give the size of each group but never state the total. If every item falls in exactly one of the groups, the total is the sum of the groups.

  • "A clinic saw 41 adults and 19 children, and no one else." The total is 41 + 19 = 6041 plus 19 equals 60 patients.
  • Half of a count: divide it by 2. Half of 60 is 30.
  • How far apart: subtract the smaller count from the larger one. 26 and 30 are 4 apart, and so are 34 and 30.

Three slips to watch for

  • Using one group as the total. If 41 adults and 19 children were seen, the total is 60, not 41.
  • Dropping a carry. Line up the ones, tens and hundreds, and carry when a column adds to 10 or more.
  • Thinking a smaller number is always farther away. Distance does not depend on which side a number is on. Subtract to compare.

Worked example: Count the votes

A club vote had 157 yes votes and 68 no votes, and every member voted yes or no. How many members voted? Which count is farther from 100?

  1. 157 + 68 = 225157 plus 68 equals 225Add the two groups. The ones column is 7 + 8 = 15, so write 5 and carry 1. The tens column is 5 + 6 + 1 = 12, so write 2 and carry 1.
  2. 157 − 100 = 57; 100 − 68 = 32157 minus 100 equals 57, and 100 minus 68 equals 32Subtract the smaller number from the larger one each time. 57 is more than 32.

Answer: 225 members voted, and 157 is farther from 100 than 68 is.

3. Writing and simplifying fractions

What you will be able to do

  • Write a fraction with the part on top and the total on the bottom.
  • Simplify a fraction by dividing the top and the bottom by the same number.
  • Tell when a fraction is fully simplified.

Part over total, then simplify

When a sentence says "18 of the 30 apples are red," the fraction of the apples that are red is 183018 over 30. The part goes on top and the total goes on the bottom.

To simplify, divide the top and the bottom by the same number. A fraction is fully simplified when the only whole number that divides both the top and the bottom is 1. Dividing by the largest number that goes into both finishes the job in one step.

Three slips to watch for

  • Stopping too soon. 183018 over 30 divided by 2 on top and bottom is 9159 over 15, but 3 still divides 9 and 15.
  • Subtracting instead of dividing. Taking the same number away from the top and the bottom changes the value of the fraction.
  • Putting a part on the bottom. If 18 apples are red and 12 are green, the bottom is the total, 30, not the 12 green apples.

Worked example: Simplify 18/30

Of 30 apples in a crate, 18 are red. Write the fraction of the apples that are red, fully simplified.

  1. 183018 over 30Part on top, total on the bottom.
  2. 18 ÷ 6 = 3 and 30 ÷ 6 = 518 divided by 6 equals 3, and 30 divided by 6 equals 56 is the largest number that goes into both 18 and 30.
  3. 353 over 5Only 1 divides both 3 and 5, so this is fully simplified.

Answer: 353 over 5

4. Fractions, decimals and percents

What you will be able to do

  • Change a fraction to a decimal by dividing the top by the bottom.
  • Change a decimal to a fraction and simplify it.
  • Change a percent to a decimal, including one-digit percents such as 4%.

Three forms of the same amount

  • Fraction to decimal: divide the top by the bottom. 7207 over 20 means 7 ÷ 207 divided by 20, which is 0.350 point 3 5.
  • Decimal to fraction: read the place value. 0.350 point 3 5 is 35 hundredths, so it is 3510035 over 100, which simplifies to 7207 over 20.
  • Percent to decimal: divide by 100, which moves the decimal point two places to the left. 72% is 0.720 point 7 2, and 4% is 0.040 point 0 4.
Some fractions and their decimals

Some fractions never end as decimals. 131 over 3 is 0.333…0 point 3 3 3, repeating, with the 3s going on forever. The fraction 131 over 3 is exact, and any decimal you type for it has to be rounded.

Three slips to watch for

  • Dividing the bottom by the top. 252 over 5 is 2 ÷ 52 divided by 5, which is 0.40 point 4. 5 ÷ 25 divided by 2 is 2.52 point 5, which is a different number.
  • Writing the digits side by side. 383 over 8 is not 0.380 point 3 8 or 3.83 point 8. Divide to find out what it is.
  • Moving the decimal point only one place. 4% is 0.040 point 0 4, not 0.40 point 4. 0.40 point 4 is 40%.

Worked example: Write 7/20 as a decimal

Write 7207 over 20 as a decimal.

  1. 7 ÷ 20 = 0.357 divided by 20 equals 0 point 3 5Divide the top by the bottom.
  2. Check: 7207 over 20 = 3510035 over 100Multiplying the top and the bottom by 5 gives 35 hundredths, which is 0.350 point 3 5.

Answer: 0.350 point 3 5

5. Comparing decimals and subtracting from 1

What you will be able to do

  • Compare decimals by lining up the decimal points.
  • Decide whether a decimal falls between two other decimals.
  • Subtract a decimal from 1.

Compare place by place

To compare decimals, line up the decimal points and give them the same number of digits by adding zeros at the end. Then compare them like whole numbers.

  • 0.050 point 0 5 and 0.50 point 5: write 0.050 point 0 5 and 0.500 point 5 0. 5 hundredths is less than 50 hundredths, so 0.050 point 0 5 is smaller.
  • 0.40 point 4 and 0.3750 point 3 7 5: write 0.4000 point 4 0 0 and 0.3750 point 3 7 5. 400 is more than 375, so 0.40 point 4 is larger, even though it has fewer digits.
  • A number is between two others when it is larger than the smaller one and smaller than the larger one.

Subtracting from 1

Write 1 with as many decimal places as the other number, such as 1.001 point 0 0, then subtract column by column, borrowing as needed.

Three slips to watch for

  • Thinking more digits means larger. 0.0450 point 0 4 5 has more digits than 0.050 point 0 5, but it is smaller.
  • Bringing down the decimals. 1 − 0.261 minus 0 point 2 6 is not 1.261 point 2 6. Write 1 as 1.001 point 0 0 first.
  • Borrowing without lowering the next digit. After you borrow from a column, that column is one less.

Worked example: Compare, then subtract from 1

Which is larger, 0.30 point 3 or 0.280 point 2 8? Then find 1 − 0.281 minus 0 point 2 8.

  1. 0.300 point 3 0 and 0.280 point 2 8Add a zero so both have two digits. 30 hundredths is more than 28 hundredths, so 0.30 point 3 is larger.
  2. 1.00 − 0.281 point 0 0 minus 0 point 2 8Write 1 as 1.001 point 0 0. Borrowing turns 1.001 point 0 0 into 0 ones, 9 tenths and 10 hundredths.
  3. 10 − 8 = 2, then 9 − 2 = 710 minus 8 equals 2, then 9 minus 2 equals 7Subtract the hundredths, then the tenths.

Answer: 0.30 point 3 is larger, and 1 − 0.28 = 0.721 minus 0 point 2 8 equals 0 point 7 2.

6. Rounding to two or three decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two decimal places or three decimal places.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Divide, then round the result.
  • Follow "as needed."

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. For two decimal places, it is the hundredths digit, the second digit after the point. For three decimal places, it is the thousandths digit, the third digit after the point.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

"As needed" means round only if there are more digits than the direction asks for. If a result already has two decimal places and the direction says two decimal places as needed, type it as it is.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 0.417490 point 4 1 7 4 9 to three decimal places is 0.4170 point 4 1 7. Rounding to 0.41750 point 4 1 7 5 first and then to 0.4180 point 4 1 8 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. 0.63890 point 6 3 8 9 to two decimal places is 0.640 point 6 4, not 0.630 point 6 3.
  • A 9 that rounds up. A 9 that goes up by 1 becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.2960 point 2 9 6 to two decimal places is 0.300 point 3 0.

Worked example: Round 0.58362 two ways

Round 0.583620 point 5 8 3 6 2 to three decimal places, and then to two decimal places.

  1. Three decimal places: keep the 3, look at the 6The 6 is 5 or more, so the 3 becomes 4: 0.5840 point 5 8 4.
  2. Two decimal places: keep the 8, look at the 3The 3 is 4 or less, so the 8 stays: 0.580 point 5 8.

Answer: 0.5840 point 5 8 4 to three decimal places and 0.580 point 5 8 to two decimal places.
