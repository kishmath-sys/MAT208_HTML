2. At least one and its complement

These checks cover items 1 and 2 on page 1 of the Section 4.3 notes.

Check 1 (The Concept of At Least One). A building has 6 elevators. The number working on a given morning could be any whole number from 0 to 6. Which set matches "at least one elevator is working" in the sense of item 1?
Show answer

{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

Where this is: Item 1 (The Concept of "At Least One") on page 1 of the notes.

Check 2 (At Least One Is Not Only One). A manager says, "At least one of our 3 new hires will finish training this week." All 3 finish. Which statement matches item 1 and its Important Discussion?
Show answer

The manager was right, because "at least one" includes all three finishing.

Where this is: Item 1 (The Concept of "At Least One") on page 1 of the notes, including the Important Discussion.

Check 3 (The Complement of At Least One). A store ships 4 orders. Which event is the complement of "at least one order arrives late"?
Show answer

No order arrives late.

Where this is: Item 2 (The Complement of "At Least One") on page 1 of the notes.

Check 4 (The Complement as a Set). For 5 flights, the number delayed could be 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5. Which set is the complement of "at least one flight is delayed"?
Show answer

{0}

Where this is: Item 2 (The Complement of "At Least One") on page 1 of the notes, including the Important Discussion.

3. The probability of at least one

These checks cover item 3 on page 2 of the notes and Classroom Examples 4.3.1 to 4.3.3 and their videos on page 3.

Check 5 (Formula for At Least One). For a set of 4 smoke detectors, the probability that none of them fails a test is 0.820 point 8 2. Use the formula in item 3 to find the probability that at least one fails the test. Do not round.

Show answer

0.180 point 1 8

Where this is: Item 3 (The Probability of "At Least One") on page 2 of the notes.

Check 6 (Rationale). For one situation, a student writes P(no successes) = 0.350 point 3 5 and P(at least one success) = 0.750 point 7 5. Which statement matches the Rationale in item 3?
Show answer

These cannot both be right, because the two values must add to 1.

Where this is: Item 3 (The Probability of "At Least One") on page 2 of the notes, including the Rationale.

Check 7 (Applying the Formula). A fair coin is tossed 4 times, and each toss lands heads with probability 121 over 2. Use the method of the Example in item 3 to find the probability of at least one head. Round to three decimal places.

Show answer

0.9380 point 9 3 8

Where this is: Item 3 (The Probability of "At Least One") on page 2 of the notes, including its Example.

Check 8 (Why Use the Complement Rule). The Important Discussion in item 3 compares two ways to find "at least one." Which statement matches it?
Show answer

Adding the separate probabilities is time-consuming, so the complement rule is quicker.

Where this is: Item 3 (The Probability of "At Least One") on page 2 of the notes, including the Important Discussion.

Check 9 (Setting Up the Factors). In a poll, 72% of shoppers say they compare prices online before buying. You want the probability that at least one of three randomly selected shoppers does this, using 1 − P(none). Which decimal goes in each of the three factors of P(none)?
Show answer

0.280 point 2 8 in every factor

Where this is: Item 3 (The Probability of "At Least One") on page 2 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 4.3.1" on page 3.

Check 10 (Volunteered Responses). Suppose the 72% in Check 9 came from shoppers who volunteered to answer an online poll. How does that affect the result?
Show answer

It is very possible the result is not valid, because the sample may not be representative of all shoppers.

Where this is: The video "Classroom Example 4.3.1" on page 3 of the notes.

Check 11 (Several Independent Copies). Your photos are stored on 3 independent drives, and each has a 5% chance of failing in a year. Which expression gives the probability that at least one drive works all year?

Check 12 (When a Mixture Tests Positive). Samples from 4 people are combined into one mixture, as in Classroom Example 4.3.3. When does the mixture test positive?
Show answer

When at least one of the 4 samples is positive.

Where this is: The video "Classroom Example 4.3.3" on page 3 of the notes.

Check 13 (Is It Low Enough?). For a mixture of samples, the probability of a positive result works out to 0.410 point 4 1. Is that low enough that further testing of the individual samples is rarely necessary?
Show answer

No. The probability is not low, so further testing will frequently be necessary.

Where this is: The video "Classroom Example 4.3.3" on page 3 of the notes.
