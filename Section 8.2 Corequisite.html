2. Percents and decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Change a whole-number percent to a decimal, including one-digit percents such as 4%.
  • Change a percent with a decimal point, such as 62.8%62 point 8 percent, to a decimal.
  • Change a small decimal to a percent.
  • Recognize the same amount written as a percent, a decimal and a fraction.

Percent means "out of 100"

Percent means "per hundred," so moving between a percent and a decimal comes down to dividing or multiplying by 100. Problems often give a percent in a sentence, such as "38.5%38 point 5 percent of the people surveyed," and the work that follows uses the decimal.

  • Percent to decimal: divide by 100. The decimal point moves two places to the left. Fill any empty places with zeros.
  • Decimal to percent: multiply by 100. The decimal point moves two places to the right, then add the percent sign.
  • Half: one half is 121 over 2, which is 0.50 point 5 and 50%.

Three slips to watch for

  • Moving only one place. 4% is 0.040 point 0 4, not 0.40 point 4. 0.40 point 4 is 40%.
  • Keeping the percent sign on a decimal. 0.640 point 6 4 is 64%. Writing 0.64%0 point 6 4 percent names an amount 100 times smaller.
  • Reading the digits as the percent. 0.0350 point 0 3 5 is not 35%. It is 35 thousandths, which is 3.5%3 point 5 percent.

3. Counts from percents and totals

What you will be able to do

  • Find a count from a percent of a total by changing the percent to a decimal and multiplying.
  • Round a count of people or things to a whole number.
  • Find a total by adding two groups, and find the rest of a group by subtracting.
  • Find what percent one group is of a total made of two groups.

From a percent to a count

When a problem says "36.5%36 point 5 percent of 820 people," the number of people is 36.5%36 point 5 percent of 820. Change the percent to a decimal and multiply. A count of people or things must be a whole number, so a result such as 299.3299 point 3 is rounded to a whole number.

Totals and the rest

Some problems give two groups instead of a total, such as "312 seeds sprouted and 88 did not." The total is the sum of the two groups: 312 + 88 = 400312 plus 88 equals 400 seeds. When a problem gives a total and one group, the rest is the total minus that group.

Three slips to watch for

  • Ignoring the percent sign. Multiplying by 36.5 instead of 0.3650 point 3 6 5 gives an answer 100 times too big.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. 356.9356 point 9 rounds to 357, not 356. Look at the digit after the point.
  • Using one group as the total. When a problem gives two groups, the total is both groups added together.

Worked example: 36.5% of 820 people

A survey of 820 people found that 36.5%36 point 5 percent of them work weekends. How many people is that, to the nearest whole number?

  1. 36.5% = 0.36536 point 5 percent equals 0 point 3 6 5Change the percent to a decimal by moving the point two places left.
  2. 0.365 × 820 = 299.30 point 3 6 5 times 820 equals 299 point 3Multiply.
  3. Round to 299The digit after the point is 3, which is 4 or less, so the 299 stays. A count of people must be a whole number.

Answer: 299 people

4. Comparing decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Compare two decimals by lining up the decimal points.
  • Avoid treating the decimal with more digits as the larger one.
  • Recognize that 0.050 point 0 5 and 0.0500 point 0 5 0 are equal.
  • Put several small decimals in order.

Line up the decimal points

  1. Write the numbers one above the other with the decimal points lined up.
  2. Add zeros at the end so both have the same number of digits after the point. This does not change either number.
  3. Compare digit by digit from the left. The first place where the digits differ decides which number is larger.

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer looks larger. 0.03720 point 0 3 7 2 has more digits than 0.050 point 0 5, but it is smaller. Written as 0.03720 point 0 3 7 2 and 0.05000 point 0 5 0 0, the hundredths digits are 3 and 5.
  • Shorter looks larger. 0.40 point 4 is not larger than 0.450 point 4 5 just because it is shorter. As 0.400 point 4 0 and 0.450 point 4 5, the second is larger.
  • Zeros at the end. 0.050 point 0 5 and 0.05000 point 0 5 0 0 are the same number. A zero at the end of a decimal does not change its value.

Worked example: Compare 0.0083 and 0.01

Which is larger, 0.00830 point 0 0 8 3 or 0.010 point 0 1?

  1. 0.00830 point 0 0 8 3 and 0.01000 point 0 1 0 0Add zeros so both have four digits after the point.
  2. Tenths: 0 and 0. Hundredths: 0 and 1.The hundredths place is the first place that differs, and 1 is more than 0.

Answer: 0.010 point 0 1 is larger

5. Comparison words and symbols

What you will be able to do

  • Match comparison words with the symbols >, <, ≥, ≤, = and ≠.
  • Tell whether a phrase includes the number itself.
  • Read a statement with a symbol from left to right.
  • Decide whether a number makes a statement such as x > 0.65x is greater than 0 point 6 5 true.

Words and the symbols they match

Comparison words and their symbols
WordsSymbolIs the number itself included?
more than, greater than, above>is greater thanNo
fewer than, less than, below<is less thanNo
at least, no fewer than≥is greater than or equal toYes

Read a symbol from left to right. n < 20n is less than 20 says "n is less than 20." Written the other way, 20 > n20 is greater than n says "20 is greater than n," which is the same statement. The small, pointed end of < or > points to the smaller number.

Three slips to watch for

  • "At least" read as "more than." At least 12 includes 12 itself, so it is ≥, not >.
  • "No more than" read as "less than." No more than 7 allows exactly 7, so it is ≤.
  • Reading the symbol backward. 20 < n20 is less than n says n is more than 20, not less.

Worked example: At least 48 inches

A ride says riders must be at least 48 inches tall. Write this with h for a rider's height in inches.

  1. "At least" means 48 or moreA rider who is exactly 48 inches tall may ride, so 48 is included.
  2. h ≥ 48h is greater than or equal to 48The symbol ≥ means "greater than or equal to."

Answer: h ≥ 48h is greater than or equal to 48

6. Rounding small and negative decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two, three or four decimal places, counting zeros right after the point.
  • Round a negative number and keep its minus sign.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Look only at the one digit right after the last kept digit.

How to round

  1. Count places after the decimal point to find the last digit you keep. Zeros right after the point count as places.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Negative numbers. Round the digits exactly as you would without the minus sign, then keep the minus sign. −1.836negative 1 point 8 3 6 to two decimal places is −1.84negative 1 point 8 4, because 1.8361 point 8 3 6 rounds to 1.841 point 8 4.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. −0.2449negative 0 point 2 4 4 9 to two decimal places is −0.24negative 0 point 2 4. Rounding to −0.245negative 0 point 2 4 5 first and then to −0.25negative 0 point 2 5 is a slip.
  • "Rounding up" a negative toward zero. For −1.836negative 1 point 8 3 6, the next digit is 6, so the 3 becomes 4: −1.84negative 1 point 8 4. Writing −1.83negative 1 point 8 3 chops the digit instead.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.079520 point 0 7 9 5 2 to three decimal places is 0.0800 point 0 8 0.

Worked example: Round −3.4462 and 0.07952

Round −3.4462negative 3 point 4 4 6 2 to two decimal places. Then round 0.079520 point 0 7 9 5 2 to three decimal places.

  1. Two decimal places: keep the 4 in the hundredths place, look at the 6The 6 is 5 or more, so the 4 becomes 5. Keep the minus sign: −3.45negative 3 point 4 5.
  2. Three decimal places: keep the 9 in the thousandths place, look at the 5The 5 is 5 or more, so the 9 goes up. It becomes 0 and carries, so 0.079 becomes 0.0800 point 0 8 0.

Answer: −3.45negative 3 point 4 5 and 0.0800 point 0 8 0

Directions decide the answer. "Two decimal places" means exactly two digits after the point, so −1.70negative 1 point 7 0 keeps its zero. "Do not round" means type every digit the calculator shows.
