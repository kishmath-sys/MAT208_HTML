2. Subtracting decimals when the answer is negative

What you will be able to do

  • Line up decimal points and fill empty places with zeros before subtracting.
  • Subtract a larger number from a smaller one and give the answer the right sign.
  • Keep the order of a subtraction the way the problem states it.

When the number taken away is larger

To find 62 − 74.8562 minus 74 point 8 5, notice that more is being taken away than you start with, so the answer is below zero. A reliable way to do it:

  1. Subtract the smaller number from the larger one, as usual.
  2. Give the answer a negative sign, because the number being subtracted was the larger one.

Before subtracting decimals, line up the decimal points. A whole number such as 62 has its decimal point at the end, so it can be written 62.0062 point 0 0 to match 74.8574 point 8 5.

Three slips to watch for

  • Dropping the sign. 62 − 74.8562 minus 74 point 8 5 is −12.85negative 12 point 8 5, not 12.8512 point 8 5.
  • Bringing down a digit. In 128.4 − 93.75128 point 4 minus 93 point 7 5, the 4 is really 4.404 point 4 0. The 0 still has to be subtracted from, so the 5 cannot just be copied down.
  • Switching the order. 4.2 − 7.84 point 2 minus 7 point 8 and 7.8 − 4.27 point 8 minus 4 point 2 have the same size but opposite signs.

Worked example: 62 − 74.85

Find 62 − 74.8562 minus 74 point 8 5.

  1. 74.85 − 62.00 = 12.8574 point 8 5 minus 62 point 0 0 equals 12 point 8 5Line up the decimal points and subtract the smaller number from the larger one.
  2. 62 − 74.85 = −12.8562 minus 74 point 8 5 equals negative 12 point 8 5The larger number, 74.85, was being subtracted, so the answer is negative.

Answer: −12.85negative 12 point 8 5

3. Dividing with negatives and rounding to two decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Divide when one of the numbers is negative and give the answer the right sign.
  • Read a question such as "how many 12.8s fit in 57.6" as a division in the right order.
  • Round to two decimal places, including negative numbers and numbers that carry.

Signs in division

  • A positive number divided by a positive number is positive.
  • A negative number divided by a positive number is negative. For example, −12 ÷ 4 = −3negative 12 divided by 4 equals negative 3.

Divide the sizes as usual, then attach the sign. Order matters: 12 ÷ 412 divided by 4 is 3, but 4 ÷ 124 divided by 12 is about 0.330 point 3 3. A question that asks how many times one amount fits into another divides the amount being measured by the size of one piece.

Rounding to two decimal places

Keep two digits after the decimal point. Look only at the third digit after the point:

  • If it is 5 or more, round the second digit up.
  • If it is 4 or less, leave the second digit as it is.
  • Then drop every digit after the second.

A negative number rounds the same way: round its digits, then keep the negative sign. −1.718negative 1 point 7 1 8 rounds to −1.72negative 1 point 7 2.

Three rounding slips

  • Chopping instead of rounding. 5.6785 point 6 7 8 to two decimal places is 5.685 point 6 8, not 5.675 point 6 7.
  • Rounding in a chain. 3.24493 point 2 4 4 9 is 3.243 point 2 4. Rounding 3.24493 point 2 4 4 9 to 3.2453 point 2 4 5 first and then to 3.253 point 2 5 is a slip. Only the third digit after the point counts.
  • Losing a carry. 2.9962 point 9 9 6 rounds to 3.003 point 0 0. Rounding the 9 up carries into the next place, and so on.

Worked example: −23.4 ÷ 9.65

Find −23.4 ÷ 9.65negative 23 point 4 divided by 9 point 6 5 and round to two decimal places.

  1. 23.4 ÷ 9.65 = 2.42487…23 point 4 divided by 9 point 6 5 equals 2 point 4 2 4 8 7 and so onDivide the sizes first.
  2. −23.4 ÷ 9.65 = −2.42487…negative 23 point 4 divided by 9 point 6 5 equals negative 2 point 4 2 4 8 7 and so onA negative divided by a positive is negative.
  3. −2.42negative 2 point 4 2The third digit after the point is 4, so the 2 stays. Rounding in a chain (to −2.425, then to −2.43) would be a slip.

Answer: −2.42negative 2 point 4 2

4. Fraction bars and parentheses on one line

What you will be able to do

  • Work out a fraction whose top is a subtraction.
  • Type a fraction on one line with parentheses so it keeps its value.
  • Spot the one-line entry that divides too early.

A fraction bar groups

A fraction bar groups everything above it and everything below it. In 46 − 61.34.5the fraction with 46 minus 61 point 3 on top and 4 point 5 on the bottom, the whole subtraction on top happens first, and then the result is divided by the bottom.

On one line, a slash (/) means divide, and division comes before subtraction. Typed as 46 − 61.3 / 4.546 minus 61 point 3 slash 4 point 5, a calculator divides only 61.361 point 3 by 4.54 point 5. To keep the fraction's meaning, put parentheses around the top: (46 − 61.3) / 4.5open parenthesis 46 minus 61 point 3 close parenthesis, slash 4 point 5. If the bottom also has an operation, it needs its own parentheses too.

Worked example: (46 − 61.3) / 4.5

Find the value of 46 − 61.34.5the fraction with 46 minus 61 point 3 on top and 4 point 5 on the bottom.

  1. 46 − 61.3 = −15.346 minus 61 point 3 equals negative 15 point 3Work out the whole top first.
  2. −15.3 ÷ 4.5 = −3.4negative 15 point 3 divided by 4 point 5 equals negative 3 point 4Then divide by the bottom. A negative divided by a positive is negative.
  3. 46 − 61.3 / 4.5 ≈ 32.3846 minus 61 point 3 slash 4 point 5 is about 32 point 3 8Typing it on one line without parentheses would give this very different value, because only 61.3 gets divided.

Answer: −3.4negative 3 point 4

5. Comparing, ordering and reading a scale

What you will be able to do

  • Tell which of two negative numbers is less.
  • Tell which number is farther from 0, whatever its sign.
  • Put decimals in order, including decimals with different numbers of digits.
  • Read a value from a number line whose marks are not labeled one by one.

Less than, and farther from 0

On a number line, numbers get larger to the right. Every negative number is to the left of 0, so:

  • −2.4negative 2 point 4 is less than −1.9negative 1 point 9, because it is farther to the left. Bigger digits after a negative sign mean a smaller number.
  • How far a number is from 0 ignores the sign. −2.4negative 2 point 4 is 2.42 point 4 away from 0, and −1.9negative 1 point 9 is 1.91 point 9 away, so −2.4negative 2 point 4 is farther from 0.

So −2.4negative 2 point 4 is both the smaller number and the one farther from 0. "Which is less?" and "Which is farther from 0?" are different questions. For positive numbers they have the same answer. For negative numbers they can feel backward, so read which one is being asked.

Putting decimals in order

Line up the decimal points and compare place by place, starting from the left. Fill empty places with zeros: 0.70 point 7 is 0.700 point 7 0, which is more than 0.650 point 6 5. A decimal with more digits is not automatically larger.

Reading a scale

When only some marks on a number line are labeled, first find the step between labels, then count the spaces between the short marks, not the marks themselves. If the labels go up by 20 and there are 4 equal spaces between them, each space is 20 ÷ 4 = 520 divided by 4 equals 5.

Worked example: order four numbers

Put −1.4negative 1 point 4, 0.350 point 3 5, −2.05negative 2 point 0 5 and 0.40 point 4 in order from least to greatest, and say which is farthest from 0.

  1. −2.05negative 2 point 0 5, −1.4negative 1 point 4The negatives come first. −2.05 is farther left than −1.4, so it is less.
  2. 0.350 point 3 5, 0.40 point 4Write 0.40 point 4 as 0.400 point 4 0. 35 hundredths is less than 40 hundredths.
  3. Farthest from 0: −2.05negative 2 point 0 5Ignore the signs: 2.05 is the largest size.

Answer: −2.05negative 2 point 0 5, −1.4negative 1 point 4, 0.350 point 3 5, 0.40 point 4; farthest from 0 is −2.05negative 2 point 0 5
