2. Graphs that Enlighten

These checks cover Topic 1 (Graphs that Enlighten) and the Dotplot on page 1 of the Section 2.3 notes, and Classroom Example 2.3.1 and its video on page 5.

Check 1 (Dotplot). A dotplot shows the number of pets owned by each of 15 students. Which statement matches the description of a dotplot on page 1?
Show answer

Each dot stands for one student's value, and dots stack above a value that appears more than once.

Where this is: Topic 1 (Graphs that Enlighten), Dotplot, on page 1 of the notes.

Check 2 (Quantitative Data). Which data set fits a dotplot, the graph that page 1 describes for quantitative data?
Show answer

The number of minutes each of 25 commuters drives to work.

Where this is: Topic 1 (Graphs that Enlighten), Dotplot, on page 1 of the notes.

Check 3 (How a Dotplot Helps). Which statement matches "How a Dotplot Helps" on page 1?
Show answer

It shows the shape of the distribution, and in some cases it shows the original data values.

Where this is: Topic 1 (Graphs that Enlighten), Dotplot and How a Dotplot Helps, on page 1 of the notes.

Check 4 (Reading a Dotplot). The dotplot shows the number of books each of several students read over the summer. Which list of data values could it show?
Show answer

3, 5, 2, 5, 4, 3, 7, 5, 4, 3, 5

Where this is: Topic 1 (Graphs that Enlighten), Dotplot, on page 1 of the notes.

Check 5 (Classroom Example 2.3.1). A coffee shop records the sizes, in ounces, of 12 drinks: 30, 35, 35, 40, 40, 40, 45, 35, 40, 60, 45, 40. Which description matches a dotplot of these data, with the axis going up by 5?
Show answer

The tallest column is above 40, and there is a gap between 45 and 60.

Where this is: Classroom Example 2.3.1 on page 5 of the notes and its video, "Classroom Example 2.3.1."

Check 6 (Outliers in Classroom Example 2.3.1). For the 12 drink sizes in Check 5, which statement about outliers matches the Classroom Example 2.3.1 video?
Show answer

Yes, 60 appears to be an outlier, because it lies far away from the rest.

Where this is: Classroom Example 2.3.1 on page 5 of the notes and its video, "Classroom Example 2.3.1."

3. Graphs that Deceive

These checks cover Topic 2 (Graphs that Deceive), including the Nonzero Axis on page 4 and Pictographs on page 5 of the Section 2.3 notes.

Check 7 (Graphs that Deceive). Which statement matches the opening of Topic 2 on page 4?
Show answer

They distort data, by accident or on purpose, and often exaggerate or minimize differences.

Where this is: Topic 2 (Graphs that Deceive) on page 4 of the notes.

Check 8 (Nonzero Axis). A bar graph shows on-time rates of 88% and 92% for two airlines. Its vertical axis starts at 86%, so the 92% bar looks three times as tall as the 88% bar. Which statement matches the notes?
Show answer

Starting the axis at 86% makes a small difference between the airlines look much larger.

Where this is: Topic 2 (Graphs that Deceive), Nonzero Axis, on page 4 of the notes.

Check 9 (Fixing a Nonzero Axis). In the political issue example on page 4, how is the misleading graph fixed?
Show answer

The vertical axis is started at 0% instead of at 50%.

Where this is: Topic 2 (Graphs that Deceive), Nonzero Axis, on page 4 of the notes.

Check 10 (Pictographs). A pictograph uses house pictures to show average monthly rent in two towns. Town B's rent is twice Town A's, and the artist makes Town B's house twice as tall and twice as wide. Which statement matches the notes?
Show answer

The difference is exaggerated, because the data are one-dimensional but both height and width grew.

Where this is: Topic 2 (Graphs that Deceive), Pictographs, on page 5 of the notes.

Check 11 (Pictograph Comparison). A pictograph shows the average yearly pay at two jobs: $64,000 and $48,000. How many times larger is the higher pay than the lower pay? Round to two decimal places.

Show answer

1.33

Where this is: Topic 2 (Graphs that Deceive), Pictographs, on page 5 of the notes.

Check 12 (A Pictograph That Does Not Exaggerate). Which change would keep the pictograph in Check 11 from exaggerating the difference?
Show answer

Keep the widths the same and make the heights proportional to the pay.

Where this is: Topic 2 (Graphs that Deceive), Pictographs, on page 5 of the notes.

  • Pictographs: Topic 2, page 5
