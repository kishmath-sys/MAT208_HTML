2. Percents, decimals and fractions

What you will be able to do

  • Change a percent to a decimal, including percents such as 7.5%7 point 5 percent.
  • Change a decimal to a percent, including decimals with one digit after the point and decimals greater than 1.
  • Change a fraction to a percent by dividing first.
  • Compare numbers written in different forms.

Percent means "out of 100"

The word percent means "per hundred." So 37% means 37 out of 100, which is the fraction 3710037 over 100 and the decimal 0.370 point 3 7. Moving between the three forms comes down to multiplying or dividing by 100.

  • Percent to decimal: divide by 100. That moves the decimal point two places to the left. Fill any empty places with zeros.
  • Decimal to percent: multiply by 100. That moves the decimal point two places to the right, then add the percent sign.
  • Fraction to percent: divide the top by the bottom to get a decimal, then multiply by 100.

Notice the last row. A percent can be more than 100%. Any decimal greater than 1 is a percent greater than 100%.

Three slips to watch for

  • Reading the digit as the percent. 0.40 point 4 is 4 tenths, which is 40 hundredths, so it is 40%, not 4%.
  • Moving only one place. 7.5%7 point 5 percent is 0.0750 point 0 7 5, not 0.750 point 7 5. Count two places to the left and fill the empty place with a zero.
  • Adding a percent sign without converting. 0.60 point 6 is 60%. Writing 0.6%0 point 6 percent names a much smaller amount.

Worked example: Write 4.5% as a decimal

Write 4.5%4 point 5 percent as a decimal.

  1. 4.5 ÷ 1004 point 5 divided by 100Percent to decimal means divide by 100.
  2. 4.5 → 0.45 → 0.0454 point 5, then 0 point 4 5, then 0 point 0 4 5Move the decimal point one place left, then a second place left. The second move needs a zero in front of the 4.

Answer: 0.0450 point 0 4 5 (not 0.450 point 4 5, which would be 45%)

3. Finding a percent of a number

What you will be able to do

  • Find a percent of a number by changing the percent to a decimal and multiplying.
  • Write the same calculation with a fraction of 100.
  • Check that an answer is reasonable with a quick estimate.
  • Answer a two-step question that asks for the part left over.

"Of" means multiply

To find a percent of a number, change the percent to a decimal and multiply. Dividing the percent by 100 does the same job, so these two lines give the same answer:

  • 35% of 240 = 0.35 × 24035 percent of 240 equals 0 point 3 5 times 240
  • 35% of 240 = 240 × (35 / 100)35 percent of 240 equals 240 times, open parenthesis 35 over 100 close parenthesis

Quick estimate. 10% of a number is the number divided by 10, and 50% is half. If the percent is less than 100%, the answer must be smaller than the number you started with.

Three slips to watch for

  • Ignoring the percent sign. Multiplying by 35 instead of 0.350 point 3 5 gives an answer 100 times too big.
  • Dividing instead of multiplying. "35% of 240" never means 240 ÷ 35240 divided by 35.
  • Answering a different question. If the question asks for the part that is left, find the percent of the number and then subtract, or use the leftover percent.

Worked example: Find 35% of 240

Find 35% of 240.

  1. 35% = 0.3535 percent equals 0 point 3 5Change the percent to a decimal by moving the point two places left.
  2. 0.35 × 240 = 840 point 3 5 times 240 equals 84Multiply.
  3. Estimate: 10% of 240 is 24So 30% is 3 × 24 = 723 times 24 equals 72, and 35% should be a little more than 72. The answer 84 fits.

Answer: 84

4. What percent is it? Part and total

What you will be able to do

  • Pick out the part and the total in a sentence.
  • Find what percent the part is of the total: divide, then multiply by 100.
  • Spot a flipped division by checking whether the answer makes sense.
  • Handle a sentence with three or more numbers in it.

Part divided by total

To find what percent one number is of another, divide the part by the total, then multiply by 100.

percent = (part ÷ total) × 100percent equals, open parenthesis part divided by total close parenthesis, times 100

  • The total is the whole group. It often comes after words such as "of the," "out of" or "among."
  • The part is the smaller group the question asks about.
  • If the part is smaller than the total, the answer must be less than 100%. An answer over 100% usually means the division was flipped.

Worked example: Adopted dogs

An animal shelter took in 140 dogs this month, and 42 of them were adopted. What percent of the dogs were adopted?

  1. Part: 42 adopted. Total: 140 dogs.Label both numbers before dividing.
  2. 42 ÷ 140 = 0.342 divided by 140 equals 0 point 3Divide the part by the total.
  3. 0.3 × 100 = 300 point 3 times 100 equals 30Multiply by 100 to change the decimal to a percent.

Answer: 30%

5. Rounding and rounding directions

What you will be able to do

  • Round to the nearest whole number.
  • Round to one decimal place.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Follow the direction "do not round."

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. For the nearest whole number, that is the ones digit. For one decimal place, it is the tenths digit, the first digit after the point.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 12.450112 point 4 5 0 1 to the nearest whole number is 12. Rounding to 12.512 point 5 first and then to 13 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. Dropping digits without looking at the next one is not rounding. 62.4862 point 4 8 to one decimal place is 62.562 point 5, not 62.462 point 4.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 4.974 point 9 7 to one decimal place is 5.05 point 0.

Worked example: Round 213.4762 two ways

Round 213.4762213 point 4 7 6 2 to one decimal place, and then to the nearest whole number.

  1. One decimal place: keep the 4, look at the 7The 7 is 5 or more, so the 4 becomes 5: 213.5213 point 5.
  2. Nearest whole number: keep the 3, look at the 4The 4 is 4 or less, so the 3 stays: 213.

Answer: 213.5213 point 5 to one decimal place and 213 to the nearest whole number.

Directions decide the answer. "Do not round" means type every digit the calculator shows. "Round to the nearest whole number" means no digits after the point. "One decimal place" means exactly one digit after the point.

6. Order of operations on one line

What you will be able to do

  • Work inside parentheses first.
  • Do multiplication and division from left to right.
  • Treat a fraction bar as grouping its whole top and its whole bottom.
  • Choose the one-line version of a fraction that keeps its value.

The order

  1. Parentheses first.
  2. Multiplication and division next, working from left to right. Neither one automatically goes first.
  3. Addition and subtraction last, also from left to right.

On one line, a slash (/) means divide, just like ÷. A fraction bar groups everything above it and everything below it, so when a fraction is typed on one line, its top and bottom may need parentheses.

Worked example: 18 / 24 × 100

Evaluate 18 / 24 × 10018 slash 24 times 100.

  1. 18 / 24 = 0.7518 slash 24 equals 0 point 7 5Division comes first because it is on the left.
  2. 0.75 × 100 = 750 point 7 5 times 100 equals 75Then multiply.
  3. 18 / (24 × 100) = 0.007518 slash, open parenthesis 24 times 100 close parenthesis, equals 0 point 0 0 7 5Multiplying first would give this very different value. That is why the order matters.

Answer: 75
