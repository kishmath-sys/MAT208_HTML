2. Central Limit Theorem

These checks cover item 1 (Central Limit Theorem) on page 1 of the Section 6.4 notes, including its Notation and Requirements, and the video "Classroom Example 6.4.1" on page 4.

Check 1 (Central Limit Theorem). The amounts customers spend at a gift shop are strongly skewed to the right, not normal. Many samples of customers are taken, and the mean amount spent is found for each sample. According to item 1, what happens as the sample size increases?
Show answer

The distribution of the sample means approaches a normal distribution.

Where this is: Item 1 (Central Limit Theorem) on page 1 of the notes.

Check 2 (Mean of the sample means). A population has mean μ = 68mu equals 68 and standard deviation σ = 10sigma equals 10. Samples of size n = 25n equals 25 are taken. What is μx̄mu sub x bar, the mean of the sampling distribution of sample means?
Show answer

68, the same as the population mean

Where this is: The Notation part of item 1 (Central Limit Theorem) on page 1 of the notes.

Check 3 (Standard deviation of the sample means). A population has standard deviation σ = 12sigma equals 12. For samples of size n = 36n equals 36, find σx̄sigma sub x bar, the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of sample means. Do not round.

Show answer

2

Where this is: The Notation part of item 1 (Central Limit Theorem) on page 1 of the notes.

Check 4 (Requirements, large sample). The shape of the population of a city's monthly household water bills is unknown. A random sample of 45 bills is taken. Based on the Requirements on page 1, can the Central Limit Theorem be applied to the sample mean?
Show answer

Yes, because the sample size is sufficiently large, n > 30n greater than 30.

Where this is: The Requirements to Use the Central Limit Theorem in item 1 on page 1 of the notes.

Check 5 (Requirements, normal population). Heights of a certain plant are normally distributed. A sample of 6 plants is taken. Why can a normal distribution be used for the sample mean even though n = 6n equals 6 is not greater than 30?
Show answer

Because the original population is normally distributed, so the sample size does not need to be greater than 30.

Where this is: The Requirements to Use the Central Limit Theorem in item 1 on page 1 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 6.4.1" on page 4.
