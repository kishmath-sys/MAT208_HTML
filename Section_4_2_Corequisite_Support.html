2. Reading a two-way table and a deck of cards

What you will be able to do

  • Find the count in one cell of a two-way table.
  • Add across a row and down a column to get row and column totals.
  • Find the grand total of every cell.
  • Know how a standard 52-card deck is made up.

Rows, columns and totals

A two-way table sorts one group of things two ways at once. Each row is one category, each column is another, and each inside number (a cell) counts the things that fit both its row and its column.

  • Row total: add every cell across one row.
  • Column total: add every cell down one column.
  • Grand total: add every inside cell. Adding all the row totals gives the same number, and so does adding all the column totals.

Three slips to watch for

  • Reading one cell as a total. A single cell counts only the things in that row and that column.
  • Using only one row for the grand total. The grand total needs every row.
  • Adding a total in with the cells. If a table already shows a total, do not add it to the cells again.

Worked example: Phone repairs at three shops

Phone repairs at three shops

3. Adding, subtracting and simplifying fractions

What you will be able to do

  • Add and subtract fractions that have the same bottom number.
  • Simplify a fraction by dividing its top and bottom by the same number.
  • Subtract a fraction from 1 by writing 1 as a fraction.
  • Change a fraction to a decimal by dividing.

Same bottom: work with the tops

When fractions have the same bottom number (denominator), add or subtract the top numbers (numerators) and keep the bottom the same. Then simplify: divide the top and the bottom by the same number until no number larger than 1 divides both.

To subtract a fraction from 1, write 1 as a fraction with the same bottom: 666 over 6, 999 over 9 and 525252 over 52 all equal 1.

Three slips to watch for

  • Adding the bottoms too. 3/10 + 4/103 over 10 plus 4 over 10 is 7107 over 10, not 7207 over 20. The pieces are still tenths.
  • Dividing only the top. To simplify, divide the top and the bottom by the same number.
  • Treating 1 as one piece. In 1 − 2/91 minus 2 over 9, the 1 is 999 over 9, not 191 over 9.

Worked example: 9/40 + 15/40 − 4/40

Work out 9/40 + 15/40 − 4/409 over 40 plus 15 over 40 minus 4 over 40 and simplify.

  1. 9 + 15 − 4 = 209 plus 15 minus 4 equals 20The bottoms are all 40, so work with the tops.
  2. 204020 over 40Keep the bottom of 40.
  3. 20 ÷ 20 = 1 and 40 ÷ 20 = 220 divided by 20 equals 1, and 40 divided by 20 equals 2Divide the top and bottom by 20, the largest number that divides both.

Answer: 121 over 2, which is 0.50 point 5

4. Percents, decimals and subtracting from 1

What you will be able to do

  • Change a percent to a decimal, including percents such as 11.9%11 point 9 percent.
  • Find a percent of a number by multiplying.
  • Subtract a decimal from 1 by lining up the decimal points.

Percent to decimal, and percent of a number

Percent means "out of 100." To change a percent to a decimal, divide by 100, which moves the decimal point two places to the left. Fill any empty places with zeros. To find a percent of a number, change the percent to a decimal and multiply.

Subtracting a decimal from 1

Write 1 with as many zeros after the decimal point as the other number has decimal places, then subtract place by place. For example, 1 − 0.361 minus 0 point 3 6 becomes 1.00 − 0.36 = 0.641 point 0 0 minus 0 point 3 6 equals 0 point 6 4. A quick check: the two numbers should add back to 1.

Three slips to watch for

  • Moving the point only one place. 6.4%6 point 4 percent is 0.0640 point 0 6 4, not 0.640 point 6 4.
  • Lining up the last digits instead of the decimal points. In 1 − 0.081 minus 0 point 0 8, the 8 is in the hundredths place. The answer is 0.920 point 9 2, not 0.20 point 2.
  • Adding instead of subtracting. Taking something away from 1 always leaves less than 1.

Worked example: 1 − 0.0473

Work out 1 − 0.04731 minus 0 point 0 4 7 3.

  1. 1 = 1.00001 equals 1 point 0 0 0 0The other number has four decimal places, so write 1 with four zeros.
  2. 1.0000 − 0.0473 = 0.95271 point 0 0 0 0 minus 0 point 0 4 7 3 equals 0 point 9 5 2 7Subtract place by place, borrowing as needed.
  3. 0.9527 + 0.0473 = 10 point 9 5 2 7 plus 0 point 0 4 7 3 equals 1Check: the two numbers add back to 1.

Answer: 0.95270 point 9 5 2 7

5. Multiplying fractions, decimals and powers

What you will be able to do

  • Multiply fractions: top times top, bottom times bottom.
  • Multiply decimals and place the decimal point correctly.
  • Expect a smaller answer when both numbers are less than 1.
  • Work out a power as repeated multiplication, and type it on one line with parentheses.

Fractions, decimals and powers

  • Fractions: multiply the tops, multiply the bottoms, then simplify. No common bottom is needed.
  • Decimals: multiply as if there were no decimal points, then count the decimal places in both numbers. The answer has that many decimal places.
  • Powers: an exponent says how many times to use the number as a factor. 0.93 = 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.7290 point 9 to the third power equals 0 point 9 times 0 point 9 times 0 point 9, which equals 0 point 7 2 9.
  • Numbers less than 1: multiplying two numbers that are both less than 1 gives an answer smaller than either one.

On one line, many calculators use * for times and ^ for a power. Put a fraction in parentheses before raising it to a power, or the power applies only to the bottom.

Three slips to watch for

  • Miscounting decimal places. 0.3 × 0.20 point 3 times 0 point 2 is 0.060 point 0 6, not 0.60 point 6. There are two decimal places in all.
  • Multiplying by the exponent. 0.930 point 9 to the third power is not 0.9 × 30 point 9 times 3.
  • Adding instead of multiplying. 3/8 × 2/53 over 8 times 2 over 5 is not 5135 over 13.

Worked example: 3/8 × 2/5

Work out 3/8 × 2/53 over 8 times 2 over 5 as a fraction and as a decimal.

  1. 3 × 2 = 6 and 8 × 5 = 403 times 2 equals 6, and 8 times 5 equals 40Top times top, bottom times bottom.
  2. 6/40 = 3/206 over 40 equals 3 over 20Simplify by dividing the top and bottom by 2.
  3. 3 ÷ 20 = 0.153 divided by 20 equals 0 point 1 5Divide to get the decimal. It is smaller than both 0.3750 point 3 7 5 and 0.40 point 4, as expected.

Answer: 3203 over 20, which is 0.150 point 1 5

6. Rounding to three, four and five decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Count decimal places, including zeros right after the decimal point.
  • Round to three, four or five decimal places.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Round once, from the original number.

How to round to a number of decimal places

  1. Count that many digits to the right of the decimal point, starting with the first digit after the point, even if it is a zero. That digit is the last one you keep.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Three slips to watch for

  • Starting the count at the first nonzero digit. In 0.04158620 point 0 4 1 5 8 6 2, three decimal places ends at the 1, not the 5: 0.0420 point 0 4 2.
  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 0.07449620 point 0 7 4 4 9 6 2 to three decimal places is 0.0740 point 0 7 4. Rounding to 0.07450 point 0 7 4 5 first and then to 0.0750 point 0 7 5 is a slip.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left.

Worked example: Round 0.0083917 to five decimal places

Round 0.00839170 point 0 0 8 3 9 1 7 to five decimal places.

  1. Places 1 to 5: 0, 0, 8, 3, 9Count from the first digit after the point, zeros included. The fifth digit is the 9.
  2. Next digit: 1The 1 is 4 or less, so the 9 stays.
  3. 0.008390 point 0 0 8 3 9Drop everything after the fifth place.

Answer: 0.008390 point 0 0 8 3 9

Directions decide the answer. "Round to four decimal places" means exactly four digits after the point. "Do not round" means type every digit the calculator shows.
