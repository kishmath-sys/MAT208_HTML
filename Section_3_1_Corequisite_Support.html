2. Comparing and ordering decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Compare two decimals by lining up the decimal points.
  • Find the smallest and the largest number in a list.
  • Put a list of decimals in order from smallest to largest.
  • Avoid the "more digits means bigger" slip.

Line up the decimal points

To compare decimals, line up the decimal points and compare place by place from the left: the whole-number part first, then the tenths, then the hundredths. If one number has fewer digits after the point, write zeros on the end so both have the same number of digits. Zeros on the end do not change the value, so 4.74 point 7 and 4.704 point 7 0 are equal.

Three slips to watch for

  • More digits looks bigger. 4.084 point 0 8 can look larger than 4.74 point 7 because 8 is bigger than 7. Write 4.74 point 7 as 4.704 point 7 0: 70 hundredths is more than 8 hundredths, so 4.74 point 7 is larger.
  • Reading the part after the point as a whole number. 3.653 point 6 5 is not larger than 3.73 point 7 just because 65 is bigger than 7. Compare 3.653 point 6 5 with 3.703 point 7 0 instead.
  • Looking only at the first digit. 98.698 point 6 starts with a 9 and 104.2104 point 2 starts with a 1, but 98.698 point 6 is smaller. Compare the whole-number parts, 98 and 104, first. A spreadsheet that sorts numbers as text makes this same slip.

Worked example: Put four decimals in order

Put 62.562 point 5, 6.256 point 2 5, 62.0562 point 0 5 and 60.960 point 9 in order from smallest to largest.

  1. Whole-number parts: 62, 6, 62 and 60The 6 is smallest, so 6.256 point 2 5 comes first. Then 60, so 60.960 point 9 comes next.
  2. Two numbers start with 62: compare 62.5062 point 5 0 and 62.0562 point 0 5Write 62.562 point 5 with two digits after the point. The tenths digits are 5 and 0, so 62.0562 point 0 5 is smaller.
  3. Smallest to largestCheck that each number is bigger than the one before it.

Answer: 6.256 point 2 5, 60.960 point 9, 62.0562 point 0 5, 62.562 point 5

3. Fractions on one line and decimal arithmetic

What you will be able to do

  • Add and subtract decimals by lining up the decimal points.
  • Work out a fraction by finishing its top and its bottom before dividing.
  • Type a fraction on one line with parentheses around its top, and around its bottom when needed.
  • Spot the value you get when the parentheses are left out.

Adding and subtracting decimals

Line up the decimal points, fill any empty places with zeros, and then add or subtract as you would with whole numbers. The point in the answer lines up with the points above it.

A fraction bar groups its whole top and its whole bottom

In a fraction, you finish everything above the bar and everything below the bar first. Then you divide the top by the bottom. When a fraction is typed on one line, the bar becomes a slash (/), and a slash does not group anything. A calculator follows the order of operations: parentheses first, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction. So the top needs parentheses whenever it has more than one number, and so does the bottom.

36.4 + 12.84the fraction with 36 point 4 plus 12 point 8 on top and 4 on the bottom is typed as (36.4 + 12.8) / 4open parenthesis 36 point 4 plus 12 point 8 close parenthesis, slash 4

Three slips to watch for

  • Dividing only the last number. 36.4 + 12.8 / 436 point 4 plus 12 point 8 slash 4 divides only the 12.812 point 8 by 4, because division comes before addition.
  • Adding the parts after the point as whole numbers. 45.6 + 7.2545 point 6 plus 7 point 2 5 is not 52.3152 point 3 1. Write 45.60 + 7.2545 point 6 0 plus 7 point 2 5, then add: 52.8552 point 8 5.
  • Grouping only the top. When the bottom has two numbers, such as 3 × 43 times 4, it needs its own parentheses too.

Worked example: A fraction on one line

Work out 36.4 + 12.84the fraction with 36 point 4 plus 12 point 8 on top and 4 on the bottom, then compare it with the value you get without parentheses.

  1. 36.4 + 12.8 = 49.236 point 4 plus 12 point 8 equals 49 point 2Finish the top first. Line up the points: 36.4 and 12.8 add to 49.2.
  2. 49.2 / 4 = 12.349 point 2 slash 4 equals 12 point 3Then divide by the bottom. Typed on one line, this is (36.4 + 12.8) / 4open parenthesis 36 point 4 plus 12 point 8 close parenthesis, slash 4.
  3. 36.4 + 12.8 / 4 = 36.4 + 3.2 = 39.636 point 4 plus 12 point 8 slash 4 equals 36 point 4 plus 3 point 2, which equals 39 point 6Without parentheses, only 12.8 is divided, and the value is very different.

Answer: 12.312 point 3

4. Rounding to two decimal places and to the nearest hundred

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two decimal places.
  • Follow "if necessary": leave a number alone when it already has two or fewer decimal places.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Round a whole number to the nearest hundred.

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. For two decimal places, that is the hundredths digit, the second digit after the point. For the nearest hundred, it is the hundreds digit.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it, and only that digit.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. After the decimal point, drop the digits past the kept digit. Before the decimal point, the places past the kept digit become zeros, so the number keeps its size.
"If necessary." A direction such as "Round all results to two decimal places, if necessary" means round only when a result has more than two decimal places. A result of 83.683 point 6 already has fewer, so it stays 83.683 point 6. Typing 83.6083 point 6 0 is the same value and is also fine.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. 7.24497 point 2 4 4 9 to two decimal places is 7.247 point 2 4. Rounding to 7.2457 point 2 4 5 first and then to 7.257 point 2 5 is a slip.
  • Chopping instead of rounding. 6.47816 point 4 7 8 1 to two decimal places is 6.486 point 4 8, not 6.476 point 4 7. And 3,467 to the nearest hundred is 3,500, not 3,400.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 12.99612 point 9 9 6 to two decimal places is 13.0013 point 0 0, and 3,962 to the nearest hundred is 4,000.

Worked example: Round two numbers

Round 52.3684952 point 3 6 8 4 9 to two decimal places. Then round 2,847 grams to the nearest hundred.

  1. Two decimal places: keep the 6, look at the 8The 8 is 5 or more, so the 6 becomes 7: 52.3752 point 3 7.
  2. Nearest hundred: keep the 8 in 2,847, look at the 4The 4 is 4 or less, so the 8 stays, and the tens and ones become zeros: 2,800.

Answer: 52.3752 point 3 7, and 2,800 grams

5. Reading lists and tables of data

What you will be able to do

  • Count the values in a table or in a list with rows of different lengths.
  • Find one value in a table by its row and column.
  • Find the largest or smallest value in one column.
  • Compare two amounts by subtracting, and say which is larger, with units.

Read before you compute

  • Read the title or caption first. It tells you what the values measure and their units, such as pounds, kilograms, dollars or grams.
  • When every row of a table is full, count the values by multiplying the number of rows by the number of columns. When the rows have different lengths, count each row and add.
  • To find one value, go across from its row label and down from its column label to the cell where they meet.
  • To compare two amounts, subtract the smaller from the larger, keep the units, and say in words which one is larger.

Three slips to watch for

  • Adding instead of multiplying. A table with 3 rows and 4 columns holds 12 values, not 7.
  • Assuming every row is full. The last row of a list is often shorter. Count it on its own.
  • Reading the cell next door. Check both the row label and the column label before you copy a value.

Worked example: Books checked out

Books checked out at three library branches
BranchMondayTuesdayWednesdayThursday
North11298105120
South8793110101
East64726980

How many values are in the table? How many books did the South branch check out on Wednesday, and how many more is that than on Tuesday?

  1. 3 × 4 = 123 times 4 equals 123 rows (branches) and 4 columns (days), and every row is full. The row labels and the column labels are not values.
  2. South row, Wednesday column: 110Go across from "South" and down from "Wednesday."
  3. 110 − 93 = 17110 minus 93 equals 17South checked out 93 on Tuesday. Wednesday is larger by 17 books.

Answer: 12 values; 110 books on Wednesday, 17 more than on Tuesday
