2. Introduction

These checks cover the Rare Event Rule and the Examples to Consider on page 1 of the Section 8.1 notes.

Check 1 (Rare Event Rule). A game company says its spinner lands on each of its 4 colors equally often. In 40 spins, it lands on red every time. Which statement matches the Rare Event Rule?
Show answer

This result is very unlikely if the statement is true, so the statement is likely not correct.

Where this is: The Rare Event Rule (Key Reminder) on page 1 of the notes.

Check 2 (Examples to Consider). A study course is said to raise scores on a reading test where the typical score is 70. In Group A, the sample mean is 94. In Group B, the sample mean is 71. Which statement matches the Examples to Consider?
Show answer

Group A shows an obvious increase, while Group B is uncertain and needs further testing.

Where this is: The Examples to Consider on page 1 of the notes.

3. Topic 1: Hypotheses and the Claim

These checks cover Topic 1 on page 1, Examples 1 to 3 on page 2, and the Classroom Example 8.1.1, 8.1.2 and 8.1.3 videos on page 6.

Check 3 (Topic 1 Symbols). Which statement matches the symbols listed in Topic 1?
Show answer

The null hypothesis uses =equals, and the alternative hypothesis uses >greater than, ≠not equal or <less than.

Where this is: Topic 1 on page 1 of the notes.

Check 4 (Claim of Equality). A claim is that the mean time to deliver a pizza in one town is equal to 28 minutes. Which pair of hypotheses matches the notes?
Show answer

H0: μ = 28 (claim)H sub 0: mu equals 28, claim; H1: μ ≠ 28H sub 1: mu is not equal to 28

Where this is: Topic 1 on page 1 and Examples 1 and 2 on page 2 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 8.1.1" on page 6.

Check 5 (Claim About a Percentage). A claim is that more than 35% of adults in a city own an electric bike. Which pair of hypotheses matches the notes?
Show answer

H0: p = 0.35H sub 0: p equals 0 point 3 5; H1: p > 0.35 (claim)H sub 1: p is greater than 0 point 3 5, claim

Where this is: Examples 1 and 2 on page 2 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 8.1.3" on page 6.

Check 6 (Claim in Symbolic Form). Claim: the mean resting heart rate of adult runners is less than 62 beats per minute. Sample data: for 40 runners, the mean is 59.459 point 4 beats per minute and the standard deviation is 7.17 point 1. Which is the claim in symbolic form?
Show answer

μ < 62mu is less than 62

Where this is: Topic 1 on page 1 and Example 3 on page 2 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 8.1.2" on page 6.

Check 7 (Standard Deviation Claim and Tails). A claim is that the standard deviation of the fill weights of cereal boxes is less than 0.40 point 4 ounces. Which statement matches Example 3?
Show answer

The claim is H1: σ < 0.4H sub 1: sigma is less than 0 point 4, and this is a left-tail (one-tail) test.

Where this is: Examples 1 and 3 on page 2 of the notes.

αalpha is the probability of rejecting H0H sub 0 when it is true, and smaller values suit serious consequences.

5. Topics 5 and 6: Decisions and Conclusions

These checks cover Topics 5 and 6 on page 4 and the Classroom Example 8.1.1 and 8.1.3 videos on page 6.

Check 12 (Making the Decision). A test uses α = 0.01alpha equals 0 point 0 1, and StatCrunch gives a p-value of 0.03400 point 0 3 4 0. Which decision matches Topic 5?
Show answer

Do not reject H0H sub 0, because 0.03400 point 0 3 4 0 is greater than 0.010 point 0 1.

Where this is: Topic 5 on page 4 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 8.1.3" on page 6.

Check 13 (Conclusion When the Claim Is the Alternative). Original claim: more than 30% of shoppers use self-checkout. With α = 0.05alpha equals 0 point 0 5, the test gives a p-value of 0.00210 point 0 0 2 1. Which final conclusion matches the Topic 6 table?

6. Topics 7 and 8: Errors and Confidence Intervals

These checks cover Topics 7 and 8 on page 5 of the Section 8.1 notes.

Check 16 (Errors). A company tests whether a new training program raises sales. The test concludes that the program works, but in reality it does not. Which statement matches Topic 7?
Show answer

This is a Type I error (αalpha): rejecting H0H sub 0 when it is true.

Where this is: Topic 7 on page 5 of the notes.

Check 17 (Confidence Interval Rule). A confidence interval for a population mean runs from 31.231 point 2 to 38.638 point 6 minutes. The claimed value is 40 minutes. Which decision matches Topic 8?
Show answer

Reject H0H sub 0, because 40 is outside the interval.

Where this is: Topic 8 on page 5 of the notes.
