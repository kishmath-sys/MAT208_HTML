2. Whole numbers, decimals and fractions

What you will be able to do

  • Tell whether a value is a whole number.
  • Change a fraction or a mixed number to a decimal.
  • Name the two whole numbers a decimal sits between.
  • Find a number between two decimals.

Whole numbers and the numbers between them

Whole numbers are numbers such as 4, 17 and 250, with no fraction or decimal part. A decimal that ends in zeros, such as 9.09 point 0, is the whole number 9. A fraction can also be a whole number: 18318 thirds is 6 because 18 ÷ 3 = 618 divided by 3 equals 6.

Most decimals sit between two whole numbers. 6.46 point 4 is more than 6 and less than 7, so it sits between 6 and 7.

There is always room for more numbers between two decimals. To find one between 2.42 point 4 and 2.52 point 5, add a zero to each: 2.402 point 4 0 and 2.502 point 5 0. Now 2.412 point 4 1, 2.452 point 4 5 and 2.492 point 4 9 all fit between them.

Fractions to decimals

A fraction bar means divide: the top divided by the bottom. Keep dividing past the decimal point instead of stopping at a remainder. A remainder is not a decimal: 17 divided by 4 is 4 with 1 left over, and that 1 left over is 141 fourth of a whole, or 0.250 point 2 5, not 0.10 point 1.

Worked example: Change 23/4 to a decimal

Write 23423 fourths as a decimal, and name the two whole numbers it sits between.

  1. 23 ÷ 4 = 5 with 3 left over23 divided by 4 equals 5 with 3 left overDivide the top by the bottom. 4 goes into 23 five times, with 3 left over.
  2. 3 ÷ 4 = 0.753 divided by 4 equals 0 point 7 5Keep dividing: the 3 left over is 3 fourths of a whole, which is 0.750 point 7 5.
  3. 5 + 0.75 = 5.755 plus 0 point 7 5 equals 5 point 7 5Put the whole part and the decimal part together.

Answer: 5.755 point 7 5, which sits between the whole numbers 5 and 6. It is not a whole number.

3. Differences on a scale with negative numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Tell which of two numbers below zero is lower.
  • Find the difference between two values on the same side of zero.
  • Find the difference between a value below zero and a value above zero.
  • Find how many years apart two years are, and keep the unit on the answer.

Below zero

On a thermometer, values below zero have a minus sign. The farther below zero a value is, the lower it is. So -20°Cnegative 20 degrees Celsius is colder than -3°Cnegative 3 degrees Celsius, even though 20 looks bigger than 3.

How far apart are two values?

  • Both on the same side of zero: subtract the smaller size from the larger size. From 47°F47 degrees Fahrenheit to 85°F85 degrees Fahrenheit is 38 degrees. From -4°Fnegative 4 degrees Fahrenheit down to -19°Fnegative 19 degrees Fahrenheit is 15 degrees.
  • One above zero and one below: find each one's distance to zero, then add the two distances.
  • Years: subtract the earlier year from the later year. From 2010 to 2025 is 15 years.
  • Keep the unit. The difference between two Fahrenheit temperatures is in degrees Fahrenheit, and the difference between two years is in years.

Worked example: Difference across zero

One day the high was 12°C12 degrees Celsius and the low was -7°Cnegative 7 degrees Celsius. How many degrees apart are the high and the low?

  1. From 12 down to 0: 12 degreesFirst find the distance from the high down to zero.
  2. From 0 down to -7negative 7: 7 degreesThen find the distance from zero down to the low.
  3. 12 + 7 = 1912 plus 7 equals 19The two values are on opposite sides of zero, so add the two distances.

Answer: 19 degrees Celsius. (Subtracting 12 − 712 minus 7 gives 5, which is wrong because it treats the low as if it were above zero.)

4. Comparing amounts: times as many, half of, every nth

What you will be able to do

  • Tell a "how many times as many" question from a "how many more" question.
  • Find half of an amount and twice an amount.
  • Find how many are picked when every nth one is chosen.
  • Find how many are left out of a total.

Two ways to compare

  • "How many more" or "how much longer" compares by subtracting. The answer keeps the unit: 24 miles longer.
  • "How many times as many," "twice" or "half of" compares by dividing or multiplying. "Twice" means 2 times, and "half of" means divide by 2. A "times as" answer has no unit: 3 times as long.
  • Every nth one. If every 5th person in a line of 40 gets a ticket, the 5th, 10th, 15th and so on get one. That is 40 ÷ 5 = 840 divided by 5 equals 8 tickets.
  • Left out. If 35 of 40 people get a ticket, the number without one is 40 − 35 = 540 minus 35 equals 5.

Worked example: Compare two bike rides two ways

Dana rode 36 miles on Saturday and 12 miles on Sunday. How many times as long was Saturday's ride? How many miles longer was it?

  1. 36 ÷ 12 = 336 divided by 12 equals 3"Times as long" compares by dividing: the larger amount divided by the smaller amount.
  2. 36 − 12 = 2436 minus 12 equals 24"How many miles longer" compares by subtracting, and the answer keeps the unit, miles.

Answer: Saturday's ride was 3 times as long, and 24 miles longer.

5. Units with every number

What you will be able to do

  • Name the unit that goes with each number in a sentence.
  • Line up decimal points to add or subtract measurements.
  • Keep the unit on a sum or a difference.
  • Write an answer with its unit.

Find the unit for each number

A unit tells what a number is measured or counted in: inches, centimeters, miles, pounds, grams, degrees, dollars, years, hours. The unit usually comes right after the number. A number with no unit leaves the reader guessing what it means.

Adding and subtracting measurements

  • Line up the decimal points, not the last digits. Write 1.61 point 6 as 1.601 point 6 0 so both have two decimal places.
  • When you add or subtract values in the same unit, the answer is in that unit too. 9 miles + 3.5 miles = 12.5 miles9 miles plus 3 point 5 miles equals 12 point 5 miles.

Worked example: Label, line up and keep the unit

"A hiker carried a 22.522 point 5 pound pack. She walked 7.257 point 2 5 miles before lunch and 4.84 point 8 miles after lunch." How far did she walk in all?

  1. 22.522 point 5 pounds, 7.257 point 2 5 miles, 4.84 point 8 milesLabel each number. The pack weight is in pounds and does not belong in a distance.
  2. 7.25 + 4.80 = 12.057 point 2 5 plus 4 point 8 0 equals 12 point 0 5Add the two distances with the decimal points lined up.
  3. 12.0512 point 0 5 milesBoth distances are in miles, so the total is in miles.

Answer: 12.0512 point 0 5 miles
