2. Concepts

These checks cover items 1 to 3 on page 1 and Classroom Example 1.3.4 on page 4 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 1 (Observational Study). A city planner stands at a busy corner and counts how many cyclists wear helmets. She does not stop or speak to anyone. Which type of study is this?
Show answer

An observational study

Where this is: Items 1 and 2 (Observational Study, Experiment) on page 1 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 2 (Experiment). A gym has 40 members follow a new stretching routine for 8 weeks and then measures their flexibility. Which statement matches item 2 and its Note?
Show answer

This is an experiment, and the 40 members are its subjects.

Where this is: Item 2 (Experiment), including its Note, on page 1 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 3 (Placebo). In a study of a new cold remedy, 50 participants get the remedy and another 50 get a look-alike tablet with no active ingredient. Which statement matches item 3?
Show answer

The look-alike tablet is a placebo. It helps determine whether the remedy works better than no treatment at all.

Where this is: Item 3 (Placebo) on page 1 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 4 (Classroom Example 1.3.4, study type). In Classroom Example 1.3.4, 1465 Internet users chose to respond to a question posted on the USA Today electronic edition: "Is news online as satisfying as print and TV news?" Is this an experiment or an observational study?
Show answer

An observational study

Where this is: Classroom Example 1.3.4 on page 4 of the Section 1.3 notes, the video "Classroom Example 1.3.4", and items 1 and 2 (Observational Study, Experiment) on page 1.

Check 5 (Classroom Example 1.3.4, major problem). What is a major problem with the USA Today study in Classroom Example 1.3.4?
Show answer

Only people who chose to respond are included (a voluntary response sample), so there is a strong potential for bias and the results may not represent all USA Today readers.

Where this is: Classroom Example 1.3.4 on page 4 of the Section 1.3 notes and the video "Classroom Example 1.3.4".

3. Design of Experiments

These checks cover items 4 to 6 on pages 1 and 2 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 6 (Replication). A company wants to test a new sunscreen. Plan A tests it on 2 volunteers. Plan B tests it on 800 volunteers. Which statement matches item 4?
Show answer

Plan B uses replication. With a large enough sample, the effects can be seen consistently across many people, and one unusual reaction is less likely to skew the conclusions.

Where this is: Item 4 (Replication) on page 1 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 7 (Blinding). In a study of a new allergy spray, the participants do not know whether their spray is real or a placebo. The researcher who records their symptoms does know. Which description fits?
Show answer

Blinding, but not a double-blind study

Where this is: Item 5 (Blinding), including Double-Blind Study, on page 2 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 8 (Placebo Effect). In a sleep study, a participant who received the placebo reports sleeping much better, simply because they believe they got the real pill. What is this called, and what helps reduce it?
Show answer

The placebo effect. Blinding helps reduce it.

Where this is: Item 5 (Blinding), including the Note on the placebo effect, on page 2 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 9 (Double-Blind Study). Which description is a double-blind study?
Show answer

Neither the participants nor the researcher knows who is receiving the treatment or the placebo.

Where this is: Item 5 (Blinding), including Double-Blind Study, on page 2 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 10 (Randomness). A researcher lets 80 volunteers choose for themselves whether to join the new-diet group or the placebo group. Which statement matches item 6?
Show answer

The assignment is not random, so the study is open to selection bias.

Where this is: Item 6 (Randomness) on page 2 of the Section 1.3 notes.

If any of these felt shaky

  • Replication: item 4, page 1
  • Blinding, the placebo effect and Double-Blind Study: item 5, page 2
  • Randomness: item 6, page 2

4. Sampling Methods

These checks cover items 7 to 12 on pages 2 and 3, Classroom Example 1.3.1 on page 3, and Classroom Examples 1.3.2 and 1.3.3 on page 4 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 11 (Simple Random Sample). A library wants to survey 200 of its 6,000 cardholders. A computer randomly generates 200 card numbers from the full list of cardholders. Which type of sampling is this?
Show answer

Simple random sample

Where this is: Item 7 (Simple Random Sample) on page 2 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 12 (Systematic Sampling). An inspector picks a random starting point among the first 50 phone cases on an assembly line, then checks every 50th case after that. Which type of sampling is this?
Show answer

Systematic sampling

Where this is: Item 8 (Systematic Sampling) on page 2 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 13 (Convenience Sampling). A reporter wants to know how a city's residents feel about a new bus route. She interviews shoppers at the mall next to her office because they are easy to reach. Which type of sampling is this?
Show answer

Convenience sampling

Where this is: Item 9 (Convenience Sampling) on page 2 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 14 (Classroom Example 1.3.1). In Classroom Example 1.3.1, a woman was audited, and the tax department said it was because her name is among the first 500 names on the taxpayer list. Which type of sampling fits best, as in the video?
Show answer

Convenience sampling

Where this is: Classroom Example 1.3.1 on page 3 of the Section 1.3 notes, the video "Classroom Example 1.3.1", and item 9 (Convenience Sampling) on page 2.

Check 15 (Stratified Sampling). A college divides its students into full-time and part-time students, then randomly selects 60 students from each of those two groups. Which type of sampling is this?
Show answer

Stratified sampling

Where this is: Item 10 (Stratified Sampling) on page 3 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 16 (Classroom Example 1.3.3). In Classroom Example 1.3.3, Keri divides her day into morning, afternoon and evening, then measures her heart rate at 3 randomly selected times during each part of the day. Which type of sampling is used?
Show answer

Stratified

Where this is: Classroom Example 1.3.3 on page 4 of the Section 1.3 notes, the video "Classroom Example 1.3.3", and item 10 (Stratified Sampling) on page 3.

Check 17 (Classroom Example 1.3.2). In Classroom Example 1.3.2, a market researcher has randomly selected four neighborhoods in a certain region and is surveying all the people from each neighborhood. Which type of sampling is used?
Show answer

Cluster

Where this is: Classroom Example 1.3.2 on page 4 of the Section 1.3 notes, the video "Classroom Example 1.3.2", and item 11 (Cluster Sampling) on page 3.

Check 18 (Stratified vs. Cluster). Which statement matches the Note in item 11 about the key difference between stratified and cluster sampling?
Show answer

Stratified: take a sample from each group. Cluster: randomly select entire groups and use every member of those groups.

Where this is: Item 11 (Cluster Sampling), including its Note, on page 3 of the Section 1.3 notes.

Check 19 (Multistage Sample Design). To study nurses in Illinois, researchers first randomly select 5 hospitals. Then they take a simple random sample of 40 nurses from the nurses at those 5 hospitals. Which design is this?
Show answer

A multistage sample design

Where this is: Item 12 (Multistage Sample Design) on page 3 of the Section 1.3 notes.
