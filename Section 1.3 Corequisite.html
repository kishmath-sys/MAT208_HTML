2. Large numbers, counts and positions

What you will be able to do

  • Read large numbers that contain commas.
  • Write a number given in words with digits.
  • Tell a count (75 callers) apart from a position (the 75th caller).
  • Count the numbers in a stretch that includes both ends.

Commas in large numbers

A comma splits the digits of a whole number into groups of three, counting from the right. It makes the number easier to read and does not change its value, so 20,416 and 20416 are the same number. A comma is not a decimal point.

A count or a position?

"300 customers" is a count: it tells how many. "The 300th customer" is a position: it tells where one customer falls in line. Endings such as -st, -nd, -rd and -th (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 300th) mark a position. "The first 300 customers" means the customers in positions 1 through 300.

Counting a stretch that includes both ends

To count the whole numbers from one number through another, subtract and then add 1. Subtracting counts the steps between the two ends, and the extra 1 puts the starting number back in. Check with a small case: 1 through 3 is three numbers, and 3 − 1 + 1 = 33 minus 1 plus 1 equals 3.

Worked example: Count the tickets

A raffle sells tickets numbered 1,201 through 1,250. How many tickets is that?

  1. 1,250 − 1,201 = 491,250 minus 1,201 equals 49Subtract the first number from the last. This counts the steps between them.
  2. 49 + 1 = 5049 plus 1 equals 50Add 1 so ticket 1,201 is counted too.

Answer: 50 tickets.

3. Equal groups, totals and what is left

What you will be able to do

  • Find a total when there are equal groups.
  • Split a total into equal groups.
  • Find what is left after part of a group is taken out.
  • Decide whether an answer about people or vehicles should be rounded up.

Which operation?

  • Equal groups to a total: multiply. 6 boxes with 12 eggs in each box hold 6 × 12 = 726 times 12 equals 72 eggs.
  • A total into equal groups: divide. 72 eggs packed equally into 6 boxes is 72 ÷ 6 = 1272 divided by 6 equals 12 eggs in each box.
  • What is left: subtract. If 72 eggs arrive and 20 are used, 72 − 20 = 5272 minus 20 equals 52 are left. Words such as "the remaining," "the rest" and "left over" point to subtraction.
  • Another group of the same size: add. "12 eggs, and another 12" is 12 + 12 = 2412 plus 12 equals 24.

Check a division by multiplying back: if 72 split into 6 groups gives 12, then 6 × 126 times 12 should be 72.

Does the answer make sense?

A count of people, buses or boxes is a whole number. If a division leaves a remainder and everyone needs a spot, round up to the next whole number, even if the decimal part is small.

Worked example: Split a total, then check

A charity run has 126 volunteers split equally into 3 teams. How many volunteers are on each team?

  1. 126 ÷ 3 = 42126 divided by 3 equals 42Splitting a total into equal groups means divide.
  2. 3 × 42 = 1263 times 42 equals 126Multiply back to check. Three teams of 42 make 126 volunteers.

Answer: 42 volunteers on each team.

4. Fractions and percents of a group

What you will be able to do

  • Write a part of a group as a fraction of the whole group.
  • Change a fraction to a decimal and a percent.
  • Change a percent to a decimal without moving the point the wrong number of places.
  • Compare a decimal with a percent.

The whole goes on the bottom

"What fraction of the group" means the part you are asked about goes on top and the whole group goes on the bottom. The whole is everyone, not the other part. In a group of 10 people where 4 wear hats, the fraction wearing hats is 4104 over 10, not 464 over 6.

Fraction, decimal, percent

  • Fraction to decimal: divide the top by the bottom. 4104 over 10 is 4 ÷ 10 = 0.44 divided by 10 equals 0 point 4.
  • Decimal to percent: multiply by 100, which moves the decimal point two places to the right. 0.40 point 4 is 40%40 percent.
  • Percent to decimal: divide by 100, which moves the decimal point two places to the left. 5%5 percent is 0.050 point 0 5, not 0.50 point 5. And 0.50 point 5 is 50%50 percent, not 5%5 percent.

To compare a decimal with a percent, first write both the same way, either both as decimals or both as percents.

Worked example: One part, three ways

Of 72 people at a workshop, 18 brought a laptop. What fraction of the people brought a laptop? Write it as a decimal and as a percent too.

  1. 187218 over 72The part asked about (18 with laptops) goes on top. The whole group (72 people) goes on the bottom.
  2. 18 ÷ 72 = 0.2518 divided by 72 equals 0 point 2 5Divide the top by the bottom to get the decimal.
  3. 0.25 = 25%0 point 2 5 equals 25 percentMultiply by 100 (move the point two places right) to get the percent.

Answer: 187218 over 72, which is 141 over 4, or 0.250 point 2 5, or 25%25 percent.

5. A percent of a group as a count

What you will be able to do

  • Find a percent of a number.
  • Choose the right calculation for "a percent of" a group.
  • Find the part of a group that is not described by a percent.
  • Round a count to the nearest whole number, including when 9s carry.

A percent of a number

"Of" after a percent means multiply. Change the percent to a decimal first, then multiply: 38%38 percent of 650 is 0.38 × 6500 point 3 8 times 650.

If 35%35 percent of a group said yes, then the rest, 100% − 35% = 65%100 percent minus 35 percent equals 65 percent, did not. You can find the "not" count either by subtracting the "yes" count from the total or by taking 65%65 percent of the total.

Rounding a count

A percent of a group can come out with a decimal, like 95.4595 point 4 5 people. Follow the rounding direction in the question.

  1. Find the last digit you keep. For the nearest whole number, that is the ones digit.
  2. Look at only the one digit right after it. If it is 5 or more, the kept digit goes up by 1. If it is 4 or less, the kept digit stays.
  3. Do not round in a chain from the right. 95.4595 point 4 5 rounds to 95, because the digit after the ones place is 4. The 5 at the end does not matter.
  4. When a 9 goes up, it becomes 0 and carries 1 to the left. 299.7299 point 7 rounds to 300.

Worked example: A percent of a group, then round

In a town of 415 households, 23%23 percent have a garden. About how many households have a garden? Round to the nearest whole number.

  1. 23% = 0.2323 percent equals 0 point 2 3Change the percent to a decimal by moving the point two places left.
  2. 0.23 × 415 = 95.450 point 2 3 times 415 equals 95 point 4 5"Of" means multiply.
  3. Kept digit: 5 (ones). Next digit: 4.The digit right after the ones place is 4, which is less than 5, so the 5 stays.

Answer: 95 households.
