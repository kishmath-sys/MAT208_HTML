Where this is: Item 1 (Binomial Probability Distributions) on page 1 of the notes.

Check 3 (Independence). A polling company randomly selects 1,200 adults, without replacement, from a city of 2 million adults. Strictly speaking, the selections are dependent. Which statement matches the note under Independence in item 1?

The 5% guideline can allow us to treat the selections as independent.

Where this is: Item 1 (Binomial Probability Distributions) on page 1 of the notes, including the note under Independence.

Check 12 (Significantly High). Suppose 25% of job applicants at a company are referred by an employee. If 30 applicants are randomly selected, would 13 be a significantly high number of referred applicants? Use StatCrunch, then choose the answer that matches the reasoning in the Classroom Example 5.2.3 video.
Show answer

Yes, because the probability of 13 or more is about 0.0216, which is less than 0.05.

Where this is: The video "Classroom Example 5.2.3" on page 10 of the notes.

Check 13 (Significantly Low). Suppose 30% of a store's customers use a coupon. If 12 customers are randomly selected, would 1 be a significantly low number of coupon users? Use StatCrunch, then choose the answer that matches the reasoning in the Classroom Example 5.2.4 video.
Show answer

No, because the probability of 1 or fewer is about 0.0850, which is more than 0.05.

Where this is: The video "Classroom Example 5.2.4" on page 10 of the notes.
