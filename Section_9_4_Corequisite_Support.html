2. Comparing decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Line up decimal points and compare place by place from the left.
  • Add zeros at the end of a decimal so two decimals have the same length.
  • Avoid the "longer is larger" and "shorter is larger" slips.
  • Compare decimals that match for several digits.

Line up the decimal points

To compare two decimals, line up their decimal points and read from left to right, one place at a time. The first place where the digits differ decides which number is larger. Adding zeros to the end of a decimal does not change its value, so 0.080 point 0 8 is the same as 0.08000 point 0 8 0 0. Adding zeros makes two decimals the same length, which makes them easier to compare.

Lining up 0.0729 and 0.08 place by place
NumberOnesTenthsHundredthsThousandthsTen-thousandths
0.07290 point 0 7 2 900729
0.080 point 0 8 written as 0.08000 point 0 8 0 000800

The ones and tenths match. In the hundredths place, 8 is more than 7, so 0.080 point 0 8 is larger. The digits after that do not matter.

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer is larger. More digits does not mean a bigger number. 0.07290 point 0 7 2 9 has more digits than 0.080 point 0 8, but it is smaller.
  • Reading the digits after the point as a whole number. 0.3850 point 3 8 5 is not bigger than 0.40 point 4 just because 385 is bigger than 4. Write 0.40 point 4 as 0.4000 point 4 0 0: 400 thousandths is more than 385 thousandths.
  • Stopping too soon. When two decimals match for several digits, such as 0.482170 point 4 8 2 1 7 and 0.482710 point 4 8 2 7 1, keep going until the digits differ. Here the fourth digits are 1 and 7, so 0.482710 point 4 8 2 7 1 is larger.

Worked example: Which is larger, 0.0387 or 0.04?

Which is larger, 0.03870 point 0 3 8 7 or 0.040 point 0 4?

  1. 0.040 point 0 4 becomes 0.04000 point 0 4 0 0Add zeros so both numbers have four digits after the point.
  2. 0.03870 point 0 3 8 7 and 0.04000 point 0 4 0 0Compare from the left. The tenths digits are both 0. In the hundredths place, 3 is less than 4.
  3. 0.0387 < 0.040 point 0 3 8 7 is less than 0 point 0 4The first place where the digits differ decides it.

Answer: 0.040 point 0 4 is larger, even though 0.03870 point 0 3 8 7 has more digits.

3. Squaring numbers and dividing squares

What you will be able to do

  • Square a whole number or a decimal.
  • Place the decimal point in the square of a decimal.
  • Know that a number between 0 and 1 gets smaller when it is squared.
  • Divide one squared number by another, squaring before dividing.

Squaring means multiplying a number by itself

The small raised 2 means "squared." 929 squared means 9 × 99 times 9, which is 81. On one line, many calculators write it with a caret: 9^29 caret 2.

  • Decimal places add up. When you square a decimal, the answer has twice as many decimal places as the number you started with (before you drop any zeros at the end). 0.30 point 3 has one decimal place, so 0.320 point 3 squared has two: 0.3 × 0.3 = 0.090 point 3 times 0 point 3 equals 0 point 0 9.
  • Numbers between 0 and 1 get smaller. Squaring a number between 0 and 1 gives a smaller number: 0.820 point 8 squared is 0.640 point 6 4, which is less than 0.80 point 8. Squaring a number greater than 1 gives a larger number.
  • Square first, then divide. In 626 squared424 squared6 squared over 4 squared, square the top and square the bottom, then divide: 36 ÷ 16 = 2.2536 divided by 16 equals 2 point 2 5.

Three slips to watch for

  • Doubling instead of squaring. 929 squared is 81, not 18. 0.320 point 3 squared is 0.090 point 0 9, not 0.60 point 6.
  • Losing a decimal place. 0.320 point 3 squared is 0.090 point 0 9, not 0.90 point 9. Count the decimal places: one in, two out.
  • Squaring only part of a fraction. In 626 squared424 squared6 squared over 4 squared, both the top and the bottom are squared. Squaring only the top gives 36 ÷ 4 = 936 divided by 4 equals 9, a different value.

Worked example: Square 2.5

  2. 25 × 25 = 62525 times 25 equals 625Multiply without the decimal points first.

  4. Check: 222 squared is 4 and 323 squared is 92.52 point 5 is between 2 and 3, so its square should be between 4 and 9. The answer 6.256 point 2 5 fits.

4. Comparison symbols

What you will be able to do

  • Read the symbols <less than, >greater than, ≤less than or equal to, ≥greater than or equal to, = and ≠not equal to aloud.
  • Turn comparison words in a sentence into the matching symbol.
  • Rewrite a comparison with the other quantity first, flipping the symbol.
  • Divide both sides of a comparison by the same positive number.

The six comparison symbols

Comparison symbols and the words that signal them
SymbolRead it asWords that often signal it
<less thanless than"is less than," "fewer than," "smaller than," "below"
>greater thangreater than"is greater than," "more than," "larger than," "above"
≤less than or equal toless than or equal to"is less than or equal to," "at most," "no more than"
≥greater than or equal togreater than or equal to"is greater than or equal to," "at least," "no less than"
=equalsequals"equals," "is the same as"
≠not equal tonot equal to"is not equal to," "is different from," "differs from"

A memory aid: the open side of <the less than symbol or >the greater than symbol faces the larger amount. The line under ≤less than or equal to and ≥greater than or equal to means "or equal," so 5 ≤ 55 is less than or equal to 5 is true.

Switching the order

"Ana's rent is less than Ben's rent" and "Ben's rent is greater than Ana's rent" say the same thing. When you switch which quantity comes first, the symbol flips: A < BA is less than B means the same as B > AB is greater than A. Swap the order and flip the symbol together, or do neither. The symbols = and ≠not equal to stay the same when the order switches.

Dividing both sides by a positive number

Dividing both sides of a comparison by the same positive number keeps the symbol the same. From 12 > 812 is greater than 8, dividing both sides by 4 gives 3 > 23 is greater than 2. In the same way, if b is positive and a > ba is greater than b, dividing both sides by b gives a/b > 1a over b is greater than 1, because b divided by itself is 1.

Three slips to watch for

  • Swapping without flipping. Rewriting A < BA is less than B as B < AB is less than A changes the meaning.
  • Treating ≤less than or equal to like <less than. 0.04 ≤ 0.040 point 0 4 is less than or equal to 0 point 0 4 is true, because "or equal" is part of the symbol.
  • Using a direction for "different." "Is different from" does not say which is larger, so it is ≠not equal to, not <less than or >greater than.

Worked example: Small and large coffee

"A small coffee costs less than a large coffee." Write this with the large coffee first.

  1. small < largesmall is less than large"Costs less than" is the less than symbol, written in the order of the sentence.
  2. large > smalllarge is greater than smallSwitch the order and flip the symbol at the same time.

Answer: large > smalllarge is greater than small. Both say the large coffee costs more.

5. Rounding to two and three decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two decimal places.
  • Round to three decimal places.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Keep zeros that the rounding direction needs, such as the zeros in 3.00.

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. For two decimal places, it is the second digit after the point. For three decimal places, it is the third digit after the point.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Three slips to watch for

  • Chopping instead of rounding. 2.007182 point 0 0 7 1 8 to two decimal places is 2.012 point 0 1, not 2.002 point 0 0. The digit after the kept 0 is 7, so the 0 goes up.
  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 0.034490 point 0 3 4 4 9 to three decimal places is 0.0340 point 0 3 4. Rounding to 0.03450 point 0 3 4 5 first and then to 0.0350 point 0 3 5 is a slip.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.299610 point 2 9 9 6 1 to three decimal places is 0.3000 point 3 0 0.

Two decimal places means exactly two digits after the point, even when they are zeros: 3.00413 point 0 0 4 1 to two decimal places is 3.003 point 0 0.

Worked example: Round 2.71846 two ways

Round 2.718462 point 7 1 8 4 6 to two decimal places, and then to three decimal places.

  1. Two decimal places: keep the 1, look at the 8The 8 is 5 or more, so the 1 becomes 2: 2.722 point 7 2.
  2. Three decimal places: keep the 8, look at the 4The 4 is 4 or less, so the 8 stays: 2.7182 point 7 1 8.

Answer: 2.722 point 7 2 to two decimal places and 2.7182 point 7 1 8 to three decimal places.

Directions decide the answer. Read the rounding direction at the end of each question before you round. "Two decimal places" and "three decimal places" give different answers from the same number.
