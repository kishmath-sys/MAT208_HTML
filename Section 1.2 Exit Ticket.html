2. Concepts

These checks cover items 1 to 6 on pages 1 and 2 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.1 and 1.2.2 videos on page 4.

Check 1 (Parameter). A college wants to describe the 1,850 students enrolled this semester. The registrar uses the records of all 1,850 students and finds that their average age is 24.624 point 6 years. Is 24.624 point 6 years a statistic or a parameter?
Show answer

A parameter, because it uses every data value from the entire population of 1,850 students.

Where this is: Items 1 and 2 (Parameter, Statistic) on page 1 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.1 video on page 4.

Check 2 (Statistic). A city wants to describe weekend attendance at its public pool for one summer, which had 14 weekends. Staff counted visitors on 11 of the 14 weekends, and the average was 486 visitors per weekend. Is 486 a statistic or a parameter?
Show answer

A statistic, because the data come from only some of the weekends, which is a sample.

Where this is: Items 1 and 2 (Parameter, Statistic) on page 1 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.1 video on page 4.

Check 3 (Quantitative and Qualitative Data). A survey records four things about each student. Which one is qualitative data?
Show answer

Each student's major, such as nursing or welding

Where this is: Items 3 and 4 (Quantitative Data, Qualitative Data) on page 1 of the Section 1.2 notes.

Check 4 (Numbers Used as Labels). A store records the zip code of each customer, such as 60115 and 61008. Which statement fits items 3 and 4?
Show answer

Qualitative (also called categorical data), because zip codes are labels and averaging them would not make sense.

Where this is: Items 3 and 4 on page 1 of the Section 1.2 notes, the Summary of Levels of Measurement on page 3, and the Classroom Example 1.2.5 video on page 5.

Check 5 (Units). A student finds that the people she surveyed spend an average of 23 minutes commuting each day. Which way of reporting the result follows the Note under item 3?
Show answer

23 minutes

Where this is: Item 3 (Quantitative Data), including the Note, on page 1 of the Section 1.2 notes.

Check 6 (Discrete Data). The number of text messages each student sends in one day. Which description fits best?
Show answer

Discrete, because the values are whole numbers that come from counting.

Where this is: Items 5 and 6 (Discrete Data, Continuous Data) on page 2 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.2 video on page 4.

Check 7 (Continuous Data). The exact weights (in grams) of the apples picked in an orchard. Which description fits best?
Show answer

Continuous, because the weights are measured and can take on any value in an interval.

Where this is: Items 5 and 6 (Discrete Data, Continuous Data) on page 2 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.2 video on page 4.

3. Levels of Measurement

These checks cover items 7 to 10 and the Summary of Levels of Measurement on pages 2 and 3 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.3, 1.2.4 and 1.2.5 videos on pages 4 and 5.

Check 8 (Nominal). The blood types of donors at a blood drive: A, B, AB and O. Which level of measurement fits best?
Show answer

Nominal

Where this is: Item 7 (Nominal Level of Measurement) on page 2 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.3 video on page 4.

Check 9 (Ordinal). Survey answers of "disagree," "neutral" and "agree." Which level of measurement fits best?
Show answer

Ordinal, because the answers can be ordered, but differences between them are not meaningful.

Where this is: Items 7 and 8 (Nominal, Ordinal) on page 2 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.3 video on page 4.

Check 10 (Order Without Meaningful Differences). Judges at a pie contest rank the top three pies 1st, 2nd and 3rd. Which statement fits item 8 and its Key Insight?
Show answer

The ranks show a clear order, but they do not tell how much better one pie was than another.

Where this is: Item 8 (Ordinal Level of Measurement), including the Key Insight, on page 2 of the Section 1.2 notes.

Check 11 (Interval). Daily high temperatures in a city, in degrees Celsius. Which level of measurement fits best?
Show answer

Interval, because differences make sense, but 0°C0 degrees Celsius does not mean the absence of heat.

Where this is: Item 9 (Interval Level of Measurement), including the "Ask" questions, and the Summary of Levels of Measurement on page 3 of the Section 1.2 notes.

Check 12 (Interval with Years). The years in which a city opened each of its public libraries, such as 1958, 1987 and 2015. Which level of measurement fits best?
Show answer

Interval, because differences between years make sense, but zero on the calendar is arbitrary and does not mean the absence of time.

Where this is: Items 9 and 10 and the Summary of Levels of Measurement on page 3 of the Section 1.2 notes, and the Classroom Example 1.2.4 video on page 4.

Check 13 (Ratio). The weights, in pounds, of packages shipped from a warehouse. Which level of measurement fits best?
Show answer

Ratio, because 0 pounds means no weight and all arithmetic operations make sense, so 10 pounds is twice 5 pounds.

Where this is: Item 10 (Ratio Level of Measurement) and the Summary of Levels of Measurement on page 3 of the Section 1.2 notes.

Check 14 (Does Zero Mean the Absence?). Item 9 suggests asking, "Does zero mean the absence of ___?" For which data is the answer yes?
Show answer

Yearly incomes, in dollars

Where this is: Items 9 and 10 and the Summary of Levels of Measurement on page 3 of the Section 1.2 notes.

Check 15 (Numbers as Category Codes). A gym survey codes each member's favorite activity as 1 for swimming, 2 for weights, 3 for yoga and 4 for cycling. Someone averages the codes for 410 members and gets 2.62 point 6. Which statement fits?
Show answer

The data are nominal, because the numbers are only labels for categories with no order, so an average of 2.62 point 6 makes no sense.

Where this is: Items 4 and 7 on pages 1 and 2 of the Section 1.2 notes and the Classroom Example 1.2.5 video on page 5.

Check 16 (Summary Table). In the Summary of Levels of Measurement table, which level has an order and meaningful differences, but no true zero?
Show answer

Interval

Where this is: The Summary of Levels of Measurement on page 3 of the Section 1.2 notes.

Check 17 (Levels for Qualitative Data). Which levels of measurement apply to qualitative (categorical) data?
Show answer

Nominal and ordinal

Where this is: Items 7 to 10 on pages 2 and 3 of the Section 1.2 notes, the Summary of Levels of Measurement on page 3, and the Classroom Example 1.2.3 video on page 4.
