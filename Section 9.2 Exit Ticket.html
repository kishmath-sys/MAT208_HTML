Check 5 (Step One, Claim in the Alternative). A study tests the claim that the mean commute time for workers in City A is less than the mean commute time for workers in City B. Population 1 is City A and population 2 is City B. Which Step One matches the notes?
Show answer

H0: μ1 = μ2H sub 0: mu 1 equals mu 2 and H1: μ1 < μ2 (claim)H sub 1: mu 1 is less than mu 2, claim

Where this is: Step One of the example in Topic 2 on page 1 of the notes.

Check 6 (Step One, Claim in the Null). A study tests the claim that two brands of tires have the same mean tread life. Population 1 is Brand A and population 2 is Brand B. Which Step One matches the notes?

4. Confidence Interval Method

These checks cover Topic 3 (Confidence Interval Method) on pages 7 to 12 of the notes, and the videos "Classroom Example 9.2.1" and "Classroom Example 9.2.2" on page 13.

Check 11 (Both Boundaries Negative). A confidence interval is −3.4 < μ1 − μ2 < −0.8negative 3 point 4 is less than mu 1 minus mu 2, which is less than negative 0 point 8. Which statement matches the Key Concept and the three phases?
Show answer

Both boundaries are negative, so μ1 − μ2 < 0mu 1 minus mu 2 is less than 0 and μ1 is smaller than μ2.

Where this is: The Key Concept: Understanding the Confidence Interval on pages 8 and 10 of the notes, and the Conclusion (Using the Three Phases from Chapter 7) on pages 8 and 11.

Check 12 (Zero Between the Boundaries). A confidence interval is −0.42 < μ1 − μ2 < 1.37negative 0 point 4 2 is less than mu 1 minus mu 2, which is less than 1 point 3 7. Which statement matches the Key Concept?
Show answer

Zero is between the boundaries, so we use μ1 = μ2mu 1 equals mu 2.

Where this is: The Key Concept: Understanding the Confidence Interval on pages 8 and 10 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 9.2.1" on page 13.
