2. Percents and decimals

What you will be able to do

  • Change a percent to a decimal, including single-digit percents such as 4% and percents below 1% such as 0.5%0 point 5 percent.
  • Change a decimal to a percent, including decimals with four digits after the point.
  • Spot the one-place and three-place slips.

Percent means "out of 100"

37% means 37 out of 100, which is the decimal 0.370 point 3 7. Moving between the two forms comes down to dividing or multiplying by 100.

  • Percent to decimal: divide by 100. That moves the decimal point two places to the left. Fill any empty places with zeros.
  • Decimal to percent: multiply by 100. That moves the decimal point two places to the right, then add the percent sign.

Three slips to watch for

  • A single-digit percent. 3% is 0.030 point 0 3, not 0.30 point 3. Two places left from 3 needs a zero in front of the 3.
  • A percent below 1%. 0.5%0 point 5 percent is 0.0050 point 0 0 5. It is less than 1%, so its decimal is less than 0.010 point 0 1.
  • Adding a percent sign without converting. 0.60 point 6 is 60%. Writing 0.6%0 point 6 percent names a much smaller amount.

Worked example: Write 0.8735 as a percent

A calculator shows 0.87350 point 8 7 3 5. Write it as a percent.

  1. 0.8735 × 1000 point 8 7 3 5 times 100Decimal to percent means multiply by 100.
  2. 0.8735 → 8.735 → 87.350 point 8 7 3 5, then 8 point 7 3 5, then 87 point 3 5Move the decimal point one place right, then a second place right. Two digits stay after the point.

Answer: 87.35%87 point 3 5 percent (not 8.735%8 point 7 3 5 percent, which moves only one place, and not 873.5%873 point 5 percent, which moves three)

3. The rest of a whole

What you will be able to do

  • Find the percent that is left by subtracting from 100%.
  • Find the decimal that is left by subtracting from 1.
  • Subtract a decimal from 1 by writing 1 with enough zeros.
  • Handle a whole with two pieces taken away.

Parts of a whole add up to the whole

When a whole group is split into parts, the percents of the parts add up to 100%. Written as decimals, the parts add up to 1. So if you know one part, subtract it from the whole to find the rest.

  • With percents: the rest is 100% − part100 percent minus the part.
  • With decimals: the rest is 1 − part1 minus the part.

Subtracting from 1. Write 1 with as many zeros after the point as the other number has digits after its point, then line up the points. For example, write 1 as 1.00001 point 0 0 0 0 before subtracting 0.02750 point 0 2 7 5.

Three slips to watch for

  • Lining up the last digits instead of the points. 1 − 0.081 minus 0 point 0 8 is not 1.0 − 0.81 point 0 minus 0 point 8. Write 1.00 − 0.081 point 0 0 minus 0 point 0 8 first.
  • Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column. In 1.0000 − 0.99631 point 0 0 0 0 minus 0 point 9 9 6 3, every column needs borrowing. Taking the smaller digit from the larger gives 1.99631 point 9 9 6 3, which is more than 1 and cannot be right.
  • Forgetting a second piece. If two parts are taken away, subtract both of them.

Worked example: The rest of a survey

In a survey, 37% of shoppers said they use self-checkout. What percent did not? Write the answer as a percent and as a decimal.

  1. 100% − 37% = 63%100 percent minus 37 percent equals 63 percentThe two groups make up all the shoppers, so subtract from 100%.
  2. 1.00 − 0.37 = 0.631 point 0 0 minus 0 point 3 7 equals 0 point 6 3As decimals, the whole is 1. Write 1 as 1.001 point 0 0 so the points line up.

Answer: 63%, which is 0.630 point 6 3

4. Rounding to several decimal places

What you will be able to do

  • Round to three or four decimal places, counting every digit after the point, zeros included.
  • Look only at the one digit right after the last kept digit.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up.
  • Change a decimal to a percent and then round the percent.

How to round

  1. Count places after the decimal point. Three decimal places keeps three digits after the point. Four decimal places keeps four. Zeros right after the point count as places.
  2. Look at the single digit right after the last kept digit.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the last kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the last kept digit.

Three slips to watch for

  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the last kept digit matters. 0.247490 point 2 4 7 4 9 to three decimal places is 0.2470 point 2 4 7. Rounding to 0.24750 point 2 4 7 5 first and then to 0.2480 point 2 4 8 is a slip.
  • Starting the count at the first digit that is not zero. 0.07310 point 0 7 3 1 has four decimal places. The zero right after the point counts.
  • A 9 that rounds up. When a 9 goes up by 1, it becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. 0.399620 point 3 9 9 6 2 to three decimal places is 0.4000 point 4 0 0.

Worked example: Round 0.61847 two ways

Round 0.618470 point 6 1 8 4 7 to three decimal places, and then to four decimal places.

  1. Three decimal places: keep 0.6180 point 6 1 8, look at the 4The 4 is 4 or less, so the 8 stays: 0.6180 point 6 1 8.
  2. Four decimal places: keep 0.61840 point 6 1 8 4, look at the 7The 7 is 5 or more, so the 4 becomes 5: 0.61850 point 6 1 8 5.

Answer: 0.6180 point 6 1 8 to three decimal places and 0.61850 point 6 1 8 5 to four decimal places.

Directions decide the answer. "Do not round" means type every digit the calculator shows. "Round to four decimal places" means exactly four digits after the point. A rounded answer can end in 0, such as 0.4100 point 4 1 0. That last zero is a kept place.

5. Comparing decimals and reading inequalities

What you will be able to do

  • Compare decimals with different numbers of digits.
  • Tell which of two numbers sits farther right on a number line.
  • Read the symbols >greater than, <less than, ≥greater than or equal to and ≤less than or equal to, and the phrases "at least," "no more than" and "between."

Comparing decimals

Compare place by place from the left. If one number has fewer digits after the point, add zeros so both have the same number of places. A longer decimal is not automatically larger.

  • 0.3070 point 3 0 7 against 0.370 point 3 7: write them as 0.3070 point 3 0 7 and 0.3700 point 3 7 0. Since 370 thousandths is more than 307 thousandths, 0.370 point 3 7 is larger.
  • On a number line, larger numbers sit farther to the right. A number smaller than another sits to its left.

Inequality symbols and phrases

Inequality symbols and how to read them
SymbolRead it asPhrases that match
x > 48x is greater than 48x is greater than 48more than 48, over 48 (48 itself is not included)
x ≥ 48x is greater than or equal to 48x is greater than or equal to 48at least 48, 48 or more (48 is included)
x < 30x is less than 30x is less than 30less than 30, under 30 (30 is not included)
x ≤ 30x is less than or equal to 30x is less than or equal to 30at most 30, no more than 30 (30 is included)
25 ≤ x ≤ 7525 is less than or equal to x, and x is less than or equal to 75x is between 25 and 75, including bothfrom 25 to 75

The symbol opens toward the larger side. In x > 48x is greater than 48, the 48 sits on the small side, so x is the larger one.

Worked example: Which is larger, 0.0627 or 0.06?

Which is larger, 0.06270 point 0 6 2 7 or 0.060 point 0 6? Which one sits farther right on a number line?

  1. 0.06 = 0.06000 point 0 6 equals 0 point 0 6 0 0Add zeros so both numbers have four places after the point.
  2. 0.0627 > 0.06000 point 0 6 2 7 is greater than 0 point 0 6 0 0The first two places match. In the third place, 2 is more than 0.

Answer: 0.06270 point 0 6 2 7 is larger, so it sits to the right of 0.060 point 0 6.
