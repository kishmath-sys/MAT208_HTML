Check 1 (Compound Event). A single six-sided die is rolled once. Which event is a compound event, as item 1 describes it?
Show answer

Rolling a number greater than 4, which is a 5 or a 6

Where this is: Item 1 (Compound Event) on page 1 of the notes.

Check 2 (Formal Addition Rule). One card is drawn from a standard 52-card deck. Which expression uses the formal addition rule in item 2 to find the probability of a king or a heart?
Show answer

4/52 + 13/52 − 1/524 over 52 plus 13 over 52 minus 1 over 52, which is 16/52 = 4/1316 over 52, which equals 4 over 13

Where this is: Item 2 (Formal Addition Rule) on page 1 of the notes.

3. Concepts: Multiplication Rules

These checks cover items 6 to 11 on pages 3 and 4 of the Section 4.2 notes, the Classroom Example 4.2.3 video on page 5 and the Classroom Example 4.2.4 video on page 6.

Check 8 (Notation). Two cards are drawn one after the other. A is the event that the first card is red, and B is the event that the second card is red. What does P(B|A)P of B given A mean?
Show answer

The probability that the second card is red, given that the first card was red

Where this is: Item 6 (Multiplication Rule for "and" Events) on page 3 of the notes.

Check 9 (Independent and Dependent Events). An inspector takes 2 batteries from a box of 24 and does not put the first one back before taking the second. Which statement matches items 7 and 8?
Show answer

Dependent, because removing the first battery changes the box for the second

Where this is: Items 7 and 8 (Independent Events, Dependent Events) on page 3 of the notes.

Check 10 (Independent Events). A hospital has a main generator and a backup generator. Each one fails on 6% of days, and they work independently. Which expression gives the probability that both fail on the same day?
Show answer

0.06 × 0.060 point 0 6 times 0 point 0 6, which is 0.00360 point 0 0 3 6

Where this is: Items 7 and 10 (Independent Events, Intuitive Multiplication Rule) on pages 3 and 4 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 4.2.4" on page 6.

Check 11 (Formal Multiplication Rule). For two events, P(A) = 0.5P of A equals 0 point 5 and P(B|A) = 0.3P of B given A equals 0 point 3. Using the formal multiplication rule in item 9, what is P(A and B)P of A and B?
Show answer

0.150 point 1 5

Where this is: Item 9 (Formal Multiplication Rule) on page 3 of the notes.

Check 12 (Intuitive Multiplication Rule). A drawer has 7 blue socks and 5 black socks. Two socks are drawn without replacement. Which expression gives the probability that the first sock is blue and the second sock is black?
Show answer

7/12 × 5/117 over 12 times 5 over 11

Where this is: Item 10 (Intuitive Multiplication Rule) on page 4 of the notes.

Check 13 (With and Without Replacement). An animal shelter has 40 dogs, and 18 of them are puppies. Two dogs are selected. Which pair gives the probability that both are puppies, first with replacement and then without replacement?
Show answer

With: 18/40 × 18/4018 over 40 times 18 over 40. Without: 18/40 × 17/3918 over 40 times 17 over 39.

Where this is: Items 7, 8 and 10 (Independent Events, Dependent Events, Intuitive Multiplication Rule) on pages 3 and 4 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 4.2.3" on page 5.

Check 14 (5% Guideline). A shipment has 1,200 phone cases. A quality check tests 9 of them without replacement. Can the 5% guideline in item 11 be used to treat the tests as independent?
Show answer

Yes, because 9 is less than 60, which is 5% of 1,200

Where this is: Item 11 (The 5% Guideline for Cumbersome Calculations) on page 4 of the notes.

Check 15 (Redundancy). A small business keeps its records on a main drive that fails on 15% of days. It adds backup drives that fail independently at the same rate. The probability that all drives fail is 0.02250 point 0 2 2 5 with one backup and 0.0033750 point 0 0 3 3 7 5 with two. Do the backups greatly improve reliability?
Show answer

Yes, since total failure is not impossible but becomes unlikely

Where this is: Item 10 (Intuitive Multiplication Rule) on page 4 of the notes, and the video "Classroom Example 4.2.4" on page 6.
