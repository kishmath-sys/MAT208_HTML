2. Subtracting in a set order

What you will be able to do

  • Subtract two numbers in the order the directions give.
  • Give a difference the right sign when the second number is larger.
  • Line up decimal points when the numbers have different numbers of digits.
  • Subtract a negative number.
  • Read matching values from the same column of a table.

Order matters in subtraction

Adding works in any order, but subtracting does not. 9 − 4 = 59 minus 4 equals 5, while 4 − 9 = −54 minus 9 equals negative 5. When directions say "first minus second," write the first number, then the minus sign, then the second number, and keep that order.

  • Second number larger: subtract the smaller from the larger to get the size, then make the answer negative.
  • First number larger: the answer is positive.
  • Same number: the answer is 0.
  • Subtracting a negative: it is the same as adding the positive. 2 − (−5) = 2 + 5 = 72 minus open parenthesis negative 5 close parenthesis equals 2 plus 5 equals 7.

With decimals, line up the decimal points and fill empty places with zeros: write 9.49 point 4 as 9.409 point 4 0 before subtracting 9.759 point 7 5.

Three slips to watch for

  • Subtracting the smaller from the larger no matter the order. 4 − 9 = −54 minus 9 equals negative 5, not 5.
  • Dropping the negative sign. The size can be right while the sign is wrong.
  • Treating subtraction like multiplication. "Two negatives make a positive" is a rule for multiplying. −4 − 3 = −7negative 4 minus 3 equals negative 7, not 12.

Worked example: Mile times

A runner's mile time was 9.49 point 4 minutes on Monday and 9.759 point 7 5 minutes on Friday. Find the Monday time minus the Friday time.

  1. 9.40 − 9.759 point 4 0 minus 9 point 7 5Keep the order: Monday first. Line up the points by writing 9.4 as 9.40.
  2. The second number is largerSo the answer will be negative.
  3. 9.75 − 9.40 = 0.359 point 7 5 minus 9 point 4 0 equals 0 point 3 5Subtract the smaller from the larger to get the size.

Answer: −0.35negative 0 point 3 5 minutes

3. Adding signed numbers and dividing

What you will be able to do

  • Add two numbers with the same sign or with different signs.
  • Add a list of positive and negative numbers one at a time.
  • Divide a negative number by a positive number.
  • Treat a fraction bar as grouping its whole top.

Rules for adding signed numbers

  • Same signs: add the sizes and keep the sign. (−6) + (−11) = −17open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis plus open parenthesis negative 11 close parenthesis equals negative 17.
  • Different signs: subtract the smaller size from the larger size, and keep the sign of the number with the larger size. (−6) + 2 = −4open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis plus 2 equals negative 4.
  • Adding 0 changes nothing.

For a long list, add two numbers at a time from left to right. Writing each negative number in parentheses keeps its sign from getting mixed up with a minus sign.

Dividing: a negative number divided by a positive number is negative. −12 ÷ 4 = −3negative 12 divided by 4 equals negative 3.

A fraction bar groups everything above it. Add the whole top first, then divide. On one line, the top needs parentheses around it.

Three slips to watch for

  • Taking the sign of the wrong number. 2 + (−9) = −72 plus open parenthesis negative 9 close parenthesis equals negative 7 is negative, because 9 has the larger size.
  • Making every number negative. A positive number in the list still adds to the total.
  • Dividing only the last number. Without parentheses, a calculator divides only the number right next to the slash.

Worked example: A four-number fraction

Evaluate (−6) + 2 + (−11) + 34the fraction with negative 6 plus 2 plus negative 11 plus 3 on top and 4 on the bottom.

  1. (−6) + 2 = −4open parenthesis negative 6 close parenthesis plus 2 equals negative 4Different signs: 6 minus 2 is 4, and the 6 has the larger size, so the result is negative.
  2. −4 + (−11) = −15negative 4 plus open parenthesis negative 11 close parenthesis equals negative 15Same signs: add the sizes and keep the negative sign.
  3. −15 + 3 = −12negative 15 plus 3 equals negative 12Different signs: 15 minus 3 is 12, and it stays negative.
  4. −12 ÷ 4 = −3negative 12 divided by 4 equals negative 3Now divide the whole top. Negative divided by positive is negative.

Answer: −3negative 3

4. Comparing decimals and negative numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Compare decimals that have different numbers of digits.
  • Compare negative numbers.
  • Tell whether a number is between two others.
  • Read the symbols <, >, ≤ and ≠, including a chain such as −2 < x < 5negative 2 is less than x, which is less than 5.

Comparing decimals

Line up the decimal points and add zeros at the end so both numbers have the same number of digits. Then compare from left to right. The first place where the digits differ decides.

A decimal with more digits is not automatically larger. 0.0082 < 0.010 point 0 0 8 2 is less than 0 point 0 1, because 0.00820 point 0 0 8 2 is less than 0.01000 point 0 1 0 0.

Comparing negative numbers

On a number line, numbers get smaller as you move left. Every negative number is less than 0, and 0 is less than every positive number. Between two negative numbers, the one with the larger size is smaller: −3.15 < −2.4negative 3 point 1 5 is less than negative 2 point 4, even though 3.15 is more than 2.4.

Reading the symbols

  • a < ba is less than b means a is less than b. The symbol points at the smaller number.
  • a ≤ ba is less than or equal to b means a is less than or equal to b.
  • a ≠ ba is not equal to b means a is not equal to b.
  • −2 < x < 5negative 2 is less than x, which is less than 5 means x is greater than −2 and less than 5 at the same time, so x is between −2 and 5.

Three slips to watch for

  • Longer is larger. 0.1250 point 1 2 5 is not larger than 0.40 point 4.
  • Ignoring the negative sign. −3.15negative 3 point 1 5 is smaller than −0.9negative 0 point 9, not larger.
  • Reading a symbol backwards. Read a chain from left to right, and check that each symbol points at the smaller number.

Worked example: Compare 0.060 point 0 6 and 0.04750 point 0 4 7 5

Which is larger, 0.060 point 0 6 or 0.04750 point 0 4 7 5?

  1. 0.06000 point 0 6 0 0 and 0.04750 point 0 4 7 5Add zeros so both have four digits after the point.
  2. Compare the hundredths digits: 6 and 4The tenths digits match (both 0), and 6 is more than 4.

Answer: 0.060 point 0 6 is larger

5. Subtracting from 1 and order of operations

What you will be able to do

  • Subtract a decimal from 1 by lining up the decimal points.
  • Multiply before subtracting in an expression such as 1 − 2 × 0.041 minus 2 times 0 point 0 4.
  • Read a typed expression that uses an asterisk for multiplication.
  • Change a decimal answer to a percent.

Subtracting from 1

Write 1 with as many zeros after the point as the other number has digits: 1 = 1.001 equals 1 point 0 0. Then subtract place by place: 1.00 − 0.03 = 0.971 point 0 0 minus 0 point 0 3 equals 0 point 9 7.

Multiply before you subtract

In 1 − 2 × 0.041 minus 2 times 0 point 0 4, multiplication comes before subtraction, even though the subtraction is on the left.

  1. Multiply: 2 × 0.04 = 0.082 times 0 point 0 4 equals 0 point 0 8.
  2. Subtract: 1 − 0.08 = 0.921 minus 0 point 0 8 equals 0 point 9 2.

On a keyboard, the asterisk (*) means multiply, so 1 − 2 × 0.041 minus 2 times 0 point 0 4 is typed as 1-2*0.041 minus 2 asterisk 0 point 0 4. A calculator follows the same order.

Decimal to percent: multiply by 100, which moves the point two places right. 0.920 point 9 2 is 92%.

Three slips to watch for

  • Subtracting first. (1 − 2) × 0.04 = −0.04open parenthesis 1 minus 2 close parenthesis times 0 point 0 4 equals negative 0 point 0 4, a negative number nowhere near the right answer.
  • Lining up the wrong places. 1 − 0.03 = 0.971 minus 0 point 0 3 equals 0 point 9 7, not 0.70 point 7. The 3 is in the hundredths place.
  • Doubling slips. 2 × 0.025 = 0.052 times 0 point 0 2 5 equals 0 point 0 5, not 0.50 point 5.

Worked example: 1 − 2 × 0.041 minus 2 times 0 point 0 4

Evaluate 1 − 2 × 0.041 minus 2 times 0 point 0 4, then write the result as a percent.

  1. 2 × 0.04 = 0.082 times 0 point 0 4 equals 0 point 0 8Multiplication first.
  2. 1.00 − 0.08 = 0.921 point 0 0 minus 0 point 0 8 equals 0 point 9 2Then subtract, with the points lined up.
  3. 0.92 × 100 = 920 point 9 2 times 100 equals 92Multiply by 100 for the percent.

Answer: 0.920 point 9 2, which is 92%

6. Rounding, including negative numbers

What you will be able to do

  • Round to two decimal places and to three decimal places.
  • Round to the nearest integer (whole number).
  • Round a negative number and keep its sign.
  • Carry correctly when a 9 rounds up, and avoid rounding in steps.

How to round

  1. Find the last digit you will keep. Two decimal places keeps the hundredths digit, three decimal places keeps the thousandths digit, and the nearest integer keeps the ones digit.
  2. Look at the single digit right after it.
  3. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the kept digit. If it is 4 or less, leave the kept digit alone.
  4. Drop every digit after the kept digit.

Negative numbers

Round the digits exactly as if there were no sign, then put the negative sign back. −2.4863negative 2 point 4 8 6 3 rounds to −2.49negative 2 point 4 9 at two decimal places, because 2.48632 point 4 8 6 3 rounds to 2.492 point 4 9. The size gets bigger, so the negative number moves further from 0.

Three slips to watch for

  • Chopping instead of rounding. −1.3462negative 1 point 3 4 6 2 to two decimal places is −1.35negative 1 point 3 5, not −1.34negative 1 point 3 4.
  • Rounding in steps from the right. Only the one digit right after the kept digit matters. 3127.483127 point 4 8 to the nearest whole number is 3127, not 3128.
  • A 9 that rounds up. It becomes 0 and adds 1 to the digit on its left. −3.0962negative 3 point 0 9 6 2 to two decimal places is −3.10negative 3 point 1 0.

Worked example: Round −2.4863negative 2 point 4 8 6 3 two ways

Round −2.4863negative 2 point 4 8 6 3 to two decimal places, and then to the nearest integer.

  1. Two decimal places: keep the 8, look at the 6The 6 is 5 or more, so the 8 becomes 9: −2.49negative 2 point 4 9.
  2. Nearest integer: keep the 2, look at the 4The 4 is 4 or less, so the 2 stays: −2negative 2.

Answer: −2.49negative 2 point 4 9 to two decimal places and −2negative 2 to the nearest integer.
